Levels of Conflict Guide

How to tell how far a team conflict has gone, and choose a response that fits, from letting the team solve it to getting help.

“What words are people using, and what level do they point to?”

Disagreement is healthy; teams need it to make good decisions. Trouble starts when conflict escalates. Speed B. Leas described five levels of conflict, from a problem to solve through disagreement, contest and crusade to world war, and Lyssa Adkins brought the model to agile teams in Coaching Agile Teams. The language people use is the best clue to the level. This board helps a coach or Scrum Master diagnose the level, pick the move that fits it, and agree a next step. It's mainly a tool for the coach to use privately, not one to fill in with the people in conflict.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or use it on screen; keep it private if it names people
  • Collect what you've seen and heard: actual words and behaviour, not impressions
  • Talk to each person involved on their own before you decide anything
  • Know your organisation's route to HR for anything beyond the team

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionNotes
0–103 · What's It About?Write the facts, who's involved and how long it's been going. Note what each side needs.
10–201 · What Level?Compare what you see and hear with each level. The highest level you see is the one that counts.
20–254 · Before You Step InWork through the questions. If anyone is at risk, stop and escalate.
25–302 · The Coach's MoveChoose the move for the level. Lower levels: step back. Higher levels: step in or get help.
30–405 · Our Next StepAgree the move, who's involved, when, when to check again, and what would trigger escalation.

3Moves by level

The higher the level, the more the coach steps in.

1 · Problem to solve

  • Collaborate
  • Let the team solve it

2 · Disagreement

  • Support
  • Each side is heard

3 · Contest

  • Facilitate
  • Facts and interests

4 · Crusade

  • Mediate
  • Neutral third party

5 · World war

  • Protect people
  • Escalate now

4Facilitation tips

Listen to the words“We” and specifics suggest level 1; “always”, “never” and labels suggest level 3 or higher.
Some conflict is goodA team with no disagreement at all may be avoiding hard conversations.
Interests, not positionsAsk what each side needs and why; positions clash, needs often don't.
Lean on agreementsA team charter with norms for disagreement gives the team a way to resolve it themselves.
Check your own biasIf you have a side, find a neutral facilitator.
Know where it stopsHarassment, discrimination and safety issues aren't team conflicts. Escalate them.
Common pitfalls: stepping in too early at level 1–2 and taking the problem away from the team; ignoring it until level 4; mediating when you're not neutral; treating harassment as a conflict to coach; sharing the board with the people involved.

5After the session

Within a day or two

Take the agreed first step, and talk to each person involved.

At the check-in date

Reassess the level. Has it gone down, stayed, or risen?

Next charter review

Add or improve norms for healthy disagreement.

If it rises to 4–5

Escalate as planned; don't wait.

Use it with the Coaching Conversation for one-to-ones with each person, the Feedback Conversation board when one person needs to raise something with another, the Team Charter for norms, and the Psychological Safety Check to see whether people feel safe to disagree.
