Release Readiness & Strategy Guide

How to plan how a change reaches users, decide go or no-go, know how to undo it, and tell the people who need to know.

“If this goes wrong at the worst moment, what's our plan?”

Deploying puts new code into production; releasing makes it available to users. Keeping them separate is one of the most useful ideas in modern delivery: with feature flags, canary releases, blue-green switches or phased rollouts, a team can deploy safely and then release a little at a time, watching as it goes. The board walks through the release approach, a go/no-go checklist agreed in advance, a rollback plan, who needs to know, and the checks after release. It's useful for engineering teams and for project and programme managers coordinating a larger release.

3Ways to release

Smaller and more gradual is usually safer.

Feature flag

  • Deploy switched off
  • Turn on for some, then all
  • Remove the flag later

Canary

  • A small share first
  • Watch, then widen

Blue-green

  • Two copies of production
  • Switch traffic back to undo

Phased rollout

  • By region or group
  • Learn between phases

Big bang

  • Everyone at once
  • Needs the strongest rollback

4Facilitation tips

Agree the checklist earlyDeciding what “ready” means on release day leads to pressure and shortcuts.
Test the rollbackA rollback plan that's never been tried is a hope, not a plan.
Know what can't be undoneData migrations, emails and payments often can't be rolled back; plan for them separately.
Release a little at a timeA canary or phased rollout turns one big risk into several small ones.
Remove old flagsEvery flag left in place adds complexity; set a date to take it out.
Make releases boringThe goal is releases so small and frequent that they stop needing a meeting.
Common pitfalls: treating deploy and release as the same thing; writing the checklist on the day; an untested rollback; forgetting support or customer-facing teams; leaving feature flags in forever; releasing on a Friday evening with no one around.

5After the session

Release day

Watch the monitoring closely and keep the agreed person on hand.

Within a week

Check the outcome measure, and roll out further if it's going well.

By the agreed date

Remove the feature flag and any old code.

Next retro

Review what went well and what to change for the next release.
