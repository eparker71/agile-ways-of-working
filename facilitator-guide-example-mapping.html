Example Mapping Guide

How to explore a story through rules, examples and questions, and decide whether it's ready.

“Lots of pink? We're not ready yet.”

Example Mapping is a short, structured conversation that builds a shared understanding of one story. Each rule (an acceptance criterion) goes on a blue card, concrete examples of each rule go on green cards, and anything nobody can answer goes on a pink question card. After 25 minutes, the shape of the map tells you whether the story is ready, too big, or not yet understood.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or use a digital board with four sticky colours
  • Yellow, blue, green and pink stickies (or index cards) and markers
  • Pick stories from the top of the backlog that are next in line for planning
  • Invite at least one person each for business, development and testing
  • A visible timer set to 25 minutes

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeStepFacilitator notes
0–2The storyThe PO writes the story on the yellow card and reads the As a / I want / so that statement.
2–8RulesList the rules the story must satisfy: one per blue card, across the top. These become the acceptance criteria.
8–18ExamplesFor each rule, give concrete examples on green cards beneath it: “Given a basket over £50, when…, then…”. Examples often reveal new rules.
—QuestionsWhenever nobody can answer something, write it on a pink card and move on. Don't let one question stall the session.
18–22Read the mapStep back. Many blue cards means the story is big, so split by rule. Many pink cards means it isn't ready yet.
22–25Thumb voteVote: ready to plan, needs splitting, or needs answers. Record the decision, any split, and who owns each question.

3Reading the map

The shape of the finished map tells you what to do next.

Yellow · Story

  • One story per session
  • If the group can't agree what it is, stop and clarify the value first

Blue · Rules

  • One per acceptance criterion
  • More than 4–5 rules? Split the story by rule
  • Rules with no examples aren't understood yet

Green · Examples

  • Concrete: real values and real users
  • Given / when / then works well
  • They become your acceptance tests

Pink · Questions

  • Note who will find out, and by when
  • Several pink cards means it isn't ready

Outcome strip

  • Ready, split or needs answers
  • Record any split as new stories
  • Give every question an owner

4Facilitation tips

Keep to 25 minutesThe timebox is the point. If you can't finish in 25 minutes, the story is too big or too unclear.
Examples, not scriptsKeep examples short and concrete. They're for conversation, not a formal test script.
Questions are progressA pink card is a discovered unknown, so better now than mid-sprint.
Three amigos minimumBusiness, development and testing perspectives each find different rules and examples.
Write as you talkThe facilitator, or the person speaking, writes the card straight away so nothing is lost.
Use the examplesTurn green cards into acceptance tests, such as BDD scenarios, so the conversation becomes the specification.
Common pitfalls: running past the timebox; debating solutions instead of rules; abstract examples (“a big basket”); leaving questions without an owner; mapping several stories at once; starting a story with lots of pink anyway.

5After the session

Straight away

Add the rules as acceptance criteria and the examples as test cases on the backlog item.

Questions

Owners find the answers before planning. Re-map briefly once they're answered.

Split stories

Add new stories from any split to the backlog, and map each one before it's planned.

During the sprint

Use the examples as the basis for automated acceptance tests.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Example Mapping”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Explore one story through its rules, concrete examples and open questions, and find out if it's ready.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Yellow = Story · Blue = Rule · Green = Example · Pink = Question

   Subtitle: Acceptance criterion
