Story Splitting Guide

How to use the poster to split large stories into thin, vertical slices during refinement.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Break Down Work · INVEST · SPIDR · Vertical Slicing
Open poster →SVG →

“If we could only ship half of this, which half?”

Stories that are too big hide risk, block flow and carry over from sprint to sprint. The poster gives teams a quick INVEST check, nine proven splitting patterns with a running example, and a reminder to slice vertically, so every piece delivers something a user can use. Pin it up in the team area and bring it to every refinement session.

1Before the session

  • Print the poster at A3 and pin it where the team refines its backlog
  • Pick the biggest or least clear stories from the top of the backlog
  • Make sure the PO is in the session: they decide which slices matter most
  • Bring the acceptance criteria, if any already exist

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeStepFacilitator notes
0–2Read the storyThe PO reads the story and why it matters. Clarify the user and the value.
2–5INVEST checkGo through the six letters. If Small fails, split. If Valuable fails, rethink or drop the story instead.
5–12Try patternsWork through the patterns in order, starting with workflow steps. For each, ask “could we split it this way?” Sketch the candidate slices on stickies.
12–15Choose the splitPick the split that lets the PO deprioritise a slice, or that gives slices of similar size. Check each slice is vertical.
15–18Check the slicesRun each new story through INVEST again. Still too big? Split again, often with a second pattern.
18–20Order & recordThe PO orders the new stories. Often the last slice can wait, or isn't needed at all.

3When each pattern fits

Clues in the story that point to a particular pattern.

1 · Workflow Steps

  • The story describes a process or journey
  • “…and then…” appears in the description

2 · Business Rules

  • Lots of “if”, “unless” or “except”
  • Different rules for different customers

3 · Happy / Unhappy Paths

  • Error handling dominates the discussion
  • Edge cases are making it look big

4 · Data Variations

  • Different types, formats or sources of data
  • “…for all countries / file types…”

5 · Interfaces

  • Multiple channels, devices or browsers
  • Several input methods

6 · Simple → Complex

  • A simple version would still help users
  • Complex calculations or logic

7 · Operations (CRUD)

  • The word “manage”
  • Create, edit and delete all lumped together

8 · Defer Performance

  • Speed or scale needs make it big
  • The basic function would already be valuable

9 · Spike First

  • Nobody can estimate it
  • A big technical or domain unknown

4Facilitation tips

Value in every sliceEach slice must be something a user, customer or stakeholder could see and give feedback on.
Never split by layer“Build the database” and “build the UI” aren't stories. They deliver nothing on their own.
Let the PO chooseA good split gives the PO options: most of the value early, and some slices they may never need.
Combine patternsLarge stories often need two passes: split by workflow, then by rules within one step.
Spikes are timeboxedA spike's output is knowledge. Keep it short (a day or two), then split the real work.
Aim for several per sprintSmaller stories flow better. A useful target is 5–15 stories finished per sprint.
Common pitfalls: splitting into technical tasks; slices that only deliver value once all are done; creating “part 1” and “part 2” stories with no value difference; using spikes to avoid committing; splitting so small that admin outweighs the work.

5After the session

Straight away

Replace the original story with the new slices in the backlog, ordered by the PO.

Next

Run Example Mapping on the top slice so it's clearly understood before planning.

Over time

Track how many stories carry over each sprint. Fewer carry-overs show splitting is working.

Retro

If big stories keep appearing, discuss it at the retro, and consider splitting earlier, at the story-map stage.
