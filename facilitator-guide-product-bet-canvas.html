Product Bet Canvas Guide

How to treat a new product or feature as a bet: state what you believe, find the riskiest assumptions and test them cheaply.

“What would have to be true for this to be a good bet?”

Every new product or feature is a bet: you're spending time and money on a belief that hasn't been proven. This canvas makes the bet explicit. Start with the problem and who has it, write the bet as one sentence, check why it's worth it, then list the riskiest assumptions against Marty Cagan's four big product risks (value, usability, feasibility and viability). Plan the cheapest tests, agree measures of success and a point to stop, and finish with a clear decision: go, test first, park or no.

1Before the session

  • Print the canvas at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Bring what you know about the problem: research, data, support tickets, quotes
  • Invite someone who'll challenge the idea, not just its champion
  • Agree who makes the decision at the end

3The four big risks

Test the weakest one first.

Value

  • Will they want it?
  • Will they pay or use it?

Usability

  • Can they use it?
  • Without help?

Feasibility

  • Can we build it?
  • With our skills and time?

Viability

  • Does it work for us?
  • Legal, cost, brand, sales

Decision

  • Go, test, park or no
  • With a reason

4Facilitation tips

Problem firstIf you can't describe the problem in the user's words, you're betting on a solution.
Low confidence is usefulA 1 or 2 isn't failure; it tells you exactly what to test next.
Cheapest test winsInterviews, a fake door, a prototype or a concierge test usually beat building it.
Set the stop line nowAgree when you'd stop before you start; it's much harder once you've invested.
Park is a real answer“Not now” with a revisit date keeps good ideas without clogging the backlog.
Revisit after testsUpdate the confidence scores once results are in, and decide again.
Common pitfalls: writing the solution as the problem; scoring every assumption 4 or 5; skipping viability; tests that can't fail; no measures or stop line; treating the canvas as a business case to win approval.

5After the session

Straight away

Share the canvas with the decision and its reasons.

As tests finish

Update the confidence scores and revisit the decision.

If it's a go

Add it to the roadmap and break it down with the Epic Breakdown Canvas.

At the measure dates

Check the results against the targets and the stop line.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Product Bet Canvas”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Treat a new product or feature as a bet: who has the problem, what you believe, the riskiest assumptions, and how you'll find out cheaply.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Idea, Owner, Date

Sections:
1. The Problem: top left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Whose, and how bad?
   Content: Who has it: · The problem, in their words: · How they cope today: · How often, how painful (1–5):
2. Our Bet: top right, about 65% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: One sentence, four parts
   Content: We believe that (who): · will (do what): · if we (offer what): · because (what we know): · The smallest version we could try:
3. Why It's Worth It: middle left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Subtitle: Value and cost
   Content: Value to users: · Value to us: · Cost of doing nothing: · Roughly what it'll take:
4. Riskiest Assumptions: middle centre, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Confidence 1 (guess) – 5 (proven)
   Content: Risk: · We're assuming…: · Conf.: · Value · Will they want it? · Usability · Can they use it? · Feasibility · Can we build it? · Viability · Does it work for us?
5. How We'll Test It: middle right, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Cheapest first
   Content: Test: · Signal we need: · By when:
6. Measures of Success: bottom left, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: How we'll know it paid off
   Content: Measure: · Now: · Target: · By when: · We'll stop or change course if:
7. Decision: bottom right, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Go, test, park or no
   Content: Go: build the smallest version · Test first: run the tests above · Park it: revisit later · No: not worth it, and here's why · Why: · Decided by: · Revisit on:
