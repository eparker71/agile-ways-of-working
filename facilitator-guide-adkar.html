ADKAR Change Readiness Guide

How to find where people are in a change, spot the barrier point, and act on the right thing first.

“What's the first thing stopping them, and what would help most?”

Organisations change only when people do, one person at a time. The ADKAR® model, created by Jeff Hiatt and developed by Prosci, describes five things each person needs, in order: Awareness of why the change is needed, Desire to support it, Knowledge of how to change, Ability to do it in practice, and Reinforcement to make it stick. Scoring each element shows the barrier point: the first one that's missing. Effort spent on later elements is wasted until it's fixed; training doesn't help people who don't yet see why. Use it for any change, from a new way of working to a new tool or an agile transformation. ADKAR® is a registered trademark of Prosci, Inc.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Agree the change you're assessing, and its sponsor
  • List the groups or people affected; the Stakeholder Map is a good source
  • Gather evidence: survey results, questions people ask, adoption data, manager feedback
  • Decide whether to score groups together or ask people to score themselves (often more honest)

3The five elements

Each must be in place before the next can work.

Awareness

  • Do people know why?
  • Sponsor and managers explain it
  • Share the cost of not changing

Desire

  • Do people want to take part?
  • Involve them in shaping it
  • Address what's in it for them

Knowledge

  • Do people know how?
  • Training, guides, examples

Ability

  • Can they do it for real?
  • Coaching, practice, time
  • Remove blockers

Reinforcement

  • Will it last?
  • Recognition, measures
  • Stop rewarding the old way

Barrier point

  • The first score of 3 or lower
  • Fix it before later elements

4Facilitation tips

Order mattersTraining (Knowledge) won't help if people don't see why (Awareness) or don't want to (Desire).
Score from evidenceUse what people say and do, not what leaders hope. Self-scores are often more honest.
Groups differDifferent groups often have different barrier points. Plan separate actions.
Desire is personalIt can't be ordered. Listen to concerns and involve people in shaping the change.
Ability needs timeKnowing isn't doing. Plan practice, coaching and pairing, and expect a dip at first.
Don't skip reinforcementWithout it, people drift back. Measure adoption and recognise it.
Common pitfalls: starting with training; scoring from leaders' assumptions; one plan for everyone; treating resistance as a character flaw; declaring success at go-live; never re-assessing.

5After the session

Straight away

Share the barrier points and actions with the sponsor and managers.

Each week

Owners act on their barrier points; managers check in with their teams.

Monthly

Re-score the grid. Barrier points should move right as the change takes hold.

After go-live

Keep reinforcing and measuring adoption until the new way is normal.

6How it fits the toolkit

ADKAR is about the people side of change. Use the Stakeholder Map to find who's affected, the Skills Matrix and Pairing & Mobbing to build Ability, the Team Health Check to see how teams are coping, and Value Metrics to show the change is paying off.
