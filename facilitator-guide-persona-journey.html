“What is this person really trying to get done?”

A persona is a short, evidence-based description of a type of user: their context, goals and frustrations. A customer journey map follows that person through a scenario in stages, showing what they do, think and feel, where it goes wrong, and where you could help. The board also frames the journey as a job to be done (“When…, I want to…, so I can…”), an idea from Clayton Christensen and others. Opportunities found here feed the Opportunity Solution Tree.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Bring real evidence: interviews, support tickets, analytics, sales notes
  • Agree which user type and which scenario to map first
  • Invite people who talk to users: support, sales, customer success
  • Yellow, blue, pink and green stickies, and markers

3Making each part strong

Quality checks for the persona and the journey.

Persona

  • Based on evidence
  • Specific, not average
  • Guesses are marked

Job to Be Done

  • A situation, a motivation, an outcome
  • Not a feature

Doing & Thinking

  • In the user's words
  • Every stage covered

Feeling

  • Highs and lows shown
  • Lows explained

Pain points & Opportunities

  • Opportunities match pain points
  • Ranked

4Facilitation tips

Evidence, not imaginationA persona invented in the room reflects the team's assumptions. Ground it in real data.
One persona per mapDifferent users have different journeys. Map them separately.
Include before and afterJourneys often start before people reach your product, and end after.
The lows matter mostThe deepest dips in the feeling line are usually the biggest opportunities.
Invite the front lineSupport and sales staff hear pain points every day.
Validate itShow the map to a few real users and ask what's missing.
Common pitfalls: personas based on demographics, not needs; one map for every user; mapping your internal process instead of the user's journey; no evidence; filing the map away instead of using it.

5After the session

Straight away

Take the top opportunities into the Opportunity Solution Tree.

This week

Check guesses on the persona with a few user interviews.

Next

Use the journey to choose the backbone of a Story Map.

Every few months

Update the persona and map as you learn.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Persona & Journey Map”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Describe a real type of user, then map their journey: what they do, think and feel, and where to help.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Yellow = Step · Blue = Thought · Pink = Pain point · Green = Opportunity

Sections:
1. Persona: top left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Based on evidence, not guesses
   Content: Name: · Role / context: · Tech comfort: · Goals: · Frustrations: · In their words: · Evidence from:
2. The Journey We're Mapping: top right, about 65% wide and 35% tall; header colour blue.
   Content: Scenario or goal: · Starts when: · Ends when: · Job to be done: · When… · I want to… · so I can…
3. Journey Map: bottom full width, about 100% wide and 60% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Stages left to right · one row at a time
   Content: Stage 1: · Stage 2: · Stage 3: · Stage 4: · Stage 5: · Doing · Steps they take · Thinking · Questions, doubts · Feeling · draw the emotion line across the stages · Highs and lows · Pain points · What goes wrong · Opportunities · How we could help

Footer questions: “What is this person really trying to get done?” “Where in the journey do we lose them, and why?”
