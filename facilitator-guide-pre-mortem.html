Pre-mortem Guide

How to surface the risks people are quietly worried about, by imagining the work has already failed.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Run a Project · Inspired by Gary Klein's Pre-mortem

“It's six months from now and this has failed. What happened?”

Asking “what could go wrong?” gets polite, cautious answers. A pre-mortem, developed by psychologist Gary Klein, asks the group to imagine the project has already failed and explain why. This prospective hindsight makes it safe to voice worries, and it surfaces far more risks. The group then votes on the most likely causes and agrees how to prevent them, or at least detect them early.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Make sure everyone understands the plan being tested: share it beforehand
  • Pink stickies, markers and dot stickers (3 per person)
  • Invite people with different views, including sceptics
  • Bring the RAID Log so top risks can be added straight away

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · The Scenario

  • A specific future date
  • Failure is described concretely

2 · Why It Failed

  • Written in the past tense
  • Silent writing first
  • Wide range of causes

3 · Top Risks

  • Chosen by vote
  • Likelihood and impact noted

4 · Prevent or Detect

  • A real prevention or a clear warning sign
  • Every risk has an owner

5 · Next Steps

  • Risks reach the RAID Log
  • A review date is set

4Facilitation tips

Past tense matters“We lost our key engineer” feels more real than “we might lose someone”, and gets better answers.
Silent firstWriting alone stops the most senior or loudest person shaping everyone's thinking.
Welcome the uncomfortableThe most valuable reasons are often about decisions, leadership or politics. Thank people for them.
Not just doomClose by focusing on what you'll do. The goal is a better plan, not a gloomy team.
Prevent or detectIf a risk can't be prevented, agree the early warning sign that would tell you it's happening.
Repeat itRun a short pre-mortem again before each big milestone.
Common pitfalls: discussing reasons before everyone has written; the sponsor dismissing reasons in the room; only listing technical risks; no owners for actions; not feeding risks into the RAID Log or the plan.

5After the session

Straight away

Add the top risks to the RAID Log and the prevention actions to the backlog.

This week

Put the early warning signs where the team will notice them.

Each iteration

Check the warning signs at the review or retro.

Before milestones

Run a quick pre-mortem again on the next phase.

6How it fits the toolkit

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Pre-mortem”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Imagine the work has already failed, explain why, then act now to prevent the most likely causes.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Pink Sticky = Why it failed · Dot = Vote (3 per person)

Sections:
1. The Scenario: top full width, about 100% wide and 20% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Read it aloud · speak as if it has already happened
   Content: It's · and this has failed badly. What went wrong? · Project, plan or release: · Failure looks like:
2. Why It Failed: middle left, about 65% wide and 45% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Silent writing first · one reason per sticky
   Content: Everyone writes alone for 5 minutes, in the past tense: “We lost our key supplier” · Think wide: people, tech, customers, money, dependencies, decisions · Pink stickies, clustered by theme
3. Top Risks: middle right, about 35% wide and 45% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: After dot voting
   Content: # · Reason: · Likely: · Impact: · Votes:
4. Prevent or Detect: bottom left, about 65% wide and 35% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: For each top risk
   Content: Top risk: · We'll prevent it by…: · Early warning sign: · Owner:
5. Next Steps: bottom right, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: Top risks added to the RAID Log · Prevention actions added to the backlog · Warning signs visible on the team board · Plan or scope changed where needed · Re-run before the next big milestone · Review these risks on:

Footer questions: “It's six months from now and this has failed. What happened?” “Which of these are we quietly hoping won't happen?”
