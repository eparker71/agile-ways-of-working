Architecture Decision Record Guide

How to record important technical decisions so future team members understand what was decided, and why.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Manage Flow · Inspired by Michael Nygard's ADR Format

“Will someone joining next year understand why we did this?”

Teams make important technical decisions all the time, then forget why. An architecture decision record (ADR), a format described by Michael Nygard, is a short document for one decision: its context, the drivers that matter, the options considered, the decision itself, and its consequences. Kept together, usually alongside the code, ADRs become a history of the system's design that new people can read in an afternoon.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or copy the structure into a Markdown file in your repository
  • Agree which decisions deserve an ADR: hard to reverse, affecting several teams, or often questioned
  • Gather the facts: requirements, constraints, any prototypes or measurements
  • Invite the people who will live with the decision

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–51 · Title & StatusGive it a number and a short, present-tense title. Mark it Proposed.
5–152 · ContextDescribe the problem and the forces at play, without arguing for a solution yet.
15–203 · Decision DriversList what matters most, such as cost, performance, skills or reversibility.
20–404 · OptionsCompare at least two options, including doing nothing, against the drivers.
40–505 · DecisionWrite the decision: “We will…”. Mark it Accepted once agreed.
50–606 · ConsequencesRecord what becomes easier and what becomes harder.

3Making each part strong

Quality checks for an ADR.

Title & Status

  • Short and specific
  • Status kept up to date

Context

  • Facts and forces
  • Readable by a newcomer

Decision Drivers

  • Ranked
  • Agreed before choosing

Options Considered

  • At least two
  • Honest pros and cons

Decision & Consequences

  • Clear “We will…”
  • Trade-offs written down

4Facilitation tips

Keep it shortOne or two pages. If it's longer, it's probably several decisions.
Write it at the timeRecord decisions when they're made, while the reasons are fresh.
Never edit, supersedeWhen a decision changes, write a new ADR and mark the old one Superseded.
Store it with the codeA folder of numbered Markdown files in the repository is easy to find and review.
Include the rejectedOptions you rejected, and why, save the next team from repeating the analysis.
Review like codePropose ADRs in a pull request so others can comment.
Common pitfalls: writing ADRs for trivial choices; skipping the options; editing old records instead of superseding them; storing them where nobody looks; writing them months after the decision.

5After the session

Straight away

Save the ADR with a number in your repository or wiki.

When it changes

Write a new ADR and mark the old one Superseded.

Onboarding

Point new team members to the ADR folder.

Quarterly

Review open Proposed ADRs and decide them.
