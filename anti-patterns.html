Anti-Pattern Catalogue

42 common anti-patterns, each with why it hurts, what to try, and the tool that helps. Start from a symptom you recognise. To find them with your team, use the Anti-Pattern Spotter board. For one-off situations, such as a deadline moving or a key person leaving, see the Scenario Playbook.

Events 6

The daily sync is a status report to the manager

Why it hurts: People talk to one person instead of planning together, so the team stops owning the day.

Try this: Walk the board, not the people. Rotate the facilitator, and ask managers to listen rather than lead.

Tools: Daily Sync

The same issues come up at every retrospective

Why it hurts: Nothing changes, so people stop raising problems at all.

Try this: Limit actions to one to three, each with an owner. Start each retro by reviewing the last actions, and dig into causes.

Tools: Retrospective Kit · Root Cause Analysis

The review is a one-way demo with no feedback

Why it hurts: Stakeholders watch politely, the backlog doesn't change, and the team learns nothing.

Try this: Let stakeholders use the product. End by showing what changed in the backlog because of their feedback.

Tools: Sprint Review

Planning is just dragging tickets into the iteration

Why it hurts: Without a goal there's no way to make sensible trade-offs when plans change.

Try this: Agree the goal first, then select work that fits real capacity.

Tools: Sprint Planning

Meetings overrun and nobody is sure why they exist

Why it hurts: Time and energy drain away from the work itself.

Try this: Give every event a purpose, an output and a timebox. Merge or drop the ones without them.

Tools: Events & Cadences · Facilitation Techniques

Refinement happens during planning

Why it hurts: Planning turns into long design debates, and work starts late or unclear.

Try this: Hold a short weekly refinement and refine one or two iterations ahead.

Tools: Backlog Refinement · Example Mapping

Backlog 6

Stories are technical tasks, such as “set up the database”

Why it hurts: Nothing is usable until every layer is done, so value and feedback arrive late.

Try this: Slice vertically, through every layer, so each story is something a user can use.

Tools: Story Splitting · Backlog Item Anatomy

Epics have no outcome or measure

Why it hurts: Nobody can tell whether the epic worked, or when to stop investing in it.

Try this: Write each epic as a hypothesis with a measurable outcome.

Tools: Epic Breakdown Canvas · Backlog Item Anatomy

The backlog has hundreds of items, some years old

Why it hurts: It's impossible to see what matters, and refinement effort is wasted.

Try this: Archive anything untouched for six months, and keep a short, ordered top of the backlog.

Tools: Prioritisation Board

Everything is a must-have or top priority

Why it hurts: Priorities stop meaning anything, and teams spread themselves thin.

Try this: Rank by cost of delay, and protect a genuine “could have” list.

Tools: Prioritisation Board

Stories carry over from one iteration to the next

Why it hurts: Items are too big or unclear, and forecasts keep slipping.

Try this: Split items smaller, and check each is understood with Example Mapping before starting.

Tools: Story Splitting · Example Mapping · Ageing WIP & SLE

Acceptance criteria are written after the work

Why it hurts: Developers and testers disagree about what done means, so rework follows.

Try this: Agree rules and examples together before work starts.

Tools: Example Mapping

Flow 6

Everyone has several things in progress at once

Why it hurts: Work waits between people and nothing finishes.

Try this: Set WIP limits, and stop starting so you can start finishing.

Tools: Kanban Board Design · Buffering & Swarming

Work sits blocked for days without anyone noticing

Why it hurts: Ageing items quietly become late items.

Try this: Show age on every card, review the oldest items daily, and swarm on anything past your SLE.

Tools: Ageing WIP & SLE · Buffering & Swarming · Blocker Clustering

“Done” means “development done”

Why it hurts: Hidden work piles up in testing and release, and delivery surprises everyone.

Try this: Strengthen the Definition of Done, and make waiting steps visible on the board.

Tools: Team Charter · Kanban Board Design

Unplanned work wrecks every plan

Why it hurts: The team keeps missing what it planned, and trust erodes.

Try this: Measure how much unplanned work arrives, and plan a buffer for it.

Tools: Buffering & Swarming

One person is the only one who can do a key task

Why it hurts: Work queues for them, and a holiday or illness stops delivery.

Try this: Pair to spread the skill, and check the bus factor with a skills matrix.

Tools: Skills Matrix · Pairing & Mobbing

The same blockers keep coming back

Why it hurts: Teams fix symptoms one at a time while the cause stays.

Try this: Log blockers for a few weeks, cluster them, and fix the biggest cluster.

Tools: Blocker Clustering · Root Cause Analysis

Teams & roles 6

The product owner is rarely available

Why it hurts: Questions wait, the team guesses, and the wrong things get built.

Try this: Agree regular PO time, and make the cost of waiting visible.

Tools: Team Charter · RACI & Decision Log

The Scrum Master has become the team's secretary

Why it hurts: Coaching stops, and the team stops owning its own process.

Try this: Hand the tools back to the team and rotate facilitation.

Tools: Scrum Master & Agile Coach Cheat Sheet · Facilitation Techniques

Nobody is sure who makes which decisions

Why it hurts: Decisions stall, or get made twice by different people.

Try this: Make decision rights explicit, area by area.

Tools: Delegation Board · RACI & Decision Log

Quiet people rarely speak in meetings

Why it hurts: Ideas and risks go unheard until it's too late.

Try this: Use silent writing and round-robins, and check psychological safety.

Tools: Facilitation Techniques · Psychological Safety Check

The team avoids open disagreement

Why it hurts: Problems are discussed in corridors instead of in the room, and decisions don't stick.

Try this: Agree how the team handles disagreement, and use an agreement scale to surface real views.

Tools: Team Charter · Psychological Safety Check

A new team starts work without agreeing how to work

Why it hurts: Friction surfaces later, under delivery pressure, when it's harder to fix.

Try this: Run a short, timeboxed team launch and write a team charter.

Tools: Team Launch (Sprint Zero) · Team Charter

Multi-team 6

Teams are constantly waiting on each other

Why it hurts: Lead times grow and plans slip together.

Try this: Make dependencies visible, then remove them by changing team boundaries or skills.

Tools: Dependency Map · Team Topology Design

The cross-team sync is a status meeting

Why it hurts: Real blockers between teams don't get solved.

Try this: Focus the sync on dependencies and blockers, and give each impediment an owner.

Tools: Cross-Team Sync

Multi-team planning is a top-down announcement

Why it hurts: Teams don't believe the plan, so it isn't really a plan.

Try this: Let teams plan their own work against shared objectives, and take a confidence vote.

Tools: Cross-Team Planning

One team owns far too much

Why it hurts: Quality and speed drop, and people burn out.

Try this: Check cognitive load, then split the team's responsibilities or add a platform.

Tools: Team Topology Design

Problems between teams never get fixed

Why it hurts: The same issues repeat every planning period.

Try this: Hold a cross-team retrospective and solve one system problem together.

Tools: Cross-Team Retro

Teams' velocity is compared

Why it hurts: Points inflate, numbers lose meaning, and trust drops.

Try this: Never compare teams' points. Use flow and outcome measures instead.

Tools: Estimation · Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt

Leadership 6

Far too many initiatives are in flight

Why it hurts: Everything moves slowly and nothing finishes.

Try this: Limit portfolio WIP. Stop something to start something.

Tools: Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix

Change is announced and expected to stick

Why it hurts: People comply on the surface, then drift back to the old way.

Try this: Find each group's barrier point and work on that first.

Tools: ADKAR Change Readiness

Funding is decided once a year

Why it hurts: Teams can't adapt to what they learn during the year.

Try this: Fund MVPs first, and review the portfolio regularly with evidence.

Tools: Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix · Value Metrics

Agile is mandated as a process rather than a goal

Why it hurts: Teams go through the motions without the benefits.

Try this: Agree the outcomes you want, and let teams choose the practices that get them there.

Tools: Value Metrics · Events & Cadences

Bad news travels slowly upwards

Why it hurts: Problems surface too late to fix cheaply.

Try this: Report confidence and trends, and thank people who bring bad news early.

Tools: Outcome Status Report · Psychological Safety Check

Roadmaps are lists of features with dates

Why it hurts: Teams build what was promised, not what turns out to be valuable.

Try this: Make roadmaps about outcomes: Now, Next and Later.

Tools: Prioritisation Board · Objectives & Key Results

Metrics & reporting 6

Velocity is used as a target

Why it hurts: Points inflate and the numbers stop meaning anything.

Try this: Use velocity only for the team's own forecasting, or count items instead.

Tools: Estimation · Release Forecast & Burn-up

Single delivery dates are promised

Why it hurts: Dates slip and trust erodes each time.

Try this: Forecast with ranges based on real throughput.

Tools: Release Forecast & Burn-up

Status is green until it's suddenly red

Why it hurts: Problems are hidden until it's too late to act.

Try this: Report outcomes, a confidence rating and its trend, instead of traffic lights.

Tools: Outcome Status Report

People are measured by tickets closed

Why it hurts: People game the numbers and collaborate less.

Try this: Measure team outcomes and flow, never individuals.

Tools: Value Metrics · Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt

OKRs are task lists

Why it hurts: Effort gets measured instead of results.

Try this: Write key results as measurable outcomes.

Tools: Objectives & Key Results

Delivery gets faster while quality drops

Why it hurts: Incidents and rework grow, and the speed doesn't last.

Try this: Balance speed metrics with stability ones, and learn from every incident.

Tools: Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt · Blameless Incident Review
