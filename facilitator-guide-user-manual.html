Personal User Manual Guide

How to help team members share how they work best, so the team spends less time guessing about each other.

“What's one thing about working with you that would save your teammates time?”

Every team spends time learning, often by trial and error, how each person likes to work. A personal user manual shortcuts that: each person writes a short guide to working with them, covering when they do their best work, how to reach them, how they like feedback, what drains and energises them, what people misread, and what to ask them about. The practice was popularised by Atlassian's Team Playbook. Each board holds four people, so print one per four team members.

1Before the session

  • Print one board per four people at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG on a digital whiteboard
  • Make it optional: invite, don't require. People share what they're comfortable sharing
  • Fill in your own first, as an example of honest, practical answers
  • Plan for remote members: a digital copy, or a photo of theirs added afterwards

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeStepFacilitator notes
0–5Set it upExplain why: less guessing, fewer misunderstandings. Share yours as an example.
5–20WriteEach person fills in their block in silence. Practical beats profound.
20–35ReadSilent reading of everyone's. People note one thing that surprised them.
35–45One change eachEach person names one thing they'll do differently, for one teammate.

3Getting useful answers

Practical, specific and current.

Best work

  • Times, places, conditions
  • Focus time needs

Reaching me

  • Channel and response time
  • What's urgent to me

Feedback

  • Written or spoken
  • Straight away or later

Drains & energises

  • Meetings, interruptions
  • What lights me up

Misread & ask me

  • Quirks explained
  • Skills to share

4Facilitation tips

Lead by exampleIf the facilitator or leader shares honestly first, others follow.
Practical, not personality“I reply to messages twice a day” helps more than a personality type.
Keep it optionalSome people share less at first. That's fine; trust grows over time.
Make it visibleKeep the boards on the team wall or wiki so new joiners can read them.
Revisit itUpdate manuals every six months or when the team changes.
Pair it with the charterPatterns across manuals often become team working agreements.
Common pitfalls: making it compulsory; turning it into a personality test; writing what sounds good rather than what's true; never reading each other's; filing it away and never updating it.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the boards and store them where the team works.

Next charter review

Turn common patterns into working agreements.

When someone joins

Add their manual to their onboarding plan.

Every six months

Update your own, and reread your teammates'.

6How it fits the toolkit

User manuals sit alongside the Team Charter (agreements for the whole team), Team Onboarding (give new joiners the manuals to read) and the Feedback Conversation board, which works better when you know how someone likes to hear feedback.
