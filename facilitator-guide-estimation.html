Estimation Guide

How to size work quickly and fairly with planning poker, T-shirt sizing or affinity estimation, or skip it.

“Is this bigger or smaller than the last thing we finished?”

Estimates help teams plan and spot items that are too big, but they cost time and are often misused. The board offers three ways to estimate relatively, comparing items with each other rather than guessing hours. Planning poker (described by James Grenning, popularised by Mike Cohn) suits a handful of items. T-shirt sizing roughly sorts many items. Affinity estimation sizes a whole backlog in an hour. It also suggests the alternative many teams find works as well: split items small and count them.

2Agenda & run-sheet

UseTechniqueHow to run it
A few items1 · Planning PokerRead the item, everyone picks a card privately, reveal together. The highest and lowest explain, then re-vote. Two rounds at most.
Many items2 · T-Shirt SizingAgree what a reference “M” is, then place each item in XS to XL. Discuss only items people disagree on.
A whole backlog3 · Affinity EstimationSilently place items left to right by size. Anyone can move anything. Discuss items that keep moving, then draw boundaries.
Any4 · Reference ItemsKeep shared examples for each size, and compare new items against them.
Consider5 · Don't EstimateIf items are split to finish within your SLE, count them and forecast from throughput instead.

3Choosing a technique

Pick the lightest technique that gives you what you need.

Planning Poker

  • A few items in detail
  • Surfaces different assumptions

T-Shirt Sizing

  • Rough sizes for roadmaps
  • Quick for many items

Affinity Estimation

  • A whole backlog
  • Mostly silent, very fast

Reference Items

  • Keep sizes consistent
  • Update occasionally

Don't Estimate

  • Right-size and count
  • Forecast from throughput

4Facilitation tips

The conversation is the valueWhen estimates differ, the discussion reveals hidden work or risk.
Relative beats absolutePeople compare sizes far better than they guess hours.
Don't averageIf votes differ widely, talk and re-vote. Averaging hides the disagreement.
Split the big onesAnything at 13 or above is usually too big. Split it before estimating again.
Estimates aren't promisesUse them to forecast, never to judge people or compare teams.
Try countingMany teams find counting small items forecasts as accurately as points, with less effort.
Common pitfalls: estimating in hours; managers estimating for the team; averaging votes; treating points as a performance measure; comparing velocity across teams; spending longer estimating than the work would take.

5After the session

Straight away

Record sizes in your backlog tool and note any items to split.

Planning

Use sizes or counts with recent throughput to plan capacity.

Each retro

Compare forecasts with what happened, and adjust.

Every few months

Try a few iterations of counting items, and compare the forecast accuracy.
