Sprint Planning Guide

How to agree a sprint goal, size the work to real capacity, and leave with a plan the team believes in.

“If we only achieve one thing this sprint, what should it be?”

Planning that is just dragging tickets into a sprint produces a list, not a plan. The board puts the sprint goal first, checks the work against real capacity, splits items into those needed for the goal and those that are also planned, and ends with a confidence vote so concerns surface before the sprint starts, not halfway through. It works for any iteration length. Scrum calls this event Sprint Planning.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Make sure the top of the backlog is refined and ordered (Example Mapping helps)
  • Know who is away, and the team's throughput or velocity for the last three sprints
  • Bring the last Sprint Review's feedback and the last retro's actions
  • Yellow and pink stickies, markers, and the team's Definition of Done

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10ContextThe PO shares the product goal, recent feedback, and what's at the top of the backlog. Check the inputs.
10–251 · Sprint GoalDraft a goal that explains why the sprint is valuable. Agree how you'll know it's achieved.
25–352 · CapacityCount working days, absences and other commitments. Compare with recent throughput.
35–654 · Selected ItemsPull items in priority order into “Needed for the goal”, then “Also planned”. Stop at capacity.
65–805 · Risks & DependenciesWhat could stop the goal? Who outside the team is needed, and when? Agree actions.
80–906 · How We'll StartAgree what starts on day one, who pairs or swarms, and the first thing to finish.
90–957 · Confidence VoteFist of five. Anyone at 1 or 2 explains why; adjust the plan and re-vote.

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Sprint Goal

  • One objective, not a list of items
  • Says why, not just what
  • Linked to the product goal

2 · Capacity

  • Absences are counted
  • Based on recent throughput, not hope

3 · Inputs Checked

  • Review feedback and retro actions included
  • The top items are ready

4 · Selected Items

  • Goal items come first
  • Room left for unplanned work
  • Items are small

5 · Risks & Dependencies

  • Each risk has an action
  • External people know they're needed

6 · How We'll Start

  • Focus on finishing, not starting everything
  • Pairing or swarming planned

7 · Confidence Vote

  • Low votes are explored, not overruled
  • Re-vote after changes

4Facilitation tips

Goal first, items secondAgree why the sprint matters before choosing items. The goal guides trade-offs mid-sprint.
Plan to recent realityUse the last three sprints' throughput. Planning to an ideal leads to carry-over and lost trust.
Leave slackPlan to about 80% of capacity. Unplanned work always arrives.
It's a forecastThe team commits to the goal, not to every item. Say so out loud.
Plan the startA clear first day stops everyone starting a different item at once.
Respect low votesA 1 or a 2 is information. Ask what would raise it, and adjust the plan.
Common pitfalls: no sprint goal, or a goal that just lists items; ignoring absences; planning to 100% capacity; the PO assigning work to people; skipping the confidence vote; carrying over unfinished work without discussion.

5After the session

Straight away

Put the goal on the team board and update the backlog tool. Photograph the board.

Every day

At the daily sync, ask: are we on track for the sprint goal? What's blocking it?

Mid-sprint

If the goal is at risk, renegotiate scope with the PO early. Don't wait for the review.

End of sprint

Bring the goal to the Sprint Review and the retro: did we achieve it, and why?

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Sprint Planning”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Agree why this sprint matters, what can be done, and how the team will start, in one visible plan.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Team, Sprint Dates

Sections:
1. Sprint Goal: top left, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Why is this sprint valuable?
   Content: By the end of this sprint… · We'll know we've achieved it when… · It supports this product goal:
2. Capacity: top centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Be realistic
   Content: Working days: · People available: · Holidays & absences: · Other commitments: · Recent throughput (last 3): · Capacity this sprint:
3. Inputs Checked: top right, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: Product goal & roadmap reviewed · Top of the backlog is ready · Last review's feedback included · Retro actions planned in · Dependencies known · Definition of Done visible
4. Selected Items: middle centre, about 75% wide and 45% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: In priority order · stop when capacity is reached
   Content: Needed for the Sprint Goal · Also planned · If we only finish these, the sprint still succeeds · Valuable, but they can move if the goal is at risk
5. Risks & Dependencies: middle right, about 25% wide and 45% tall; header colour red.
   Content: What could stop us reaching the goal? · Who outside the team do we need, and when?
6. How We'll Start: bottom left, about 50% wide and 20% tall; header colour sky blue.
   Subtitle: The first days
   Content: Day 1, we start with: · Pairing / swarming on: · First thing to finish:
7. Confidence Vote: bottom right, about 50% wide and 20% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Fist of five
   Content: 1. 2 · 3. 4 · 5 · No confidence · Serious concerns · Some concerns · Confident · Very confident · Any 1s or 2s? Concerns raised:

Footer questions: “If we only achieve one thing this sprint, what should it be?” “What would stop us from reaching the goal?”
