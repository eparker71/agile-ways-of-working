Motivation & Recognition Guide

How to find out what motivates each person, test how a change will affect them, and recognise good work specifically.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Lead the Organisation · Inspired by Management 3.0

“Which motivator would this change put at risk, and for whom?”

People are motivated by different things, and leaders often guess wrong. This board combines two practices from Jurgen Appelo's Management 3.0. In Moving Motivators, each person ranks ten intrinsic motivators (curiosity, honour, acceptance, mastery, power, freedom, relatedness, order, goal and status) from least to most important, then moves each one up or down to show how a planned change would affect it. Kudo cards make recognition a habit: a short, specific thank-you that anyone can give anyone.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, one per person, or load the SVG on a digital whiteboard
  • Write the ten motivators on cards or stickies, one set per person
  • If you're testing a change, describe it in one sentence beforehand
  • Do your own first, and be ready to share it

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–51 · The Ten MotivatorsRead through them. Each person may interpret a motivator in their own way; that's fine.
5–152 · RankEach person lays out their cards from least important (left) to most important (right). No discussion yet.
15–252 · Test a changeRead the change aloud. Move each card up if it would motivate you more, down if less.
25–403 · What I LearnedShare what you choose: surprises, what you need more of, what you'll ask for.
40–504 · Kudo CardsEach person writes at least one specific thank-you, then gives or posts them.

3Reading the results

Patterns are more useful than single cards.

Top three

  • What to protect
  • in any change

Bottom three

  • Less important now
  • May change over time

Moved down

  • Risks in the change
  • Talk about these first

Moved up

  • What the change offers
  • Build on it

Kudos

  • Specific behaviour
  • Given soon, often

4Facilitation tips

No right answersA ranking says what matters to someone now, not who they are; it changes over time.
Share by choiceSome people won't want to show their cards. Respect that; the private insight still helps.
Focus on the movesThe cards that move down when you describe a change are where to listen.
Kudos for behaviourThank people for something specific they did, not for being great.
Peer to peerRecognition from teammates often means more than recognition from a manager.
Not a reward schemeNever link kudos to pay or targets; that turns thanks into a transaction.
Common pitfalls: using rankings to judge or label people; forcing people to share; testing a change that's already decided; kudo cards that are vague or only from managers; turning kudos into a competition.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the rankings (with permission) and note what each person needs.

In 1:1s

Revisit the top motivators every few months.

Before a change

Run the change test with the people affected.

Every week

Keep a kudo wall or channel going.
