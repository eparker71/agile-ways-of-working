Right-Size Your Structure Guide

How to decide how much structure a project needs as it grows from one team to many, and spot when you have too little or too much.

“What's the least structure that would keep this project safe?”

A project of four people and a project of forty need different things. The work isn't harder; the coordination is. The number of links between people grows much faster than the number of people: four people have 6, forty have 780. Fred Brooks made the point in The Mythical Man-Month: adding people adds communication overhead. The poster shows three levels, one team, a team of teams and a programme, and what planning, roles, events, tracking, risks, reporting and decisions need at each, with the tool to use. Size isn't the only factor, so it also lists what should turn the dial up, such as risk, regulation or many dependencies. The size bands are rules of thumb drawn from Scrum's guidance of ten or fewer people per team, SAFe's release trains of 50 to 125 people, and Team Topologies; adjust them to your context.

3The three levels

Add structure when it solves a problem you have.

One Team

  • 3–10 people
  • Conversation over documents
  • One board, short events

Team of Teams

  • 10–50 people, 2–5 teams
  • Shared planning and sync
  • RAID log, decision log

Programme

  • 50+ people
  • Outcomes at the top
  • Portfolio and governance

Turn the Dial Up

  • Risk and regulation
  • Dependencies, fixed dates
  • Distributed teams

Wrong Size

  • Too little: surprises
  • Too much: bureaucracy

4Facilitation tips

Start lightBegin with the least structure that could work, then add what problems show you need.
Remove as well as addEach quarter, ask what you could stop doing. Structure tends to pile up.
Same tools, more of themBigger projects mostly add cross-team versions of team practices, not a different method.
Watch the transitionsProblems often appear when a project grows past a level boundary without changing how it works.
Size isn't everythingA small, high-risk or regulated project may need more structure than a big, simple one.
Keep teams smallGrow by adding teams, not by making teams bigger; keep each team to about ten people or fewer.
Common pitfalls: using the big-programme toolkit on a four-person project; keeping one-team habits at forty people; adding reports and meetings without removing any; treating the size bands as rules; growing one team past twelve or so instead of splitting it.

6How it fits the toolkit

Use the poster to choose tools. For a single team, start with the Team Charter and Events & Cadences. For a team of teams, add Cross-Team Planning, the RAID Log and the Outcome Status Report. For a programme, add Portfolio Kanban and Team Topology Design. The starter kits list the tools for each level in order.
