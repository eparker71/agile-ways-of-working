Impediments Board Guide

How to make impediments visible, give each one an owner, escalate on time, and show the value of removing them.

“What's slowing us down that we've stopped noticing?”

An impediment is anything that limits how well the team works and that the team can't easily fix alone: a slow approval, a missing tool, an unclear decision owner, a flaky environment. Unlike a blocker on a single item, impediments often affect everything. The board makes each one visible with what it costs, gives it an owner, and uses an escalation ladder with time limits so nothing sits unresolved for weeks. The resolved log shows stakeholders the value of removing impediments.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 and put it next to the team board, or keep a digital copy
  • Pink and green stickies, and markers
  • Agree with management who sits on rungs 3 and 4 of the ladder, and that they'll respond in time
  • Bring any impediments from recent retros or the Blocker Clustering board

3Making each part work

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Impediments

  • Each sticky shows its cost
  • Each has an owner and a date
  • Few in Being worked at once

2 · Escalation Ladder

  • Named people at every rung
  • Time limits are short
  • Leaders agreed to respond

3 · Resolved Log

  • What it cost and what fixed it
  • Shared with stakeholders
  • Lessons feed improvement

4Facilitation tips

Show the cost“Waiting 3 days for approvals each sprint” gets action. “Approvals are slow” doesn't.
Owner, not escalatorThe owner drives the impediment to resolution, even after it's escalated.
Time-box each rungWithout a time limit, impediments sit at the team level for months.
Limit work in progressWork on a few impediments at a time and finish them, just like backlog items.
Celebrate removalsShare the resolved log at reviews. It builds support for removing the next one.
Look for patternsIf the same kind keeps appearing, it's a system issue. Raise it at the retro or with leaders.
Common pitfalls: a board full of complaints with no owners; no time limits; escalating without the data on cost; leaders who don't respond; the SM fixing everything alone; never recording what was resolved.

5After the session

Straight away

Put the board where the team and visitors can see it.

Every day

Check it at the daily sync and move anything past its time limit.

Each review

Share the resolved log and any escalations that need stakeholder help.

Quarterly

Look at the log for patterns and bring them to the Team Health Check or leaders.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Impediments Board”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Make what's holding the team back visible, give each impediment an owner, and escalate it on time.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Pink Sticky = Impediment · Green Sticky = Resolution

Sections:
1. Impediments: top full width, about 100% wide and 60% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Each sticky: what · what it costs us · owner · date raised
   Content: New · Being worked · Escalated · Resolved · Raised, not yet picked up · Owner and next step agreed · Needs help from outside the team · Add a green sticky: what fixed it
2. Escalation Ladder: bottom left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Move up if not resolved in time
   Content: 4 · leadership: · Who: (×4) · Decision by · Changes policy or structure · 3 · management: · Escalate after (×3) · days (×3) · Decides or funds the fix · 2 · SCRUM MASTER / COACH · Removes or chases it · 1 · team: · The team tries first
3. Resolved Log: bottom right, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Show the value of removing impediments
   Content: Impediment: · What it cost us: · Resolved by: · Days open: · Lesson:

Footer questions: “What's slowing us down that we've stopped noticing?” “Who could remove this, and do they know it's costing us?”
