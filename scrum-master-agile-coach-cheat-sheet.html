Stances, events, questions and facilitation moves to keep within reach.

1Coaching StancesPick on purpose

Which stance does this moment need?

TeacherThe team lacks knowledge of a practice or of Scrum itself.“Would it help if I explained how this usually works?”
MentorSomeone is growing into a role, and your experience helps.“Here's what I've seen work. What fits you?”
FacilitatorThe group needs a good process for a conversation or decision.“How should we use this time together?”
CoachThe answer lives with them. Your job is to help them find it.“What options do you see?”
ObserverThe team is capable. Step back and let them own it.“What would happen if I said nothing?”

Default to coach and observer. Teach only when asked or when the team is truly stuck.

3Artifacts & Their CommitmentsPart of Scrum · one commitment each

Product Backlog→ Product GoalThe long-term objective the backlog serves. Ordered and refined, and owned by the Product Owner.“Which item moves us closest to the Product Goal?”
Sprint Backlog→ Sprint GoalThe single objective for the sprint. Owned by the Developers, and flexible in scope, not in goal.“If we only finish half, what must be in it?”
Increment→ Definition of DoneThe quality bar every increment meets. Work that isn't Done isn't released or reviewed.“Could we release this today?”
AccountabilitiesProduct Owner maximises value and orders the backlog · Developers build a Done increment and own the plan · Scrum Master makes Scrum effective for the team and the organisation.
Pillars & valuesTransparency → Inspection → Adaptation. Commitment · Focus · Openness · Respect · Courage. When a pillar breaks, ask which value is missing.

4Powerful QuestionsOpen & curious

When the team is stuck
  • What have you already tried?
  • What's the smallest next step?
  • What would you do if you knew it couldn't fail?
  • Who could help with this?
Clarifying goals
  • What does success look like?
  • How will we know we're done?
  • Why does this matter to the customer?
In conflict
  • What do you both agree on?
  • What's the need behind that position?
  • What would a good outcome be for everyone?
After a setback
  • What did we learn?
  • What was within our control?
  • What would we do differently next time?
In a 1:1
  • What's on your mind?
  • And what else?
  • What do you want from me?
  • What was most useful today?

6Anti-Patterns → What to TryExperiment, don't impose · See all 42

You notice…Try…
Daily Scrum is a status report to youStep out of the centre or stay silent. Walk the board right to left. Ask “How are we tracking toward the Sprint Goal?”
The Sprint Goal is a list of ticketsAsk “Why does this sprint matter?” Write one outcome sentence and use it to decide scope trade-offs.
Retro actions never happenCap actions at 1–3, each with an owner, put them in the Sprint Backlog, and review them first at the next retro.
The Product Owner is unavailableAgree regular PO time. Make the cost of waiting visible. Coach the PO on delegating to the team.
Review is a demo with no feedbackInvite real users. Ask questions, not for applause. Update the backlog live in the room.
Work carries over every sprintLimit WIP. Split items smaller. Watch work item age, and swarm on the oldest item.
“Done” means “dev done”Strengthen the Definition of Done to include test and deploy. Make undone work visible.
The team keeps waiting on othersVisualise dependencies. Raise them at a cross-team sync or Scrum of Scrums. Look for ways to cut the dependency.
The SM is the team's secretaryHand events and tools back to the team. Make your coaching work visible, and say no to admin.
Silence in retrosChange the format. Use silent writing and a safety check (1–5). Ask what would make it safe to speak.
Estimates used as commitmentsTalk about forecasts and ranges. Use throughput to forecast instead of debating points.

7Flow MetricsFrom Kanban · learn, never judge

WIPItems started, not finishedHigh WIP means slow flow. Stop starting, start finishing.
ThroughputItems finished per weekUse it to forecast. “How many?” and “When?”
Work item ageTime since an item startedAn early warning. Discuss the oldest item at the Daily Scrum.
Cycle timeStart → done, per itemUse the 85th percentile for a forecast: “85% of items finish in 6 days or less.”

Don't compare teams, set individual targets, or turn metrics into goals. Once a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure (Goodhart's law).

8Team DevelopmentTuckman · your stance shifts as the team grows

FormingLooks likePolite, unclear roles, depends on the leaderYouTeach and set structure. Agree working agreements and a Definition of Done.
StormingLooks likeConflict, pushback, frustrationYouFacilitate conflict and make disagreement safe. Return to agreements.
NormingLooks likeShared ways of working, growing trustYouCoach. Step back, strengthen ownership, raise the bar.
PerformingLooks likeSelf-managing, high trust, deliveringYouObserve. Offer stretch goals, and protect the team from disruption.
Teacher→ Facilitator→ Coach→ Observer

Any change in membership resets the cycle. Expect some forming again, and plan for it.

Signals of a healthy team · check these in 1:1s and retros
Safe to speakPeople raise bad news and ask for help early.
One clear goalEveryone can state the Sprint Goal without looking.
Healthy conflictIdeas get challenged, and people don't get attacked.
SwarmingPeople help finish work rather than starting more.
Owning DoneThe team holds itself to the Definition of Done.

9Decisions & ConflictMake how we decide explicit

Delegation poker · who decides?
1Tell
2Sell
3Consult
4Agree
5Advise
6Inquire
7Delegate

Agree the level for recurring decisions (tools, hiring, architecture) and post it as a delegation board.

Fast agreement

Decider: the proposer states one clear proposal, then everyone votes at once: yes, support it or no.

For any no, ask “What would it take to get you in?” Then adjust and re-vote, or drop it.

Disagree & commit: once a decision is made, everyone supports it, and it gets reviewed on a set date.

10Beyond the TeamCoaching the system

Team-of-teams events

Big-room planning (SAFe: PI Planning) · Scrum of Scrums · Joint demo & retro. Help teams turn shared impediments into improvement items.

Coach the system

Map the value stream, visualise dependencies across teams, and measure flow end-to-end, not just within a team.

Influence leaders

Bring data and frame it in business outcomes. Propose small experiments. Make impediments visible and ask for one decision.
