Project Close-out Guide

How to finish a project well: check what was achieved, hand over cleanly, learn, tie up loose ends and say thank you.

“What would we tell the next team that runs a project like this?”

Projects often just stop: people move on, loose ends stay loose, and the lessons are forgotten. A close-out session makes the ending deliberate. It compares what was delivered with the original goal, lists the benefits still to come (most value usually arrives after the project ends) and who will check them, confirms a clean handover to whoever runs the result, captures what we learned for the next project, gives every loose end an owner, and makes time to thank people. In agile delivery much of this happens continuously, but a project or programme with a defined end still needs a proper finish.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Bring the original goal and success measures, from the Project Kickoff board if you used it
  • Gather the actual results for each measure
  • Invite whoever will own the result after the project: support, operations or the business
  • Ask people to think about what they'd keep and change, beforehand

3What a good close-out covers

Finish the work, then finish the project.

Achievement

  • Goal vs. delivered
  • Target vs. actual

Benefits

  • Most arrive later
  • An owner checks them

Handover

  • Support ready
  • Nothing without an owner

Lessons

  • Keep, change, surprises
  • Shared, not archived

Thanks

  • Name people
  • Celebrate the finish

4Facilitation tips

Learn, don't blameAsk what the system made easy or hard, not who got it wrong.
Bring the next ownersHandover works best with the people who'll run the result in the room.
Benefits need an ownerIf no one is named to check the benefits, no one will.
Make lessons travelShare the top three lessons with teams starting similar work, not just in a document.
Celebrate properlyMarking the end well helps people move on, and makes the next project easier to staff.
Close the small thingsAccounts, licences, budgets and contracts left open cost money and create risk.
Common pitfalls: skipping close-out because people have moved on; a lessons-learned document no one reads; no owner for benefits; handing over to people who weren't involved; blaming individuals; forgetting to say thank you.

5After the session

Within a week

Send the summary, loose ends and owners to everyone involved.

Within a month

Share the top lessons with teams starting similar work.

At each benefit date

The owner checks the measure and reports it to the sponsor.

Next project

Bring these lessons to its kickoff and pre-mortem.

6How it fits the toolkit

The project tools work together: run a Project Kickoff first, then build the Stakeholder Map, keep the RAID Log up to date, and refresh the Release Forecast after every iteration. Inside the project, teams use the lifecycle tools (Team Charter, Prioritisation, Sprint Planning and Review) and the break-down tools.

Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
