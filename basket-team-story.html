The Basket Team Story

Every worked example in the toolkit, in the order it happened: one fictional team at an online grocery service, from launching in February to closing out in July. Read it as a case study of the tools working together. Each step opens the filled-in board, with a link back to the blank one. Short on time? Read the highlights: twelve moments and what they teach.

The cast. The Basket team: Priya (product owner), Sam and Jo (developers), Alex (tester), Mei (designer), Tom (Scrum Master), and Ravi (developer, from March). Around them: the Head of Digital (sponsor), and the Checkout, Payments, Search & Browse, Store Platform and Warehouse teams. The goal: make the weekly shop effortless, so repeat orders rise from 22% to 30%.

February · Getting started

  1. 3 FebProject KickoffThe Head of Digital sponsors “Reorder made easy”: repeat orders from 22% to 30% by the end of June, with only the campaign date fixed.
  2. 5 FebStakeholder MapEleven stakeholders go on the map. Store Ops and Legal could block it, so they get the closest attention.
  3. 6 FebProduct Vision BoardPriya writes the vision: busy households get their weekly shop done in two minutes.
  4. 10 FebPersona & Journey MapTwelve interviews become Sarah, 38, who rebuilds the same basket on her phone every Sunday night.
  5. 12 FebOpportunity Solution TreeOne outcome, three opportunities, six solutions. A fake-door button shows 31% of customers want one-tap reorder.
  6. 18 FebEpic Breakdown CanvasThe epic as a hypothesis, with an MVP: a last-order card and one-tap reorder for 10% of customers.
  7. 20 FebImpact MapFamilies, pickers and customer service each need to behave differently; each gets a deliverable.
  8. 24 FebTeam Topology DesignFour teams mapped by type. Checkout is carrying too much, and every release waits for Payments.
  9. 17–28 FebTeam Launch (Sprint Zero)A two-week launch: charter drafted, backlog ready, and the last-order card running on staging.

March · Forming the team

  1. 3 MarTeam CharterThe Basket team agrees its purpose, values, working agreements and Definitions of Ready and Done.
  2. 3 MarSprint PlanningSprint 14's goal: returning customers see their last order and reorder it in one tap.
  3. 5 MarPersonal User ManualPriya, Sam, Jo and Mei write how to work with them: focus time, feedback, what drains them.
  4. 7 MarUser Story MapThe reorder journey, sliced into an MVP, a second release and later.
  5. 10 MarPrioritisation BoardCost of delay ranks reorder first and subscriptions third; the Now · Next · Later plan follows.
  6. 12 MarExample Mapping“Reorder last basket” has four rules and three open questions, so it's split into two stories.
  7. 14 MarStart · Stop · ContinueSprint 14's retro: stop starting new work while items are blocked; try Example Mapping before planning.
  8. 17 MarTeam OnboardingRavi joins, with Alex as his buddy and a first week that ends with something shipped.
  9. 20 MarSkills MatrixThe skills matrix shows two single points of failure: only Sam knows payments, only Priya knows the data.
  10. 28 MarTeam Health CheckEnd of the quarter: releases and waiting on other teams are the two areas to fix.

April · Delivering, and the first knocks

  1. 1 AprCross-Team PlanningFive teams plan the quarter together around three shared objectives; confidence vote: 4.
  2. 1 AprObjectives & Key ResultsThree objectives for Q2, from effortless weekly shops to a quicker, trustworthy checkout.
  3. 2 AprKanban Board DesignThe team redesigns its board: seven columns, WIP limits and exit policies.
  4. 2 AprAI Working AgreementThe team agrees where AI helps, where it's off limits, and how its output is reviewed.
  5. 9 AprBlameless Incident ReviewSaturday 5 April: checkout fails for four hours. The blameless review finds no alert on certificate errors.
  6. 11 Apr1% Experiment CanvasExample Mapping before planning worked: rework fell from 4 in 9 stories to 1 in 10.
  7. 14 AprProduct Bet CanvasThe staples subscription bet: test it by hand with 50 customers before building anything.
  8. 15 AprEvent StormingAn order from reorder to delivery, mapped with Store Ops and Payments; three hotspots surface.
  9. 22 AprDependency MapFive dependencies across six teams; the Stock API that substitutes need is at risk.
  10. 23 AprPsychological Safety CheckAn anonymous check: problems are raised one to one, not in the room, so the team tries silent writing.
  11. 25 AprDelegation BoardEight decision areas, and where each is heading; spending decisions move to the team in July.
  12. 28 AprRAID LogThe RAID log: repeat billing is the biggest risk, the Stock API the dependency at risk.

May · Under pressure

  1. 1 MayLean CoffeeThe team's own agenda: they'll try counting items instead of story points for two iterations.
  2. 5 MayBlocker ClusteringApril's blockers clustered: sign-offs cost 12 of 24 blocked days, so that's the one to fix.
  3. 6 MayDaily SyncSix days left in Sprint 18. Substitutes are blocked on the Stock API, so two people swarm on it.
  4. 6 MayBacklog RefinementFive items taken through order, understand, split, examples, size and ready.
  5. 6 MayEstimationPlanning poker converges on most items; substitutes come out an 8, so they're split.
  6. 6 MayCross-Team SyncFive teams in 20 minutes: the Stock API is escalated, and release checks are agreed.
  7. 6 MayPortfolio Kanban & Investment MixTwelve initiatives, WIP limits, and an investment mix that's drifted from what was agreed.
  8. 8 MayValue Stream MapFrom story to live takes 16 days, and only 3 of them are work. The longest wait is before building starts.
  9. 8 MayArchitecture Decision RecordADR 007: read stock levels from a replica behind an adapter, and switch to the API when it ships.
  10. 9 MaySprint ReviewSprint 18 met its goal partly; stakeholders want substitutes, and Finance worries about margins.
  11. 9 MayRelease Forecast & Burn-up29 items done, 14 left, with scope still growing: likely finish 13–20 June.
  12. 9 MayOutcome Status ReportAmber: reorder is live and repeat orders are at 25%, but substitutes are an iteration late.
  13. 9 MayValue MetricsValue metrics across the four areas: faster and more valued, but blocked time is rising.
  14. 9 MayRACI & Decision LogWho does what for the subscriptions pilot, and three decisions logged, including ADR 007.
  15. 9 MayBuffering & SwarmingA 20% buffer, sized from five sprints of unplanned work, and swarm triggers with real numbers.
  16. 9 MayAgeing WIP & SLEThree items are past the 9-day SLE; the oldest gets swarmed today.
  17. 12 MayCynefin® Sense-MakingWill customers accept substitutes? It's complex, so three safe-to-fail experiments, not a big design.
  18. 12 MayPairing & MobbingA pair stair for seven people, and the skills from the matrix spread by pairing.
  19. 12 MayMotivation & RecognitionJo ranks ten motivators against a change: learning payments by pairing with Sam.
  20. 12 MayImpediments BoardImpediments from new to resolved, with an escalation ladder and what each one cost.
  21. 13 MayCoaching ConversationTom coaches Alex on raising test risks earlier; Alex commits to one “what if…?” per refinement.
  22. 14 MayDelivery Metrics & Tech DebtFaster and more stable since self-service deploys; five technical debts placed in the quadrant.
  23. 14 MayRoot Cause AnalysisOld promo prices in reordered baskets, traced to copied price rules with no owner.
  24. 15 MayKanban CadencesMeanwhile the Store Platform team, working in flow, reviews its service against SLEs.
  25. 15 MayDelivery Pipeline CanvasSeven pipeline stages: changes wait four hours for review and a day for release.
  26. 16 MayTest StrategyWhat each level of the pyramid tests, seven quality gates, and where to test hardest.
  27. 16 MayContinuous Delivery Health CheckTwelve practices scored: 39 out of 60, up from 33, with flaky tests and monitoring the gaps.
  28. 20 MayPre-mortemIt's 1 October and the subscriptions pilot has failed. Why? Repeat billing tops the list.
  29. 21 MayADKAR Change ReadinessRolling substitutes out to store pickers: each group's barrier point, and what to do about it.

June · Shipping

  1. 2 JuneRelease Readiness & StrategySubstitutes 1.0 switches on in one store, behind a feature flag, with a tested rollback.
  2. 6 JuneNow · Next · Later RoadmapThe roadmap after the pilot: substitutes everywhere now; subscriptions next, if the pilot works.
  3. 9–13 JuneDesign SprintFive days on staples subscriptions: four of five customers set it up, but skipping a week was hard.
  4. 20 JuneAI in DeliveryThe group agrees where AI fits at each stage, how much it does alone, and who's accountable.
  5. 27 JuneCross-Team RetroFive teams look back on the quarter: the top cross-team problem was waiting on the Stock API.

July · Finishing well

  1. 4 JulyProject Close-outRepeat orders reached 29%, nearly the 30% target. The team hands over, thanks people and records what it learned.

All names, numbers and events are made up, but the problems are real ones. For the tools grouped by job instead of by date, see the worked examples gallery; for situations rather than a story, see the Scenario Playbook.
