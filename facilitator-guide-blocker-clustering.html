“Which cluster, if we fixed it, would give us the most days back?”

Teams clear blockers one by one and rarely ask why they keep happening. Blocker clustering, a technique popularised by Klaus Leopold and Troy Magennis, collects every blocker over several weeks, with its cause and how many days it cost. Clustering similar causes shows which problem is really slowing the team down, so you can fix the system rather than the symptom.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 and put it next to the team board, or use a digital copy
  • Plenty of pink stickies, and yellow stickies for cluster names
  • Agree with the team to log every blocker, however small, for at least four weeks
  • Agree a simple rule: a sticky goes on the card the moment it's blocked

2Agenda & run-sheet

WhenStepFacilitator notes
Ongoing1 · LogEvery time work is blocked, add a pink sticky to the card with the cause and date. Add a row to the log.
Ongoing2 · CollectWhen the blocker clears, write the days blocked and move the sticky to the Blocker Stickies area.
Week 4–6 · 0–203 · ClusterIn a session, group similar stickies. Name each cluster on a yellow sticky. Count blockers and total days.
20–35Pick the biggestRank clusters by total days blocked, not by count. Discuss the top one.
35–504 · FixFind the root cause (5 Whys works well) and agree an experiment to fix it, with an owner and review date.
50–605 · TrendAgree to keep logging, and record the totals so you can see if the fix worked.

3Making each part useful

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Blocker Log

  • Every blocker, however small
  • The cause, not who to blame
  • Days counted

2 · Blocker Stickies

  • Kept after clearing
  • Start date and days written on

3 · Clusters

  • Similar causes grouped
  • Each cluster named
  • Count and days totalled

4 · Fix the Biggest

  • Chosen by days, not count
  • A real root cause
  • An owned experiment

5 · Trend

  • Compared period to period
  • Shows whether the fix worked

4Facilitation tips

Log everythingSmall blockers add up. A two-hour wait three times a week is a cluster worth fixing.
Cause, not person“Waiting for test environment” helps. “Waiting for Sam” blames someone and hides the system.
Days beat countsTen one-hour blockers matter less than one blocker that lasted two weeks.
Inside vs. outsideSeparate what the team controls from what needs help from others, and escalate the outside ones.
One fix at a timeTackle the top cluster, measure, then move to the next.
Make it routineKeep the board running. A quick look at each retro keeps the data honest.
Common pitfalls: logging only big blockers; blaming people; clustering too early with too little data; ranking by count; fixing several clusters at once; stopping logging after the first session.

5After the session

Straight away

Start the experiment on the biggest cluster and put its review date in the calendar.

Each retro

Glance at the log: any new patterns? Is the top cluster shrinking?

Next cluster

When the trend shows the fix worked, move on to the next biggest cluster.

Outside causes

Take clusters outside the team's control to the Cross-Team Sync or to leaders, with the data.

Purpose: Log every blocker, cluster them by cause, and fix the cluster that costs the most days.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Pink Sticky = One blocker · Yellow Sticky = Cluster name

Sections:
1. Blocker Log: top left, about 65% wide and 45% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: One row per blocker · cause, not person
   Content: Date: · Item: · Blocked by (what): · Inside / outside team: · Days blocked:
2. Blocker Stickies: top right, about 35% wide and 45% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Keep them after they're cleared
   Content: A pink sticky on the card the moment it's blocked · Write the cause and the start date · When it's cleared, add the days and move it here
3. Clusters: middle full width, about 100% wide and 35% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: Group similar causes · name each cluster
   Content: Cluster: (×5) · Count: (×5) · Days: (×5)
4. Fix the Biggest Cluster: bottom left, about 65% wide and 20% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: By total days blocked, not count
   Content: Biggest cluster: · Experiment to fix it: · Root cause: · Owner & review date:
5. Trend: bottom right, about 35% wide and 20% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Is it working?
   Content: Period: · Blockers: · Days blocked:
