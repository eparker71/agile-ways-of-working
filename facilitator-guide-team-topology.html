Team Topology Design Guide

How to design teams around the flow of value, with clear team types, interaction modes and manageable load.

“Which team is carrying more than it can hold?”

Many delivery problems, such as dependencies, hand-offs and overloaded teams, come from how teams are organised. Team Topologies by Matthew Skelton and Manuel Pais offers four team types (stream-aligned, platform, enabling and complicated subsystem) and three interaction modes (collaboration, X-as-a-service and facilitating). It also asks whether each team's cognitive load is manageable. This board helps leaders and teams map today's structure and find changes worth trying.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A1 or A3 (or 11×17), or load the SVG as a digital background
  • List current teams with their size, what they own, and who they depend on
  • Bring the Dependency Map and any recent Team Health Check results
  • Invite people from several teams, not just leaders
  • Yellow and pink stickies, markers, and three colours of pen or string for interaction modes

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–201 · Team TypesExplain the four types. Write each current team under the type it most resembles today.
20–502 · Team MapPlace each team on the map, grouped by value stream. Draw how they interact today, using the three line styles.
50–753 · Cognitive LoadFor each team, list the domains and systems it owns and rate its load. Mark teams that hold too much.

130–150ClosePick one change to try first, with an owner and a review date.

3Reading the map

Patterns that suggest a change.

Overloaded team

  • Owns too many domains
  • Split it, or move work to a platform

Endless collaboration

  • Two teams always together
  • Merge them, or define a service

No stream-aligned teams

  • Work flows through many teams
  • Re-form around value streams

Skill gaps everywhere

  • Consider an enabling team
  • It should step back when done

Interaction Agreements

  • Each has a purpose
  • Collaborations have an end date

4Facilitation tips

Stream-aligned firstMost teams should own a flow of value end to end. Others exist to help them.
Load is the limitA team can only hold so much. If it owns too much, quality and speed drop.
Interactions are deliberateChoose the mode on purpose, and change it as the relationship matures.
Collaboration is temporaryClose collaboration is expensive. Use it to discover, then move to a service.
Small changes firstTry one boundary change at a time and watch dependencies and flow.
Involve the teamsPeople in the teams know where the friction is. Design with them, not for them.
Common pitfalls: redrawing an org chart without changing how work flows; ignoring cognitive load; permanent collaboration; platform teams that become gatekeepers; designing teams without the people in them; changing everything at once.

5After the session

Straight away

Share the map and the first change with the teams affected.

Over the next quarter

Run the change and watch dependencies, flow and team health.

At the review date

Check what changed and decide the next step.

Twice a year

Re-map teams as products and strategy change.
