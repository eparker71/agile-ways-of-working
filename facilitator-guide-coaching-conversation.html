Coaching Conversation Guide

How to run a one-to-one coaching conversation with the GROW model: goal, reality, options and will.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Facilitate · GROW Model (Sir John Whitmore)

“What do you want to be different by the end of this conversation?”

Coaching helps people find their own answers rather than being told what to do. The GROW model, popularised by Sir John Whitmore in Coaching for Performance, gives a one-to-one conversation a simple shape: agree the Goal, explore the current Reality, generate Options, and finish with the coachee's Will, their committed next steps. The canvas starts with a short contract and ends with the coach's own reflection. Scrum Masters, agile coaches, managers and mentors can all use it.

1Before the session

  • Print the canvas at A3, or use it on screen; the coachee can keep it afterwards
  • Book a private space and enough uninterrupted time
  • Agree in advance that this is coaching, not a performance review
  • Clear your head: plan to ask more than you tell

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–51 · ContractAsk what they want from the conversation. Agree confidentiality and how long you have.
5–152 · GoalHelp them describe what they want and what success looks like. Make it specific.
15–303 · RealityExplore what's happening now, what they've tried, and what's in their control. Listen more than talk.
30–454 · OptionsGenerate many options. Keep asking “what else?” Hold back your own ideas, or offer them last.
45–555 · WillAsk which option they'll take, the first step, what might stop them, and how committed they are.
55–606 · Commitments · 7 · ReflectionThey write the commitments in their own words. Afterwards, reflect on your coaching.

3Using each stage well

What to aim for in each part of the conversation.

Goal

  • Their goal, not yours
  • Specific and meaningful

Reality

  • Facts and feelings
  • What they control

Options

  • Quantity before quality
  • Their ideas first

Will

  • A first step and a date
  • Commitment 1–10

Coach's Reflection

  • Did I ask or tell?
  • What will I follow up?

4Facilitation tips

Ask, then waitSilence gives people room to think. Count to five before you speak.
Open questionsStart with what, how and who. “Why” can sound like judgement; use it gently.
Their wordsWrite commitments in the coachee's words, not your summary.
Loop backIf options stall, return to Reality or Goal. GROW isn't strictly one-way.
Low commitment is usefulIf they say 5 out of 10, ask what would make it an 8.
Follow upStart the next conversation by asking how the commitments went.
Common pitfalls: giving advice too early; turning it into a status update; setting the goal for them; leading questions; running out of time before Will; no follow-up.

5After the session

Straight away

Give the coachee the canvas or a photo of it.

Before the next one

Read your reflection notes and the commitments.

Next conversation

Start with the commitments: what happened, what did you learn?

For yourself

Ask for feedback on your coaching every few sessions.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Coaching Conversation”, a workshop board.

Purpose: A simple structure for one-to-one coaching: agree the goal, explore reality and options, and commit to action.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Coachee, Date

Sections:
1. Contract: top full width, about 100% wide and 15% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Agree this first
   Content: What do you want from this conversation? · Confidentiality agreed · Time:
2. Goal: middle left, about 25% wide and 60% tall.
   Subtitle: What do you want?
   Content: Questions to try: · What would you like to achieve? · What would success look like? · Why does this matter to you now? · How will you know you've got there? · Notes:
3. Reality: middle centre, about 25% wide and 60% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Where are you now?
   Content: Questions to try: · What's happening at the moment? · What have you tried so far? · What's getting in the way? · What's within your control? · Notes:
4. Options: middle centre, about 25% wide and 60% tall.
   Subtitle: What could you do?
   Content: Questions to try: · What could you do? · What else? And what else? · What would you advise a friend? · What if there were no limits? · Notes:
5. Will: middle right, about 25% wide and 60% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: What will you do?
   Content: Questions to try: · Which option will you take? · What's the first step, and when? · What might stop you? · How committed are you, 1–10? · Notes:
6. Commitments: bottom left, about 65% wide and 20% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: In the coachee's words
   Content: Action: · By when: · Support needed:
7. Coach's Reflection: bottom right, about 35% wide and 20% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Afterwards
   Content: What I noticed: · Did I ask more than tell? · Follow up on:

Footer questions: “What do you want to be different by the end of this conversation?” “What's the smallest step you could take this week?”
