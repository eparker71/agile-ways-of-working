Anti-Pattern Spotter Guide

How to help a team spot the anti-patterns holding it back, choose the ones that matter, and act on them.

“If we fixed only one, which would make the biggest difference?”

Every team slips into habits that quietly slow it down: daily syncs that turn into status reports, stories that are really tasks, too much work in progress. Naming them is the first step to fixing them. The board lists 28 common anti-patterns in seven areas, from events and the backlog to leadership and metrics. People tick the ones they recognise, vote on those that hurt most, then choose a tool to tackle the top three. Each symptom has a fuller entry, with why it hurts and what to try, in the Anti-Pattern Catalogue.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Open the Anti-Pattern Catalogue so you can read out fuller descriptions
  • Dot stickers (three per person) and markers
  • Agree that this is about the system, not about blaming anyone
  • For leaders and teams together, consider anonymous ticking first

2Agenda & run-sheet

MinutesStepFacilitator notes
0–5FrameExplain that every team has some of these. The aim is to pick a few to improve, not to score the team.
5–15SpotEveryone ticks the symptoms they recognise, in silence. Add any missing ones in the Our Own panel.
15–20VoteThree dots each on the anti-patterns that hurt most.
20–40UnderstandFor the top three, read the catalogue entry and discuss why it's happening here.
40–55ActPick a tool to try for each, with an owner and a date to check.
55–60CloseBook a re-run in six to eight weeks.

4Facilitation tips

Normalise itEvery team has anti-patterns. Saying so up front makes honest ticking easier.
Symptoms, not peopleThe board names patterns in the system, never individuals.
Three is plentyWorking on three anti-patterns well beats a long list of good intentions.
Ask why hereThe same anti-pattern has different causes in different teams. Discuss yours.
Use the catalogueEach entry says why the pattern hurts and what to try, which keeps discussion constructive.
Track progressRe-running the board shows which ticks have disappeared, a visible sign of improvement.
Common pitfalls: using it to judge a team or a person; trying to fix everything at once; voting on the easiest instead of the most harmful; no owners; never re-running it.

5After the session

Straight away

Start the three actions, using the tools you chose.

Each retro

Check progress on the top three.

In 6–8 weeks

Re-run the spotter and compare.

Share patterns

Coaches can collect anonymised results across teams to spot system-wide issues.

6How it fits the toolkit

The spotter is a way in to the rest of the toolkit: every symptom points to a tool. Use it with the Anti-Pattern Catalogue, the Team Health Check and the Psychological Safety Check to understand how a team is doing.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Anti-Pattern Spotter”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Tick the anti-patterns you recognise, vote on the ones that hurt most, and pick a tool to tackle each.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Dot = Vote (3 per person) · Tick = We recognise this

Sections:
1. Events: top left, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: Daily sync is a status report · Same issues at every retro · Review is a one-way demo · Planning has no goal
2. Backlog: top centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: Stories are technical tasks · Epics have no outcome · Backlog graveyard · Everything is top priority
3. Flow: top centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: Too much work in progress · Blocked work goes unnoticed · “Done” means dev done · Unplanned work wrecks plans
4. Teams & roles: top right, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour purple.
   Content: Product owner unavailable · SM is the team's secretary · Unclear decision rights · Quiet voices go unheard
5. Multi-team: middle left, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: Teams wait on each other · Cross-team sync is status · Top-down big-room planning · One team owns too much
6. Leadership: middle centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Content: Too many initiatives · Change announced, not led · Annual funding only · Agile as a mandate
7. Metrics & reporting: middle centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: Velocity as a target · Single-date promises · Green until suddenly red · Individuals measured by tickets
8. Our Own: middle right, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour slate grey.
9. Our Top Three: bottom left, about 65% wide and 25% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Why here? what will we try?
   Content: Anti-pattern: · Why it's happening here: · Tool to try: · Owner: · Check on:
10. Next Time: bottom right, about 35% wide and 25% tall; header colour dark green.
   Content: Re-run the spotter in 6–8 weeks · Compare which ticks have gone · Look up each symptom in the catalogue · Next session:
