SLOs & Error Budgets Guide

How to agree how reliable a service needs to be, measure it as users feel it, and use the error budget to balance new work with reliability.

“Which users would notice first if this service got worse?”

A service level objective (SLO) is a target for how reliable a service should be, measured the way users feel it: for example, 99.5% of checkouts succeed over 30 days. The gap between the target and 100% is the error budget: the amount of unreliability you can accept. While there's budget left, the team ships freely; when it runs out, reliability work comes first. It turns the old argument between "ship faster" and "keep it stable" into a shared number that product and engineering agree on in advance. The ideas come from Google's site reliability engineering, described in Site Reliability Engineering (Beyer, Jones, Petoff and Murphy) and The Site Reliability Workbook, and are set out here in our own words. The board works left to right: users and their journeys, the indicators (SLIs) that measure them, the objectives, the budget, the policy for when it runs low, and the review and backlog that keep it real.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or share it on screen
  • Pick one service that matters to users; don't start with everything
  • Bring what you already measure: request success, response times, recent incidents and complaints
  • Invite the product owner or manager: the policy only works if product agrees to it

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–15Users & journeysList the few journeys users care about most, and what good feels like to them.
15–35IndicatorsFor each journey, choose one or two SLIs: good events divided by valid events.
35–50ObjectivesSet a target and a window. Start just below what you achieve today, not at 100%.
50–60Error budgetWork out the budget, using the downtime table, and how much has been used recently.
60–80PolicyAgree what happens when the budget is healthy, running low and spent. Product must agree.
80–90Review & backlogSet the review rhythm and an alert, and list reliability work to start now.

3Key ideas

Four terms that do most of the work.

Indicator (SLI)

  • What you measure
  • Good events / valid events

Objective (SLO)

  • The target, over a window
  • e.g. 99.5% over 30 days

Error budget

  • 100% minus the SLO
  • Room to take risks

Policy

  • What changes when it runs low
  • Agreed before it's needed

4Facilitation tips

Users, not serversMeasure what users experience, such as failed checkouts, not CPU or memory.
100% is the wrong targetUsers can't tell 99.99% from 100%, and chasing it stops all change.
Few and simpleTwo or three SLOs per service is plenty. More gets ignored.
Start where you areSet the first target near today's performance, then tighten it if users need more.
Spend the budgetUnused budget means you could release faster or experiment more.
Not a contractAn SLO is an internal target. A service level agreement (SLA) with customers should be looser.
Common pitfalls: setting every SLO at 99.99%; measuring infrastructure instead of user experience; a policy product never agreed to; blaming people when the budget is spent; too many SLOs for anyone to watch; never reviewing the targets.

5After the session

Straight away

Build the dashboard and the burn-rate alert, and share the policy with stakeholders.

Each month

Review the budget together. Is the target still right for users?

When it's spent

Follow the policy, run a Blameless Incident Review, and add the fixes to the reliability backlog.

Each quarter

Revisit the journeys and targets as the product changes.
