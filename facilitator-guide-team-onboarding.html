“What do you wish someone had told you in your first week?”

A new person's first weeks shape how quickly they contribute and whether they stay. Good onboarding is planned, not improvised: access ready before day one, a first-week plan that builds from meeting people to finishing something small, a list of people to meet, a walk through how we work here, a buddy to ask the questions people feel awkward asking, and 30, 60 and 90-day check-ins that run both ways. The new person is also a fresh pair of eyes: ask what they notice that the team has stopped seeing.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Start a week or two before the person joins, so access and equipment are ready
  • Choose a buddy: someone friendly and patient, not necessarily the most senior
  • Pick a small, real first task they can finish in the first week
  • Gather links to the team charter, Definition of Done, board and documentation

4Facilitation tips

Ready on day oneWaiting days for a laptop or access sends a message; sort it before they start.
Pair earlyPairing on a real item teaches more about the code and the team than any document.
Small first winFinishing something in week one builds confidence and shows the whole workflow.
The buddy isn't the managerA buddy is for the questions people won't ask their manager.
Ask what they noticeNew people spot friction the team has stopped seeing. Capture it for the retro.
Update the charterWhen someone joins, revisit the team charter together: the team has changed.
Common pitfalls: access that takes weeks; a pile of documents instead of a plan; no buddy, or a buddy with no time; a first task that's too big or not real; no check-ins after week one; treating onboarding as only HR's job.

6How it fits the toolkit
