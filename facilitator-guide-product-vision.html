Product Vision Board Guide

How to agree the change a product will bring, who it's for, and how you'll know it's working.

“What will be different for our users when this succeeds?”

Without a shared vision, every backlog decision becomes an argument about opinions. The board, based on Roman Pichler's Product Vision Board, separates the vision (the change you want to bring about) from the strategy (target group, needs, product and business goals) underneath it. Adding measures of success and riskiest assumptions turns it into something you can test, not just a statement.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Bring evidence: user research, support data, analytics and market information
  • Invite people who know the users, the business and the technology
  • Ask the product owner to draft a vision sentence in advance, as a starting point
  • Yellow and pink stickies, and markers

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10FrameExplain the board and the decision it supports. Share the evidence you brought.
10–251 · VisionWrite the positive change the product will bring. Not features. Keep it short enough to remember.
25–452 · Target GroupWho are the users and customers? Pick the most important group, and say who it's not for.
45–603 · NeedsWhat problem does each group have, and how do they solve it today? Prioritise the main need.
60–754 · ProductList the 3–5 things that make the product stand out and meet the main need.
75–905 · Business GoalsHow does the product help the organisation? Pick the goal that matters most.
90–1056 · Measures of SuccessFor the top outcomes, agree a measure, a baseline and a target.
105–1207 · Riskiest Assumptions · CloseWhat must be true for this to work? Rank them and agree how to test the riskiest first.

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Vision

  • Describes a change, not a product
  • Would still hold if the product changed
  • Memorable

2 · Target Group

  • Specific, not “everyone”
  • Primary group is clear
  • Says who it's not for

3 · Needs

  • Problems, not features
  • Based on evidence where possible

4 · Product

  • 3–5 key features at most
  • Each links to a need

5 · Business Goals

  • Ranked
  • Measurable over time

6 · Measures of Success

  • Baseline and target for each
  • Outcomes, not output

7 · Riskiest Assumptions

  • Ranked by risk
  • Each has a cheap way to test it

4Facilitation tips

Vision before featuresIf people describe features in the vision box, ask “and what will that change for users?”
One primary userTrying to serve everyone equally leads to a product that serves no one well.
Evidence over opinionMark stickies that are backed by data. Treat the rest as assumptions to test.
Say who it's not forExcluding a group is a strategic choice, and makes later trade-offs easier.
Measures make it realA vision with no measure of success can't be proven wrong, so it can't guide decisions.
Revisit itBring the board to quarterly planning and update it when evidence changes the picture.
Basics, performance, delightersThe Kano model is a useful lens for the Product box: which features users simply expect, which add satisfaction the more you have, and which would delight them?
Common pitfalls: a vision that is a feature list; target group “everyone”; needs invented in the room with no evidence; skipping measures; treating assumptions as facts; never testing the riskiest assumption.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the board and share it with stakeholders who weren't there.

Next week

Turn the riskiest assumption into a test with the 1% Experiment Canvas or an MVP.

Then

Use the Prioritisation Board to decide which needs and features come first.

Every quarter

Check the measures of success. Update the board if the evidence has changed.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Product Vision Board”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Describe the change the product will bring, who it's for, what they need, and how we'll know it's working.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Product, Owner

Sections:
1. Vision: top full width, about 100% wide and 20% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: The positive change the product will bring · not what it is
   Content: Our vision is…
2. Target Group: middle left, about 25% wide and 50% tall.
   Subtitle: Who?
   Content: Which users and customers? · Who is the most important group? · Who is it not for?
3. Needs: middle centre, about 25% wide and 50% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Subtitle: Why?
   Content: What problem does it solve? · What benefit does it bring? · How do they solve it today?
4. Product: middle centre, about 25% wide and 50% tall.
   Subtitle: What?
   Content: The 3–5 things that make it stand out · Is it feasible? · How is it different?
5. Business Goals: middle right, about 25% wide and 50% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Why us?
   Content: How does it help the organisation? · Revenue, cost, reach, reputation? · Which goal matters most?
6. Measures of Success: bottom left, about 50% wide and 25% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: How we'll know it's working
   Content: Outcome: · Measure: · Baseline: · Target:
7. Riskiest Assumptions: bottom right, about 50% wide and 25% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Test these first
   Content: What must be true for this vision to work? · Test the riskiest with the 1% Experiment Canvas or an MVP

Footer questions: “What will be different for our users when this succeeds?” “Who is this not for?”
