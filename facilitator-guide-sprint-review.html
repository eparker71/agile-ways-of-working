Sprint Review Guide

How to inspect what was built with stakeholders, gather real feedback, and adapt the backlog together.

“Based on what we've seen, what should we do next?”

A review is a working session, not a demo. The team shows working product that meets the Definition of Done, stakeholders say what they think, and everyone looks at what has changed around the product. The output is a changed backlog and a clear view of what's next. If nothing in the backlog changes, the review didn't do its job. Scrum calls this event the Sprint Review.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Invite the stakeholders whose feedback matters most, including users if you can
  • Prepare working product to show, in an environment people can try themselves
  • Gather the data for key measures: before, now and target
  • Green, pink, blue and yellow stickies, and markers
  • Ask the PO to prepare a short update on the backlog, release and forecast

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–5WelcomeExplain the purpose: to inspect the product and decide what's next together, not to approve work.
5–101 · Sprint Goal & ResultThe PO states the goal and whether it was achieved. Be honest about what wasn't done.
10–152 · Key MeasuresShare the numbers that matter. Did the last release move them?
15–403 · What We BuiltTeam members show working product. Let stakeholders try it. Only Done work is shown.
—4 · Stakeholder FeedbackThroughout the demo, stakeholders add green, pink and blue stickies. Ask questions; don't defend.
40–505 · What's ChangedWhat's new in the market, with users, budget or timelines? How does it affect the plan?
50–656 · Backlog ChangesTurn feedback and changes into backlog additions, changes and removals. The PO decides.
65–757 · What's Next · CloseShare the likely next goal and any forecast update. Agree decisions needed and who follows up.

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Sprint Goal & Result

  • An honest answer: yes, partly or no
  • What we learned is captured

2 · Key Measures

  • Outcomes, not just velocity
  • Before, now and target shown

3 · What We Built

  • Only Done work
  • Working product, not slides
  • Stakeholders try it

4 · Stakeholder Feedback

  • Concerns are welcomed
  • Ideas are captured, not promised

5 · What's Changed

  • Market, users, budget, timeline
  • Impact on priorities discussed

6 · Backlog Changes

  • Something changed
  • Removals are allowed
  • The PO decides

7 · What's Next

  • Next goal is shared
  • Decisions have an owner

4Facilitation tips

Not a demoIf stakeholders only watch, you'll get politeness, not feedback. Hand them the product.
Show Done onlyShowing unfinished work creates false expectations. Mention it, but don't demo it.
Invite the right peopleA review with no stakeholders is just a team meeting. Chase the people whose opinion matters.
Listen, don't defendWrite every piece of feedback down, especially the uncomfortable ones.
Change the backlogEnd by showing what changed. It proves the feedback was heard.
Keep it short and regularSame time each sprint, tight timebox. Stakeholders come back when it's useful.
Common pitfalls: a one-way demo with no feedback; showing work that isn't Done; no stakeholders present; defending every criticism; promising every idea; no backlog changes at the end; a status report instead of working product.

5After the session

Straight away

Update the backlog with the agreed changes and share the board with anyone who missed it.

Follow up

Owners chase the decisions needed from stakeholders before Sprint Planning.

Retro

Bring anything about how the team worked to the retrospective, which usually comes next.

Next planning

Use the feedback and backlog changes as inputs for the next sprint goal.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.
