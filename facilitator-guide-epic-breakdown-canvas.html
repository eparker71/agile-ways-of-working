Epic Breakdown Canvas Guide

How to turn a big idea into a testable MVP, a set of valuable features, and the first stories.

“What's the smallest thing we could build to learn whether this epic is worth it?”

Big initiatives often arrive as a solution with a deadline. The canvas slows the group down just enough to agree why the epic exists, what outcome would prove it worked, and what the smallest viable version is. It then splits the epic into a few features that each deliver value, and identifies the first stories a team can start. Use it before quarterly planning, or whenever an epic is too vague to plan.

1Before the session

  • Print the canvas at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Ask the epic owner to draft the hypothesis statement in advance
  • Bring any evidence: customer research, data, competitor information
  • Invite people who know the users, the technology and the constraints
  • Yellow stickies and markers; a copy of the Story Splitting poster
  • Agree who decides on MVP scope (usually the epic owner or product manager)

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10Frame the epicThe epic owner explains the idea and why now. Clarify questions only; don't design solutions yet.
10–301 · Epic HypothesisComplete the For / who / the / is a / that / unlike / our solution statement together. If the group can't agree the “who”, stop and resolve that first.
30–452 · OutcomesAgree 1–3 business outcomes and the leading indicators that would show early progress. Prefer numbers.
45–603 · MVPAsk: what's the smallest thing that would test the hypothesis? Write the persevere and pivot-or-stop conditions.
60–854 · FeaturesSplit the epic into 3–8 features, each with a name, benefit and rough size. Tick the ones needed for the MVP.
85–1006 · Out of Scope · 7 · RisksWrite down what you are explicitly not doing, plus any non-functional needs. Add dependencies and risks as stickies.
100–1155 · First StoriesFor the MVP features, write the first few stories as vertical slices. Use the Story Splitting poster if any look too big.
115–120CloseConfirm the next step: story mapping, release planning, or a go / no-go decision.

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the canvas.

1 · Epic Hypothesis

  • Is the customer specific, not “everyone”?
  • Does “that…” describe value, not features?
  • Could it be proven wrong?

2 · Outcomes & Measures

  • Is there a baseline and a target?
  • Would a leading indicator move within weeks?

3 · MVP

  • Is it the smallest test, not the first release of everything?
  • Are the persevere / pivot conditions measurable?

4 · Features

  • Does each feature deliver value by itself?
  • Are there more than 8? Group or cut some
  • Is the MVP set genuinely minimal?

5 · First Stories

  • Is each story a vertical slice?
  • Could the team start one next sprint?

6 · Out of Scope & NFRs

  • Have stakeholders seen the out-of-scope list?
  • Are security, performance and compliance needs captured?

7 · Dependencies & Risks

  • Who outside the room do we depend on?
  • What's the riskiest assumption, and how will we test it early?

4Facilitation tips

Problem before solutionGroups jump to features. Keep pulling the conversation back to the customer and the outcome until the hypothesis is solid.
MVP means learningAn MVP is the smallest experiment that tests the hypothesis, not a cut-down version of the full plan.
Features must stand aloneIf a feature only delivers value once three others are done, it's a component. Re-slice it.
Write the out-of-scope listSaying what you won't do prevents scope creep later, and surfaces disagreement early.
Size roughlyT-shirt sizes are enough at this stage. Precise estimates come later, from the teams.
Revisit after the MVPWhen the MVP results come in, update the canvas: persevere, pivot or stop.
Common pitfalls: a hypothesis that is really a feature list; an MVP that is most of the epic; features split by technical layer; no measurable outcome; skipping out-of-scope so everything stays in; designing the solution in detail.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the canvas. Create the epic and its features in your backlog tool, linked to the canvas.

Next step

Run a Story Map for the MVP features to lay out the user journey and the first release slice.

Before quarterly planning

Refine the first stories with Example Mapping so teams can plan them.

After the MVP

Review the outcomes and leading indicators against the persevere / pivot conditions.

6How it fits the toolkit

The break-down tools work from big to small: the Epic Breakdown Canvas turns an idea into an MVP and features, the Story Map lays out the user journey and release slices, the Story Splitting poster makes stories small enough for a sprint, and Example Mapping checks that each story is understood and ready. The Backlog Item Anatomy poster shows what a good epic, feature and story look like.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Epic Breakdown Canvas”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Turn a big idea into an MVP, a handful of valuable features, and the first stories a team can start.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Epic, Owner

Sections:
1. Epic Hypothesis: top left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Why are we doing this?
   Content: (the customers or users) · For… · (have this need or problem) · who… · (epic name) · the… · (what it is: product, service, capability) · is a… · (provides this value) · that… · (the current solution or competitor) · unlike… · (does this better) · our solution…
2. Outcomes & Measures: top centre, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: Business outcomes: how we'll know it worked · Leading indicators: early signals
3. Minimum Viable Product: top right, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Test it small
   Content: Smallest thing that tests the hypothesis: · Persevere if… · Pivot or stop if…
4. Features: middle full width, about 100% wide and 30% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: Split the epic into 3–8 features that each deliver value
   Content: Feature 1: · Feature 2: · Feature 3: · Feature 4: · Feature 5: · Name: (×5) · Benefit: (×5) · Size: (×5) · S (×5) · M (×5) · L (×5) · XL (×5) · Part of the MVP (×5)
5. First Stories: bottom left, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: From the MVP features
   Content: Slice vertically: each story is usable end-to-end · Too big for an iteration? Use the Story Splitting poster
6. Out of Scope & NFRs: bottom centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour amber.
   Content: We are explicitly not doing: · Non-functional needs (security, scale…):
7. Dependencies & Risks: bottom right, about 25% wide and 30% tall; header colour red.
   Content: Other teams, systems or suppliers we need · What could stop or delay this?

Footer questions: “What's the smallest thing we could build to learn whether this epic is worth it?” “Which feature, if it were the only one, would still deliver value?”
