Project Kickoff Guide

How to start an agile project with a shared why, clear scope, agreed trade-offs and named people.

“If we had to cut half the scope, what would we keep?”

Projects that start with a plan but no shared understanding pay for it later. The kickoff board gets the sponsor, product owner and team to agree why the project exists, what's in and out of scope, which of scope, time, budget, quality and team size can flex, the constraints, the people, the biggest worries, and the first milestones. It draws on the inception deck idea from Jonathan Rasmusson's The Agile Samurai.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Ask the sponsor to come ready to explain why the project matters now
  • Gather any business case, contract, deadlines or budget constraints
  • Invite the people who will do the work, not just those who approved it
  • Yellow and pink stickies, markers and dot stickers

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10WelcomePurpose of the session, and introductions. Explain that disagreement now is cheaper than later.
10–301 · Why Are We Here?The sponsor explains the problem. The group writes the two sentences together until everyone agrees.
30–552 · ScopeList what's in and, just as importantly, what's out. Anything disputed goes to the sponsor to decide.
55–803 · Trade-off SlidersFor each row, agree how fixed it is. Only one can be fully fixed. Discuss what gives when plans change.
80–954 · Constraints · 5 · PeopleList the givens. Name the sponsor, product owner, delivery lead, teams, experts and decision forum.
95–1156 · What Keeps Us Up at NightSilent writing of risks and worries, then cluster. Move the biggest to the RAID Log.
115–1357 · First MilestonesAgree 3–6 milestones described as outcomes, with target dates as ranges where possible.
135–1508 · Next Steps · CloseTick which tools come next and who owns them. Agree when to review this board.

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Why Are We Here?

  • A problem, not a solution
  • Success is observable

2 · Scope

  • Out of scope is as long as in scope
  • Disputes have a decision owner

3 · Trade-off Sliders

  • Only one fully fixed
  • Everyone can say what flexes first

4 · Constraints & Givens

  • Real constraints, not preferences
  • Each has a source

5 · People & Roles

  • One accountable sponsor
  • One product owner
  • A named decision forum

6 · What Keeps Us Up at Night

  • Worries are spoken, not hidden
  • Big ones go to the RAID Log

7 · First Milestones

  • Outcomes, not tasks
  • Early ones test the riskiest parts

8 · Next Steps

  • Each next step has an owner
  • A review date is set

4Facilitation tips

Start with whyIf the group can't agree the problem, stop there. Everything else depends on it.
Make out-of-scope visibleSaying what you won't do surfaces hidden expectations before they become complaints.
Sliders force honestyIf someone wants every slider at Fixed, ask what should happen when the plan meets reality.
Invite the doersThe team spots risks and constraints the sponsors can't see.
Milestones as outcomes“Customers can pay online” beats “payment module complete”.
Keep it aliveBring the board to reviews. Update it when scope, people or trade-offs change.
Common pitfalls: a sponsor who presents and leaves; no out-of-scope list; every slider fixed; milestones that are tasks; ignoring the worries; treating the board as sign-off rather than a starting point.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the board and share it with everyone involved, including those who missed it.

This week

Run the Team Charter, Product Vision Board and Stakeholder Map sessions.

First iteration

Set up the RAID Log and the Release Forecast, and plan the first slice.

Each month

Review the board: has the why, scope or any trade-off changed?

6How it fits the toolkit

The project tools work together: run a Project Kickoff first, then build the Stakeholder Map, keep the RAID Log up to date, and refresh the Release Forecast after every iteration. Inside the project, teams use the lifecycle tools (Team Charter, Prioritisation, Sprint Planning and Review) and the break-down tools.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Project Kickoff”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Agree why the project exists, what's in and out, what can flex, and who's involved, before the first iteration.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Project, Sponsor

Sections:
1. Why Are We Here?: top left, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: The problem and the outcome
   Content: We're doing this because… · Success looks like…
2. Scope: top right, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Saying what's out matters as much as what's in
   Content: In scope · Out of scope
3. Trade-off Sliders: middle left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: Only one can be fully fixed
   Content: Mark one dot per row. If everything is fixed, nothing can give when plans change. · Scope · Flex: (×5) · Fixed: (×5) · Time · Budget · Quality · Team size
4. Constraints & Givens: middle centre, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Content: Fixed dates, regulations, budgets · Technology and platform choices
5. People & Roles: middle right, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour sky blue.
   Content: Sponsor: · Product owner: · Delivery lead: · Team(s): · Key experts: · Decision forum:
6. What Keeps Us Up at Night: bottom left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Top risks
   Content: What could stop or derail this? · Move the big ones to the RAID Log
7. First Milestones: bottom centre, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Outcomes, not tasks
   Content: Milestone: · Outcome: · Target:
8. Next Steps: bottom right, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: Team Charter for each team · Product Vision Board · Stakeholder Map · Prioritised backlog and first slice · Release Forecast · RAID Log set up · Next review of this board:

Footer questions: “What problem are we solving, and for whom?” “If we had to cut half the scope, what would we keep?”
