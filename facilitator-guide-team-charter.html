Team Charter Guide

How to help a new or changing team agree its purpose, ways of working, and what Ready and Done mean.

“What would make this the best team any of us has worked on?”

Teams that skip chartering discover their disagreements later, under delivery pressure. A charter makes the unwritten rules explicit: why the team exists, who is in it, what it values, how it works and decides, and the quality bar for starting and finishing work. The team writes it together, so they own it, and revisits it whenever the team or its work changes.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Ask the sponsor or product owner for a one-line draft of the team's purpose
  • Gather any existing agreements, Definitions of Done or team norms
  • Book a space where everyone can contribute, and make sure the whole team can attend
  • Yellow stickies, markers and dot stickers for voting
  • Plan an icebreaker that helps people share how they like to work

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–15Check-inEach person shares one thing that made a past team great, and one thing that made one hard.
15–401 · PurposeDraft the three sentences together. Start with the sponsor's draft and challenge it until everyone could explain it.
40–552 · Members & RolesEach person adds their name, role, and what they bring: skills, experience, interests.
55–753 · ValuesSilent writing, cluster, then dot-vote down to 3–5 values. Give each value a concrete example.
75–1054 · Working AgreementsBrainstorm agreements as “We…” statements. Discuss each; keep only those everyone can commit to.
105–1205 · How We Decide · 6 · Ways of WorkingPick a default decision method and note who decides what. Fill in cadence, tools and channels.
120–1457 · Ready · 8 · DoneAgree the checklist for starting an item and for calling it done. Keep each to what the team will really check.
145–1609 · Conflict & Review · CloseAgree how to handle disagreement and when to review. Everyone signs; photograph the board.

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the charter.

1 · Purpose

  • Could a stakeholder repeat it?
  • Does it name who the team serves?
  • Is success observable?

2 · Members & Roles

  • Is everyone listed, part-time members too?
  • Are skill gaps visible?

3 · Values

  • 3–5, not 12
  • Each has an example of it in action

4 · Working Agreements

  • Written as “We…”
  • Specific enough to notice if broken
  • Everyone can commit

5 · How We Decide

  • A default method is chosen
  • Who decides product, technical and process questions?

6 · Ways of Working

  • Cadence and times are filled in
  • One place for the board and the backlog

7 · Definition of Ready

  • Short, and a guide not a gate
  • Includes “small enough for an iteration”

8 · Definition of Done

  • Includes testing and review
  • Done means releasable

9 · Conflict & Review

  • A first step when we disagree
  • A review trigger is ticked

4Facilitation tips

The team writes itA charter handed down by a manager is a policy. Facilitate; don't author.
Specific beats nice“We respect each other” can't be broken. “We reply to review requests within a day” can.
Decide how you decideMany team conflicts are really about decision rights. Settle them before they matter.
Ready is a guideA strict Definition of Ready can become a hand-off gate. Use it to prompt conversation.
Done grows over timeStart with what the team can achieve now, and raise the bar at retrospectives.
Keep it visiblePin it up or link it from the team board, and point to it when an agreement is broken.
Common pitfalls: a purpose written by someone outside the team; generic values with no examples; dozens of agreements nobody remembers; a Definition of Done the team can't meet yet; signing it once and never looking at it again.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the board and put a copy where the team works, physically or online.

First retro

Ask: which agreements are we keeping? Which aren't working? Update the charter.

When the team changes

Walk new members through it and invite them to challenge it.

Every quarter

Review the purpose and the Definition of Done. Raise the bar if the team is ready.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Team Charter”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Agree why the team exists, how it works together, and what Ready and Done mean, before the work starts.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Team, Date

Sections:
1. Purpose: top left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Why does this team exist?
   Content: We exist to… · for… · We succeed when…
2. Members & Roles: top centre, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour blue.
   Content: Name: · Role: · What I bring:
3. Our Values: top right, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Subtitle: What matters to us
   Content: 3–5 values, each with an example of it in action
4. Working Agreements: middle left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: We agree to…
   Content: Communication, core hours, meetings, feedback, how we handle interruptions · One agreement per sticky, written as “We…”
5. How We Decide: middle centre, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour sky blue.
   Content: Consent: no one objects · Consensus: everyone agrees · Majority vote · Consult, then one person decides · Delegated to an owner · Default method, and who decides what:
6. Ways of Working: middle right, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Cadence & tools
   Content: Iteration length: · Planning: · Daily sync: · Review: · Retro: · Board / tools: · Channels:
7. Definition of Ready: bottom left, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Before we start an item
   Content: e.g. value is clear, it's small enough, examples agreed
8. Definition of Done: bottom centre, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Before we call it done
   Content: e.g. tested, reviewed, documented, releasable
9. Conflict & Review: bottom right, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour red.
   Content: When we disagree, we… · We review this charter: · Every quarter · When someone joins · When it stops working · Signed by the team:

Footer questions: “What would make this the best team any of us has worked on?” “Which of these agreements will we find hardest to keep?”
