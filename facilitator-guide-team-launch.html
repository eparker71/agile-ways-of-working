Team Launch Guide

How to get a new team or initiative ready to deliver in one or two weeks, without endless up-front planning.

“What's the least we need to agree before we can start delivering?”

New teams and initiatives need a short period to get going: agree the purpose and vision, form the team, build a first backlog, and set up the board, tools and environments. It's often called Sprint Zero or Iteration 0; Disciplined Agile calls it inception. The Scrum Guide doesn't include it, because every sprint should deliver something usable, and the common criticism is that it drifts into weeks of design with nothing delivered. This board keeps it timeboxed to one or two weeks, uses a checklist that points to the right tool for each item, insists on a thin first slice of real work, and ends with a clear ready-to-start check.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A1 or A3 and put it on the team wall, or load the SVG as a digital board
  • Agree the timebox and the launch owner with the sponsor
  • Book the key sessions in the first week: kickoff, charter, vision, story map
  • Arrange access to tools and environments before day one, if you can
  • Pick only the checklist items this team needs; strike out the rest

2Agenda & run-sheet

WhenFocusSessions and tools
Day 1Why and whoProject Kickoff (half day). Start the Team Charter. Set the launch goal and timebox on the board.
Days 2–3What and for whomProduct Vision Board, Persona & Journey Map, and an Impact Map or OKRs for the first outcome.
Days 3–5The backlogUser Story Map for the first release slice; Epic Breakdown for the main epic; split the top items.
Week 1, throughoutEnvironmentRepository, pipeline, board and access. Pair on the walking skeleton as soon as the tools work.
Week 2, earlyWays of workingKanban Board Design, Definition of Done, Skills Matrix, AI Working Agreement, risks (Pre-mortem).
Week 2, lateReady to startExample Mapping on the top items, a first forecast, the exit check, then plan the first real iteration.

3Making the launch work

What good looks like for each part of the board.

1 · Launch Goal

  • A date to start delivering
  • What “ready” means, in one line

2 · Timebox

  • One or two weeks
  • A daily check-in on progress

3 · Launch Checklist

  • Only the items you need
  • Each has an owner
  • Just enough, not perfect

4 · Thin First Slice

  • Something real by the end
  • End to end, however small

5 · Environment & Tools

  • Everyone can work on day one
  • Pipeline to a test environment

6 · Ready to Start?

  • Every exit item ticked
  • If not, decide: extend briefly or start anyway

4Facilitation tips

Timebox it hardSet the end date first. Unfinished items carry into the first iteration, as backlog items.
Just enoughAim for a vision and backlog good enough to start, not complete. They'll change once you learn.
Deliver anywayA walking skeleton or one usable story proves the tools, pipeline and team work together.
Strike out what you don't needA small internal team may not need a RACI or a stakeholder map. Skip them.
Start the habits earlyHold daily check-ins and a short retro at the end of the launch, so the rhythms start now.
Don't make it a phaseIf the launch is turning into months of design, stop and start delivering.
Common pitfalls: no timebox; trying to complete every checklist item; detailed design of the whole solution; no working environment until week three; nothing delivered; repeating a “sprint zero” before every release.

5After the session

End of the launch

Run a short retro on the launch itself, and plan the first iteration.

First iteration

Carry over unfinished launch items as normal backlog items.

After a month

Revisit the Team Charter and Definition of Done with what you've learned.

Next time

Keep the checklist the team found useful for future launches.

6How it fits the toolkit

The launch board is a map of the toolkit's set-up tools: Project Kickoff, Team Charter, Product Vision Board, Persona & Journey Map, User Story Map, Epic Breakdown, Kanban Board Design and the others named on each checklist item. Once the team is ready, it moves on to Sprint Planning or its first replenishment, and the rest of the lifecycle.
