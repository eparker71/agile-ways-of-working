They work with any AI assistant, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Atlassian Rovo: copy one, fill in the [square brackets], and paste it in. Further down, every board's whiteboard prompt is in one place. For reports, such as an iteration report or an executive brief, see Reports by Role.

Set up the team 3

Draft working agreements

After a team discussion about how you want to work together.

Here are notes from our team's discussion about how we want to work together: [paste notes]. Draft 6 to 8 working agreements. Write each as a short, positive behaviour we can see (for example "We review pull requests within one working day"), not a value or a wish. Keep our own words where you can, and flag any agreements that contradict each other. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Agreements only work if the team writes and owns them. Use this as a first draft to edit together.

Pairs with: Team Charter

Turn notes into a personal user manual

When someone wants to share how they like to work, but finds it hard to write.

Here are my rough notes about how I like to work: [paste notes]. Turn them into a short personal user manual with these headings: I do my best work when…, The best way to reach me is…, Give me feedback by…, What drains me and what energises me, Things people misread about me, and Ask me about…. Keep it under 200 words, in the first person and my own voice. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Share only what you're comfortable sharing. It's yours to edit.

Pairs with: Personal User Manual

Plan a new joiner's first week

A week or two before someone joins the team.

[Name] is joining our team as a [role] on [start date]. Our team [one line on what the team does]. Plan their first week, one theme a day: meeting people and getting set up, the product and its users, pairing on a small piece of work, joining our events, and finishing something small. For each day, suggest who they should meet and one thing they should finish. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: A plan, not a firehose. Leave room for questions and for the person's own priorities.

Pairs with: Team Onboarding

Decide what to build 5

Turn an idea into a product bet

When an idea is gaining support before anyone has tested it.

Here's an idea for our product: [describe the idea]. Rewrite it as a one-sentence bet: "We believe that [who] will [do what] if we [offer what], because [what we know]." Then list the riskiest assumptions under value, usability, feasibility and viability, and suggest the cheapest test for the riskiest one. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The AI doesn't know your customers. Treat its assumptions as a starting list, then test them with real people.

Pairs with: Product Bet Canvas

Write customer interview questions

Before talking to customers about a problem.

We want to understand this problem: [describe the problem and who has it]. Write 8 interview questions that ask about real past behaviour ("Tell me about the last time…"), not opinions or hypothetical futures. Avoid leading questions and don't mention our solution. Add two follow-up prompts for going deeper. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Good interviews listen more than they ask. Don't read the questions out like a survey.

Pairs with: Opportunity Solution Tree · Persona & Journey Map

Summarise interview notes

After a round of customer interviews.

Here are notes from [number] customer interviews: [paste notes, with names removed]. Summarise them into: the jobs people are trying to get done, their main pains, what they do today instead, and three or four short quotes that capture each theme. Say how many interviews support each theme, and note anything only one person said. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Remove names and personal details first. Check the themes against the notes yourself.

Pairs with: Persona & Journey Map · Opportunity Solution Tree

Rewrite features as outcomes

When a roadmap or plan is a list of features and dates.

Here is our list of planned features: [paste list]. For each one, suggest the outcome it's meant to achieve (what will be different for users or the business), and a measure that would show it worked. Group features that serve the same outcome, and flag any where the outcome is unclear. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: If nobody can name the outcome for a feature, that's a finding, not a gap for the AI to fill.

Pairs with: Product Vision Board · Objectives & Key Results · Now · Next · Later Roadmap

Compare options by cost of delay

When several items compete for the next slot.

Here are the options we're choosing between, with what we know about each: [paste options]. For each, estimate user or business value, time criticality, and risk reduction on a 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 scale, plus a rough size. Calculate cost of delay divided by size, rank them, and explain the ranking in plain words. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The numbers are a conversation starter. The team's judgement, and what it knows about context, decides.

Pairs with: Prioritisation Board

Break down work 4

Turn a request into a user story

When a request arrives as a sentence in a chat or an email.

Here's a request we've received: [paste request]. Write it as a user story (As a…, I want…, so that…), with 3 to 6 acceptance criteria written as concrete examples. Note what's out of scope, and list the questions we need answered before it's ready to build. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Check who the user really is. The AI will guess if the request doesn't say.

Pairs with: Backlog Item Anatomy · Example Mapping

Split a large story

When a story is too big to finish in a few days.

This story is too big for our team to finish in a few days: [paste story and criteria]. Suggest 3 to 6 ways to split it into thin, vertical slices that each deliver something a user could see or use. Use patterns such as workflow steps, business rules, data variations or simple first, then more. Recommend which slice to build first, and why. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Avoid splits by technical layer ("front end", "database"). Each slice should be usable on its own.

Pairs with: Story Splitting

Generate rules, examples and questions

Before an Example Mapping session, or when a story feels fuzzy.

Here's a user story: [paste story]. List the business rules it involves. Under each rule, give two or three concrete examples, including edge cases. Then list the open questions nobody has answered yet. If there are more than four rules, suggest splitting the story by rule. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Use it to prepare, not to replace the conversation. The value of Example Mapping is the shared understanding.

Pairs with: Example Mapping

Break down an epic

When a large initiative needs shaping before work starts.

Here's an epic: [describe it]. Write it as a hypothesis (For… who… the… is a… that… unlike… our solution…). Suggest the outcome and a leading indicator, the smallest version that would test the hypothesis, and three to five features that each deliver value on their own. Mark which features belong in the first version. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Keep the first version small enough to learn from quickly. The AI tends to include too much.

Pairs with: Epic Breakdown Canvas · User Story Map

Run events & iterations 3

Draft a sprint goal

During planning, once the team has a rough selection of items.

These are the items our team is considering for the next iteration: [paste items]. Our product goal is [goal]. Suggest two or three possible sprint goals: one sentence each, about an outcome for users rather than a list of tasks. For each, say which items support it, and which items don't fit. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The team should agree the goal. Pick, edit or reject these together.

Pairs with: Sprint Planning

Plan a review with stakeholders

A few days before an iteration review.

Our iteration review is [length] long. We finished: [list items]. Our goal was [goal], and the key measures are [measures]. Stakeholders attending: [who]. Draft an agenda with timings that shows working product rather than slides, makes room for feedback, and ends with what the review changes in the backlog. Suggest three questions to ask stakeholders. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: A review is a conversation, not a demo. Keep most of the time for feedback.

Pairs with: Sprint Review

Tighten a stand-up that runs long

When the daily sync regularly overruns or turns into status reporting.

Our daily sync is meant to take 15 minutes but often takes [time]. Here's what usually happens: [describe it]. Suggest five specific changes to make it shorter and more useful, such as walking the board right to left. For each, explain the problem it solves. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Try one or two changes at a time, and check at the retro whether they helped.

Pairs with: Daily Sync

Manage flow 4

Group blockers by cause

When the same kinds of blockers keep coming back.

Here's our list of blockers from the last [period], with how many days each lasted: [paste list]. Group them into clusters by underlying cause (not by who caused them), total the blocked days for each cluster, and suggest one change that would tackle the biggest cluster. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Blame the system, not people. Remove names before pasting.

Pairs with: Blocker Clustering · Impediments Board

Suggest WIP limits and exit policies

When designing or redesigning a Kanban board.

Our team has [number] people. Our workflow columns are: [list columns]. Typically there are [number] items in each column. Suggest a starting WIP limit for each column and an explicit exit policy (when an item is done with that column). Explain your reasoning, and say what to watch to know if a limit is too tight or too loose. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: WIP limits are an experiment. Start a little tight, and review them after a few weeks.

Pairs with: Kanban Board Design · Ageing WIP & SLE

Find waste in a process

Before a Waste Walk or Value Stream Map, or when work feels slow.

Here's how work moves through our process, step by step, with rough times: [describe the steps]. Identify likely waste under the eight lean wastes (hand-offs, partly done work, task switching, waiting, features nobody uses, over-processing, defects, unused skills). Estimate which costs most, and suggest one change to try first. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The AI only sees what you describe. Go and see the real work before acting on its guesses.

Pairs with: Waste Walk · Value Stream Map · Lean Principles

Explain cycle-time data

When sharing flow data with people who don't use it every day.

Here are the cycle times, in days, of our last [number] finished items: [paste numbers]. Work out the 50th, 70th and 85th percentiles. Explain in plain words what they mean for someone asking "when will my item be done?", and suggest a service level expectation. Note any outliers worth looking at. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Check the arithmetic. AIs can get percentiles wrong, so a spreadsheet is a good cross-check.

Pairs with: Ageing WIP & SLE · Release Forecast & Burn-up

Engineering practices 4

Draft an architecture decision record

After the team has discussed a significant technical decision.

Here are notes from our discussion about [decision]: [paste notes]. Draft an architecture decision record with: a title, context (the problem and constraints), decision drivers, at least two options considered (including doing nothing) with pros and cons, the decision, and its consequences: what becomes easier and what becomes harder. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The record must reflect what the team actually decided and why. Correct anything the AI has smoothed over.

Pairs with: Architecture Decision Record

List test ideas and edge cases

When refining a story, or before testing starts.

Here's a feature and its acceptance criteria: [paste]. List test ideas grouped by happy path, edge cases, error handling, accessibility, security and performance. Highlight the five riskiest cases, and say which level of the test pyramid (unit, integration, end to end, or exploratory) suits each. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Use it to widen your thinking, not as the test plan. Testers' knowledge of the system matters more.

Pairs with: Test Strategy

Draft a go / no-go checklist and rollback plan

A week or so before a significant release.

We're releasing [what] to [who] on [date], using [approach, e.g. a feature flag or canary]. Draft a go / no-go checklist, a rollback plan (how we'd know it's going wrong, how we'd undo it, how long that takes, and what can't be undone), and a list of who needs to know what, and when. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Test the rollback before release day. A plan nobody has tried isn't a plan.

Pairs with: Release Readiness & Strategy

Write a blameless incident timeline

Before a post-incident review.

Here are notes and chat messages from an incident: [paste, with names replaced by roles]. Build a timeline with: time, what happened, what people knew at that point, and what they did and why. Use neutral, blameless language. Then list possible contributing factors under technology, process, knowledge, environment and communication. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Replace names with roles first. Contributing factors are for the review to agree, not the AI to decide.

Pairs with: Blameless Incident Review

Reflect & improve 4

Group retro notes into themes

After a retro with a lot of stickies, or a remote retro with typed notes.

Here are the notes from our retrospective: [paste notes]. Group them into themes, and say how many notes each theme has. For the top three themes, suggest one specific action each, with a suggested owner role and a "done when" we could check by the next retro. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The team chooses the actions. Use the themes to speed up sorting, not to skip the discussion.

Pairs with: Retrospective Kit

Freshen up a stale retro

When retros have started to feel the same every time.

Our team of [number] has been running the same retro format for [time], and it's gone stale. Recently we've been struggling with [topic]. Suggest five different retro formats or activities that would fit, each with a short description, timings for [length], and why it might help. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Stale retros are often a sign that actions aren't being followed up. Check that first.

Pairs with: Retrospective Kit · Facilitation Techniques

Run five whys on a problem

When a problem keeps coming back.

This problem keeps happening: [describe it, with facts and examples]. Ask "why?" five times to work towards a root cause, writing down each answer. Then challenge the chain: point out any link that is a guess rather than a fact, and suggest one or two other branches worth exploring. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Root causes are about the system, never a person. Check each link with real evidence.

Pairs with: Root Cause Analysis · A3 Problem-Solving

Turn an idea into an experiment

When someone suggests an improvement at a retro.

Our team wants to try this improvement: [describe it]. The problem it's meant to fix is [problem]. Turn it into a small experiment: a hypothesis (If we…, for…, then we expect…, we'll know when we see…), a baseline and a target, a measure, a guardrail (what mustn't get worse), and a timebox of one or two iterations. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Keep it small enough to start tomorrow.

Pairs with: 1% Experiment Canvas · Marginal Gains Workshop

Run a project 4

Run a pre-mortem

At the start of a project or before a big milestone.

Imagine it's [date] and our project, [describe it], has failed badly. List 15 plausible reasons why, in the past tense, grouped into themes such as people, technology, customers, money, dependencies and decisions. Then pick the five most likely, and for each suggest a way to prevent it and an early warning sign. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The team's own fears matter more than the AI's list. Use it after the team has written its own.

Pairs with: Pre-mortem · RAID Log

Draft an outcome-based status report

When writing a regular update for sponsors or a steering group.

Here are this period's raw updates from the team: [paste updates]. Draft a one-page status report with: a one-line headline, outcomes against their measures, a forecast as a range with its assumptions, what was delivered and learned, the top three risks or issues with owners, and the decisions we need, from whom, by when. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Don't let the AI turn amber into green. The report must be honest about problems.

Pairs with: Outcome Status Report · Release Forecast & Burn-up

Build a first RAID log

At the start of a project.

Here's our project brief: [paste brief]. Draft a first RAID log: risks (with likelihood, impact and a response), assumptions (and how we'd test each), current issues, and dependencies (who needs what from whom, and by when). Note which items need an owner from outside the team. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The log is only useful if it's reviewed regularly and every item has an owner.

Pairs with: RAID Log

Map stakeholders

At the start of a project, or when someone important seems unhappy.

Our project is [describe it]. Here are the people and groups involved: [list them, with what you know]. Place each on an influence and interest grid, say what each wants or worries about, and suggest how and how often to engage them. Then suggest who might be missing from the list. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Keep personal judgements about named people out of shared tools.

Pairs with: Stakeholder Map

Coordinate teams 1

Find hidden dependencies across teams

Before or during cross-team planning.

Here are the plans for the next [period] from these teams: [paste each team's plan]. Find the dependencies between them: who needs what from whom, and by when. Flag ones that look late or at risk, and suggest which could be removed rather than managed, for example by reordering work or using a stub. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The AI can only see dependencies that are written down. Ask the teams what's missing.

Pairs with: Dependency Map · Cross-Team Planning

Lead the organisation 2

Explain a change to the people affected

Before announcing a change to how people work.

We're changing [what's changing] for [who's affected], starting [when]. Draft a short message that explains why the change is needed now, what it means for them day to day, what's staying the same, what support they'll get, and how they can ask questions or raise concerns. Plain, honest language, under 250 words. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: A message is only the start. People also need to be heard. Follow up in person.

Pairs with: ADKAR Change Readiness

Sort situations into Cynefin® domains

When a group disagrees about how to approach a problem.

Here are the situations our team is facing: [list them]. Using the Cynefin® framework's domains (clear, complicated, complex, chaotic, and confused), suggest which domain each belongs in and why. For the complex ones, suggest two or three safe-to-fail experiments, with signs that one is working and signs to stop. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The value is in the group's discussion of where things belong. Use the AI's view as one voice in it.

Pairs with: Cynefin® Sense-Making

Facilitate & coach 2

Prepare coaching questions

Before a coaching conversation.

I'm coaching someone who wants to [their goal, in their words]. Using the GROW model (goal, reality, options, will), suggest three open questions for each stage. Avoid questions that hide advice ("Have you thought about…?"). Add two questions to check their commitment at the end. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Questions are a guide. Follow the person, not the list.

Pairs with: Coaching Conversation

Plan a workshop

When you need to design a session from scratch.

I'm running a [length] workshop for [who, and how many] to [purpose]. By the end we need [outcome]. Design it: an agenda with timings, a technique for each part (for example 1-2-4-All, dot voting, silent writing), what to prepare, and how to make sure quieter people are heard. Include a check-in and a close. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Build in more time than you think. Workshops nearly always run long.

Pairs with: Facilitation Techniques · Lean Coffee

   Subtitle: If · for · then · we'll know

  • ADKAR Change ReadinessGuide
    Create an editable whiteboard for “ADKAR Change Readiness”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Find where people are in a change, spot the barrier point, and act on it before moving on.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Change, Sponsor

Sections:
1. Change on a Page: top full width, about 100% wide and 15% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: What, why, who, when
   Content: What's changing: · Who's affected: · Why now: · Key dates:
2. Readiness Grid: middle full width, about 100% wide and 35% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Score 1–5 · the first low score is the barrier point
   Content: Person or group: · A · awareness: · D · desire: · K · knowledge: · A · ability: · R · reinforcement: · Barrier point: · 1. 2 (×15) · 3. 4 (×15) · 5. 1 (×10) · 2. 3 (×10) · 4. 5 (×10) · 5 (×5) · Circle each score. Scores run 1 (not at all) to 5 (fully). The barrier point is the first element scored 3 or lower, working left to right. Fix it before working on later elements.
3. Awareness: bottom left, about 20% wide and 25% tall.
   Content: Missing if…: · People ask “why are we doing this?” Rumours fill the silence · What helps: · Sponsor explains the why, early and often · Share the cost of not changing · Our action:
4. Desire: bottom left, about 20% wide and 25% tall; header colour purple.
   Content: Missing if…: · People understand, but resist or stall “What's in it for me?” is unanswered · What helps: · Involve people in shaping the change · Managers discuss concerns one-to-one · Our action:
5. Knowledge: bottom centre, about 20% wide and 25% tall; header colour sky blue.
   Content: Missing if…: · Willing, but unsure what to do Lots of “how do I…?” questions · What helps: · Training, guides and worked examples · Show the new way on real work · Our action:
6. Ability: bottom right, about 20% wide and 25% tall.
   Content: Missing if…: · Know how, but can't do it yet Old habits return under pressure · What helps: · Coaching, pairing and practice time · Remove blockers: tools, time, access · Our action:
7. Reinforcement: bottom right, about 20% wide and 25% tall; header colour orange.
   Content: Missing if…: · Early wins, then people slide back The old way is still rewarded · What helps: · Recognise and celebrate adoption · Measure use; fix what pulls people back · Our action:
8. Action Plan: bottom left, about 65% wide and 15% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: One action per barrier point
   Content: Who (person or group): · Barrier point: · Action: · Owner: · By when:
9. Reinforce & Re-assess: bottom right, about 35% wide and 15% tall; header colour dark green.
   Content: We'll recognise success by: · We'll measure adoption by: · Re-assess on:

Footer questions: “Where is each group in this change, really?” “What's the first thing stopping them, and what would help most?”

  • Ageing WIP & SLEGuide
    Create an editable whiteboard for “Ageing WIP & Service Level Expectation”, a workshop board.
