Impact Map Guide

How to connect a business goal to the people whose behaviour you need to change, and what to build.

“Whose behaviour has to change for us to reach this goal?”

Impact mapping, introduced by Gojko Adzic, builds a simple tree from a goal (why) to the actors who can help or hinder it (who), the impacts on their behaviour you need (how), and the deliverables that might cause those impacts (what). Every feature then traces back to the goal, which makes it easy to say no to work that doesn't, and to test the riskiest assumption first.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Agree one measurable business goal, ideally from the OKRs or Product Vision Board
  • Bring what you know about users, customers and other actors
  • Invite people who understand the business and the users

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10GoalWrite the goal and its measure. Check it's an outcome, not a feature.
10–25ActorsWho can help us reach the goal, or get in the way? Include users, partners, internal teams.
25–45ImpactsFor each actor, how must their behaviour change? Write it as a behaviour, not a feature.
45–65DeliverablesWhat could we build or do to cause each impact? Several options per impact are fine.
65–802 · Riskiest AssumptionWhich impact are we least sure will happen? How will we test it?
80–903 · First StepPick the deliverable to try first, and how you'll know the impact happened.

3Making each level strong

Quality checks for each level of the map.

Goal

  • Measurable
  • An outcome, not output

Actors

  • Specific groups
  • Include those who could block it

Impacts

  • Behaviour changes
  • Observable

Deliverables

  • Options, not commitments
  • Each links to an impact

Riskiest Assumption

  • Tested first
  • Cheaply

4Facilitation tips

Impacts are behaviour“Customers reorder weekly” is an impact. “Add a reorder button” is a deliverable.
Include blockersActors who could get in the way are as important as those who help.
Many optionsSeveral deliverables per impact give you choices. Pick the cheapest that might work.
Cut freelyDeliverables that don't trace to an impact don't belong on the roadmap.
Test the riskiest linkIf the behaviour change doesn't happen, nothing below it matters.
Keep it visibleRevisit the map when the goal or evidence changes.
Common pitfalls: a goal that's a feature; impacts written as features; one deliverable per impact; mapping everything already planned and calling it done; never testing the riskiest impact.

5After the session

Straight away

Share the map and the first deliverable with the team and stakeholders.

This iteration

Build or test the first deliverable.

When results come in

Did the impact happen? Keep, change or drop that branch.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.
