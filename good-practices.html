Good Practices

21 principles that run through the whole toolkit, each with why it matters, what it looks like, the tools that put it into practice, and the anti-patterns it prevents. They're good practices, not “best practices”: what works depends on your situation.

How we work 17

01

Outcomes over output

Judge work by what changed for users and the organisation, not by how much was shipped.

Why it matters: Output is easy to count, but busy teams can deliver lots of features that change nothing.

What it looks like

  • Goals and epics state a measurable outcome
  • Reviews ask “did it work?”, not just “is it done?”
  • Roadmaps are organised by goals, not features and dates

Popularised by Joshua Seiden, Outcomes Over Output (2019).

Tools: Product Operating Model · Value Metrics · Objectives & Key Results · Now · Next · Later Roadmap · Product Bet Canvas · Outcome Status Report

Prevents: Epics have no outcome or measure · OKRs are task lists · Roadmaps are lists of features with dates

02

Remove waste, starting with waiting

Keep asking which steps add value for the customer, and remove or shrink the rest.

Why it matters: In most knowledge work, items spend far longer waiting than being worked on, so the biggest gains are in the gaps.

What it looks like

  • Waiting time is measured, not just work time
  • Hand-offs and approvals are questioned, not assumed
  • The team looks for waste at its retros, using the eight wastes

From lean thinking, which grew out of the Toyota Production System.

Tools: Lean Principles · Waste Walk · Value Stream Map · Blocker Clustering · Ageing WIP & SLE · Marginal Gains Workshop · A3 Problem-Solving

Prevents: Teams are constantly waiting on each other · The same blockers keep coming back · Everyone has several things in progress at once

03

Stop starting, start finishing

Limit work in progress so work flows to done instead of piling up half-finished.

Why it matters: Every extra item in progress slows all the others down and hides blocked work.

What it looks like

  • Explicit WIP limits on the board
  • Swarm on blocked or ageing items before pulling new ones
  • Fewer initiatives running at once at portfolio level

A saying from the Kanban community.

Tools: Kanban Board Design · Ageing WIP & SLE · Buffering & Swarming · Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix

Prevents: Everyone has several things in progress at once · Work sits blocked for days without anyone noticing · Unplanned work wrecks every plan · Far too many initiatives are in flight

04

Small batches, early feedback

Deliver in thin, usable slices so you learn from real users early and often.

Why it matters: Big batches delay feedback, hide risk and make every change expensive.

What it looks like

  • Stories slice vertically through every layer
  • Each release is small enough to roll back easily
  • Stakeholders use the product at every review

Tools: Story Splitting · User Story Map · Epic Breakdown Canvas · Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Sprint Review

Prevents: Stories are technical tasks, such as “set up the database” · Stories carry over from one iteration to the next · The review is a one-way demo with no feedback

05

Say no so the yes means something

Keep priorities few and ranked, and say out loud what you're not doing.

Why it matters: When everything is a priority, nothing is, and teams spread themselves thin.

What it looks like

  • One ordered list, not several “top priorities”
  • A visible “not doing” list with reasons
  • Old backlog items are archived, not kept forever

Tools: Prioritisation Board · Now · Next · Later Roadmap · Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix

Prevents: Everything is a must-have or top priority · The backlog has hundreds of items, some years old · Far too many initiatives are in flight

06

Make work and decisions visible

Put the work, risks and decisions where everyone can see them.

Why it matters: Hidden work and unrecorded decisions turn into surprises, rework and blame.

What it looks like

  • One board shows all the team's work, including unplanned work
  • Decisions are written down with who made them
  • Status shows confidence and trend, not only a colour

Tools: Kanban Board Design · RAID Log · RACI & Decision Log · Architecture Decision Record · Outcome Status Report

Prevents: Status is green until it's suddenly red · Bad news travels slowly upwards · Nobody is sure who makes which decisions

07

Safety before performance

Make it safe to speak up, admit mistakes and disagree; performance follows.

Why it matters: People hide problems and ideas when speaking up feels risky, so leaders learn too late.

What it looks like

  • Leaders respond to bad news with thanks and curiosity
  • Disagreement happens openly, on the problem not the person
  • Quiet voices are invited in

Psychological safety research by Amy Edmondson.

Tools: Psychological Safety Check · Leading for Psychological Safety · Levels of Conflict · Feedback Conversation · Blameless Incident Review

Prevents: Quiet people rarely speak in meetings · The team avoids open disagreement · Bad news travels slowly upwards

08

Inspect and adapt on a rhythm

Look at results and the way you work at short, regular intervals, and change something each time.

Why it matters: Without a regular loop, problems repeat and plans drift away from reality.

What it looks like

  • Every event has a purpose and changes something
  • Retro actions are reviewed at the next retro
  • Cross-team problems get their own improvement loop

Empirical process control, at the heart of most agile methods.

Tools: Events & Cadences · Retrospective Kit · Team Health Check · Cross-Team Retro · Kanban Cadences

Prevents: The same issues come up at every retrospective · Planning is just dragging tickets into the iteration · Problems between teams never get fixed

09

Test the riskiest assumption first

Treat ideas as bets and find the cheapest way to learn whether you're wrong.

Why it matters: Building first and testing later is the most expensive way to find out nobody wanted it.

What it looks like

  • Assumptions are written down and scored
  • Interviews, prototypes and fake doors come before full builds
  • Funding follows evidence in stages

Tools: Product Bet Canvas · 1% Experiment Canvas · Design Sprint · Opportunity Solution Tree · Pre-mortem

Prevents: Funding is decided once a year

10

Match the approach to the situation

Plan what's predictable, experiment where it isn't, and use only as much process as the work needs.

Why it matters: One method applied everywhere either over-plans uncertain work or under-plans risky work.

What it looks like

  • Complex work gets experiments; complicated work gets analysis
  • Forecasts are ranges, not single dates
  • Structure is sized to the project, not copied from the last one

Draws on the Cynefin® framework (Dave Snowden).

Tools: Delivery Approach · Cynefin® Sense-Making · Right-Size Your Structure · Release Forecast & Burn-up

Prevents: Agile is mandated as a process rather than a goal · Single delivery dates are promised

11

Involve the people who do the work

The people closest to the work help decide how it's done and how change happens.

Why it matters: Imposed processes and changes get compliance at best; involvement gets commitment and better ideas.

What it looks like

  • Teams agree their own working agreements
  • Decision rights are explicit, and delegated as far as is safe
  • Change is led with people, not announced to them

Tools: Team Charter · Delegation Board · Team Launch (Sprint Zero) · ADKAR Change Readiness · RACI & Decision Log

Prevents: A new team starts work without agreeing how to work · Nobody is sure who makes which decisions · The product owner is rarely available · Change is announced and expected to stick

12

Build quality in

Make quality part of every step, with a shared Definition of Done and automation, instead of inspecting at the end.

Why it matters: Quality added at the end is slow, costly and still misses things.

What it looks like

  • Acceptance criteria are agreed before work starts
  • Tests and checks run automatically on every change
  • “Done” means usable by users, not “dev done”

From W. Edwards Deming's “cease dependence on inspection”.

Tools: SLOs & Error Budgets · Test Strategy · Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Example Mapping · Team Charter

Prevents: “Done” means “development done” · Acceptance criteria are written after the work · Delivery gets faster while quality drops

13

Learn without blame

When things go wrong, look at the system that let it happen, not the person who was nearest.

Why it matters: Blame teaches people to hide mistakes, so the same failures come back.

What it looks like

  • Incident reviews ask how, not who
  • Recurring problems are traced to root causes
  • Lessons are shared beyond the team

Tools: Blameless Incident Review · Root Cause Analysis · Blocker Clustering · Retrospective Kit

Prevents: The same blockers keep coming back

14

Optimise the whole, not the parts

Improve the flow of value end to end, across teams, rather than making each team look busy.

Why it matters: Local improvements can make the whole system slower, especially where teams depend on each other.

What it looks like

  • The value stream is mapped from idea to customer
  • Team boundaries reduce hand-offs and dependencies
  • Teams plan together, rather than being planned for

From systems thinking and lean (W. Edwards Deming, Taiichi Ohno).

Tools: DevOps Principles · Value Stream Map · Team Topology Design · Dependency Map · Cross-Team Planning · Cross-Team Sync

Prevents: Teams are constantly waiting on each other · One team owns far too much · The cross-team sync is a status meeting · Multi-team planning is a top-down announcement

15

Measure to learn, not to judge

Use metrics to understand and improve the system, never as targets for individuals or teams.

Why it matters: When a measure becomes a target, people game it and it stops telling you anything.

What it looks like

  • Team measures, never individual ones
  • Speed is always balanced with quality and outcomes
  • Velocity stays inside the team that produced it

Goodhart's law (Charles Goodhart).

Tools: Delivery Metrics at a Glance · Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt · Estimation · Release Forecast & Burn-up · Value Metrics

Prevents: Velocity is used as a target · Teams' velocity is compared · People are measured by tickets closed

16

Share knowledge, avoid heroes

Spread skills so the team can keep going when anyone is away.

Why it matters: A single expert becomes a bottleneck, and a risk the day they leave.

What it looks like

  • Skills are mapped and gaps planned for
  • Pairing and mobbing are normal, not special
  • New joiners are onboarded with a plan and a buddy

Tools: Skills Matrix · Pairing & Mobbing · Team Onboarding

Prevents: One person is the only one who can do a key task

17

People stay accountable, including for AI

Tools, including AI agents, can do the work, but named people stay accountable for the result.

Why it matters: Without clear ownership, mistakes from automated work fall between the cracks.

What it looks like

  • Autonomy for AI is agreed stage by stage
  • AI output is reviewed like any other work
  • Every decision and tool has a named owner

Tools: AI in Delivery · AI Working Agreement · RACI & Decision Log

How we facilitate 4

18

Think alone, then talk together

Give people silent time to write their own ideas before any group discussion.

Why it matters: Open discussion favours the loudest and most senior voices; silent writing gets everyone's thinking.

What it looks like

  • Silent writing on stickies before sharing
  • Dot voting instead of debate to choose
  • Pairs or small groups before the whole room

Tools: Facilitation Techniques · Lean Coffee · Retrospective Kit · Design Sprint

Prevents: Quiet people rarely speak in meetings

19

Purpose, output, timebox

Every session states why it exists, what it will produce, and how long it will take.

Why it matters: Meetings without a clear purpose drift, overrun and leave people wondering why they came.

What it looks like

  • The purpose and output are written up at the start
  • A visible timer for each part
  • Events that lack a purpose are merged or dropped

Tools: Events & Cadences · Facilitation Techniques · Daily Sync · Backlog Refinement

Prevents: Meetings overrun and nobody is sure why they exist · The daily sync is a status report to the manager · Refinement happens during planning

20

Close with an owner and a date

Every action leaving a session has one named owner and a date.

Why it matters: Actions owned by “the team” or with no date quietly disappear.

What it looks like

  • Few actions, each with one owner
  • Actions are reviewed at the start of the next session
  • Blockers go on a visible impediments list

Tools: Retrospective Kit · Impediments Board · RAID Log

Prevents: The same issues come up at every retrospective

21

Ask more than you tell

Facilitators and coaches help people find their own answers through questions.

Why it matters: Answers people reach themselves stick; answers handed to them rarely do.

What it looks like

  • Open questions: what, how, who
  • Silence is given time to work
  • Advice is offered last, and labelled as advice

Tools: Coaching Conversation · Scrum Master & Agile Coach Cheat Sheet · Facilitation Techniques

Prevents: The Scrum Master has become the team's secretary

Why “good”, not “best”

A single best practice only exists when cause and effect are obvious. Most delivery work is complex, where the right move depends on context and has to be discovered. Use these as principles to reason from, and adapt them; the Cynefin® Sense-Making board helps you decide which approach fits. For situations rather than principles, see the Scenario Playbook.
