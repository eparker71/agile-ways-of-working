Delivery Approach Guide

How to choose a predictive, agile or hybrid approach for each part of a project, and connect the parts.

“Which parts of this work will we only understand by doing them?”

Not every project, or every part of one, suits the same approach. A predictive approach plans and designs up front and controls change; it suits work that is well understood and costly to change. An agile approach delivers in short iterations and learns as it goes; it suits work where requirements will change and value can arrive in small pieces. Most real projects are hybrid: a regulated payments change and a new customer app can sit side by side. Choosing deliberately is what PMI calls tailoring. The board's suitability check is written in our own words but inspired by the suitability filter in the Agile Practice Guide, published by PMI and the Agile Alliance, and it scores the culture, the team and the project. Then you choose an approach for each work stream and agree how the parts connect. PMI® is a registered trademark of Project Management Institute, Inc.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or share it on screen
  • Bring the project brief, and a rough list of its main parts or work streams
  • Invite people who see the work differently: sponsor, delivery, a team member, governance
  • Ask each person to score the ten factors privately first, to avoid anchoring

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–5Why chooseExplain the three approaches, and that hybrid is normal, not a compromise.
5–25Suitability checkReveal private scores row by row. Discuss the rows where people differ most.
25–35Read the scoreTotal it, but look at the pattern: which rows pull in which direction?
35–50Work streamsSplit the project into parts. Choose an approach for each, and say why.
50–60Connect the partsAgree shared milestones, gates and their evidence, the reporting rhythm, and a date to revisit.

3The three approaches

An approach per part of the work, not one label for everything.

Agile

  • Short iterations
  • Learn and adapt

Hybrid

  • Mix by work stream
  • Agree how they meet

Predictive

  • Plan up front
  • Control change

Connections

  • Milestones and gates
  • One reporting rhythm

Revisit

  • As you learn
  • Change the approach if needed

4Facilitation tips

It's not a loyalty testChoosing predictive for a part of the work isn't failing at agile; it's choosing well.
Score privately firstPeople anchor on the first number said aloud. Reveal together.
Disagreement is dataWhere scores differ most, people see the project differently. Talk about why.
Look for cheap changeWherever change is cheap, iterate. Wherever it's expensive, design first.
Gates need evidenceIn a hybrid project, answer each governance gate with working results where you can.
Revisit itThe right approach can change as uncertainty falls. Put a date in the diary.
Common pitfalls: picking an approach to match the organisation's fashion; one approach for every part of a varied project; hybrid that means waterfall with stand-ups; never agreeing how agile and predictive parts connect; never revisiting.

5After the session

Straight away

Record the approach for each part in the Project Kickoff and RAID Log.

This week

Set up the shared milestones and reporting rhythm.

At each milestone

Check: is each part's approach still right?

At close-out

Note what worked about the approach, for the next project.
