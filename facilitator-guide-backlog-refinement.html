“Which items do we need ready for the next iteration?”

Refinement is the ongoing work of getting the next few backlog items ready: ordered by value, understood by the team, split small enough to finish in a few days, explored through examples, and roughly sized. Scrum describes it as an ongoing activity rather than a formal event. Many teams hold a regular session, often taking around 10% of their capacity. The board walks each item through six steps and points to the toolkit tool for each one, so the session stays focused and finishes with ready items.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • The PO brings the top items in order, with any context or data
  • Have the Story Splitting poster and Example Mapping board to hand
  • Agree the team's Definition of Ready, if you have one
  • Invite experts only for the items that need them

2Agenda & run-sheet

MinutesStepFacilitator notes
0–51 · Session goalAgree which items to refine and the iteration they need to be ready for.
5–15OrderConfirm the order with the PO. Drop or defer anything no longer valuable.
15–60Understand · Split · ExamplesFor each item: who it's for and why, split it if it's too big, then map rules and examples.
60–75SizeSize the items quickly, or check they're small enough to count.
75–85Ready?Check each item against the Definition of Ready. Record open questions with owners.
85–90CloseRead back what's ready and what isn't, and when the next session is.

3Making each step strong

Quality checks for each step of the flow.

Order

  • Most valuable first
  • Drop what's no longer needed

Understand

  • Who it's for, and why
  • What done looks like

Split

  • Finishes in a few days
  • Vertical slices

Examples

  • Rules and examples
  • Questions have owners

Size

  • Relative and quick
  • Or just count small items

Ready?

  • Checked against the DoR
  • A guide, not a gate

4Facilitation tips

Just in timeRefine one or two iterations ahead. Refining further out wastes effort when plans change.
Whole teamDevelopers and testers find hidden work and risks the PO can't see alone.
Timebox each itemIf an item takes more than 15–20 minutes, it probably needs splitting or a spike.
Questions are progressAn open question found now is cheaper than one found mid-iteration.
Don't design it allAgree enough to start. The team will work out details while building.
Little and oftenA short weekly session beats one long, tiring one before planning.
Common pitfalls: refining too far ahead; the PO presenting while the team listens; designing solutions in detail; no splitting; endless debate on one item; treating the Definition of Ready as a gate.

5After the session

Straight away

Update the backlog: splits, examples, sizes and open questions.

Before planning

Question owners find the answers.

At planning

Ready items go in first.

At the retro

Ask whether items arrived in planning ready enough.
