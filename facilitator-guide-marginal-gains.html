Marginal Gains Facilitator Guide

How to run the Marginal Gains Workshop, from preparation through follow-up.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Systems-Thinking Approach
Sprint board →Quarter board →SVG · Sprint →SVG · Quarter →Experiment Canvas →

“What 1% improvement would help us deliver customer value faster, better, and more predictably?”

The workshop borrows the marginal gains idea made famous by British Cycling: rather than hunting for one big fix, look across the whole system for many small improvements that compound. The board breaks the system into eight areas so teams look beyond their own backlog, then turns the best ideas into a few small experiments with an owner and a measure.

1Before the workshop

  • Choose the sprint board (team-level, 90 min) or quarter board (several teams or a quarter, longer)
  • Print the board at A3 or larger, or upload the SVG as a Miro / Mural / FigJam background
  • Yellow and green sticky notes, markers, and dot stickers (3 per person)
  • Print 3 copies of the 1% Experiment Canvas
  • Invite people from across the system: product, engineering, design, ops, stakeholders
  • Bring data: flow metrics, cycle time, escaped defects, last health check, open retro actions
  • Send a short pre-read with the core question and the eight areas
  • Agree a decision-maker who can approve experiments on the day
  • Book a 15-min follow-up slot for the experiment review
  • Prepare a parking lot for big issues that aren't 1% changes

3Probing questions by area

Use these when a section stays empty or the conversation stays shallow. You don't need to cover every area. Go where the energy and the data point.

1 · Strategy

  • Do we know which priority wins when two collide?
  • How long do decisions wait for the right person?
  • Could everyone here name our top 3 objectives?

2 · Outcomes

  • How do we know a release created customer value?
  • Which metric do we look at but never act on?
  • How long until we learn whether a feature worked?

3 · Product

  • How often does work start before it's ready?
  • Is the backlog a plan or a graveyard?
  • Where does the roadmap surprise the team?

4 · Teams

  • Where are we overloaded or context-switching?
  • Which ritual feels like theatre?
  • Who do we wait on inside the team?

5 · Dependencies

  • Which hand-off causes the longest wait?
  • Which team do we always end up chasing?
  • Where do we find dependencies too late?

6 · Engineering

  • How long from commit to production?
  • Which environment or test slows us most?
  • What tech debt taxes every change?

7 · Delivery

  • Where does work sit idle in our flow?
  • How predictable were we vs. commitments?
  • What makes releases stressful?

8 · Learning

  • Which retro action did we never finish?
  • How do we share what other teams learn?
  • When did we last run a real experiment?

4Facilitation tips

Keep it 1%If an idea needs budget, a reorg or a quarter of work, it goes to the parking lot. Ask: “What's the smallest slice we could try next week?”
Friction before fixesHold off on solutions while people write yellow stickies. Write first, solve later. Early solutioning narrows the field.
Look across areasThe best insights connect sections. Draw lines between related stickies and ask “What's upstream of this?”
Protect safetyTalk about the system, not people. If one voice dominates, use silent writing and round-robin read-outs.
Data beats opinionBack up the top frictions with a number wherever you can. That number becomes the experiment's baseline.
Owner ≠ doerThe owner makes sure the experiment happens and is reviewed. They don't have to do all the work.
Common pitfalls: too many experiments (cap it at 3); vague measures (“improve quality”); no review date; ideas that depend entirely on another team that wasn't in the room; repeating last time's actions without asking why they didn't stick.

5After the workshop

Within 24 hours

Photograph or export the board. Share the 3 Experiment Canvases and add each experiment to the team backlog as a visible item. Log parking-lot items with an owner.

Mid-point

Quick stand-up check: has it started? Is the leading indicator moving? Remove blockers early.

At the review

Use section 7 of the canvas. Decide whether to persevere, pivot or stop, and share what you learned. Then make it standard or let it go.

Running remotely

Use the SVG as a locked background layer. Pre-create sticky stacks in both colours, use the tool's built-in voting, and add 10 minutes for tech and breakouts.
