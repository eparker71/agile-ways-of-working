Delegation Board Guide

How to agree who decides what, at which of seven levels, and how to move decisions toward the team over time.

“Which decisions could the team make without us?”

Empowerment isn't all or nothing. The delegation board, from Jurgen Appelo's Management 3.0, uses seven levels from Tell (the leader decides) to Delegate (the team decides and doesn't need to report back). For each decision area, the leader and team agree today's level and where they want to get to. Making it explicit avoids two common problems: teams waiting for permission they already have, and leaders overruling decisions they'd said were delegated.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • List decision areas the team cares about, or ask the team to bring them
  • Leaders: think about which areas you're genuinely ready to delegate further
  • Bring any policies or limits that always apply (budget, legal, security)
  • Optionally, use delegation poker cards (1–7) for voting

3The seven levels

Each level describes who decides and how the other party is involved.

1 Tell · 2 Sell

  • The leader decides
  • Sell explains why

3 Consult

  • Leader asks, then decides
  • Input is genuinely considered

4 Agree

  • Decided together
  • Takes the most time

5 Advise · 6 Inquire

  • The team decides
  • Leader advises or hears afterwards

7 Delegate

  • The team decides alone
  • No need to report back

Guardrails

  • Limits at every level
  • Written down, not assumed

4Facilitation tips

Every level is validThe aim isn't level 7 for everything. It's clarity about the right level for each area.
Vote simultaneouslyDelegation poker stops people anchoring on the leader's view.
Leaders go first in trustMoving an area up one level is a small, concrete way to build trust.
Guardrails enable delegationClear limits make it easier for leaders to let go.
Keep your wordOverruling a delegated decision costs more trust than never delegating it.
Revisit itAs the team grows, move areas up. Review every few months.
Common pitfalls: leaders announcing levels instead of agreeing them; everything at level 7 on paper but not in practice; overruling delegated decisions; vague decision areas; no guardrails; never reviewing it.

5After the session

Straight away

Post the board where the team and leader can both see it.

When a decision comes up

Check the board first: who decides, at what level?

At the review date

Move areas up where the team is ready; discuss any that went wrong.

When people change

Revisit the board with new leaders or team members.

6How it fits the toolkit

The leadership tools work together: Value Metrics show whether investment is paying off, the Portfolio Kanban decides what to fund and limits how much runs at once, the Delegation Board makes decision rights explicit, and ADKAR Change Readiness helps people through the change. OKRs and Team Topology Design connect them to the teams.
