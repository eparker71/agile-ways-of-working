1% Experiment Canvas Guide

How to turn a small improvement idea into an experiment that gets run, measured and reviewed.

“What's the smallest change that could prove us right or wrong?”

Improvement ideas often die because they are vague: no owner, no measure, no end date. The canvas makes each idea testable. Fill it in when you choose an experiment (sections 1–6) and complete it at the review (section 7). Use one canvas per experiment.

1Before the session

  • Print one A3 canvas per experiment, or duplicate the SVG frame digitally
  • Know which friction or idea you are testing, ideally from the Marginal Gains board or a retro
  • Find the data source for your baseline before the session if you can
  • Make sure the likely owner is in the room
  • Book the review date before you leave the session

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–31 · FrictionTick the board area and describe the pain point in the team's own words. Be specific: “PRs wait over a day for review”, not “slow reviews”.
3–102 · HypothesisComplete the If / for / then / we'll know sentence. If you can't name the signal, the idea isn't testable yet.
10–143 · The 1% ChangeDescribe exactly what will be different, then shrink it. What could we try tomorrow, with no approval needed?
14–204 · MeasureRecord the baseline (today), the target, and how it will be measured. Add an early-signal indicator and a guardrail metric.
20–245 · Plan & TimeboxName one owner, any supporters, a start date, a mid-point check and a review date. Tick the timebox.
24–286 · RisksWhat must be true? What could go wrong? Who needs to know? Use stickies for each risk.
Review7 · Result & DecisionAt the review date: result vs. target, then persevere, pivot or stop, and what we learned and who should hear it.

3Making each section strong

A quick quality check for each section of the canvas.

1 · Where's the Friction?

  • Is it an observed problem, not a guess?
  • Could someone outside the team understand it?

2 · Hypothesis

  • Does it name the change, who it affects, the outcome and the signal?
  • Could it be proven wrong?

3 · The 1% Change

  • Could we start within a week?
  • Is it within the team's control?

4 · Measure

  • Is there a number for the baseline?
  • Is the target realistic for the timebox?
  • Does the guardrail protect quality?

5 · Plan & Timebox

  • Is there exactly one owner?
  • Is the review date in calendars?

6 · Risks & Assumptions

  • What's the riskiest assumption?
  • How will we spot early that it's failing?

7 · Result & Decision

  • Persevere = make it standard practice
  • Pivot = adjust and run again
  • Stop = no gain, so share why

4Worked example

FrictionEngineering: pull requests wait more than a day for review.
If we…hold a 15-minute review slot straight after stand-up
for…the team's own pull requests
then we expect…faster flow from code-complete to merge
We'll know when…median PR wait time drops
Baseline → Target26 hours → 18 hours, measured from repository analytics weekly
GuardrailEscaped defects do not increase
Owner · TimeboxTech lead · 2 sprints · review at the sprint 2 retro

5Facilitation tips

Small beats perfectA small experiment run this week teaches more than a perfect one that never starts.
One ownerThe owner makes sure it happens and gets reviewed. Supporters do the work with them.
Measure before you changeCapture the baseline before starting, or you can't show improvement.
Guardrails matterSpeeding something up by cutting quality isn't a gain. Always pick a metric that must not get worse.
Stopping is successA clean “stop” with a clear learning is a good result. Share it so others don't repeat it.
Make it visiblePut the canvas on the team wall or wiki next to the board, and review it at stand-up.
Common pitfalls: measures like “better quality” with no number; targets with no baseline; experiments that need another team's work; no review date; quietly dropping experiments instead of deciding to stop; running too many at once.

6After the session

Straight away

Add the experiment to the team backlog. Book the mid-point check and the review.

Mid-point

Check the early-signal indicator. If it's not started, find out why and remove the blocker.

At the review

Complete section 7 together. Decide persevere, pivot or stop, and record what you learned.

Share

Post completed canvases where other teams can see them, such as at the system demo or I&A.

7How it fits the toolkit

The canvas is the experiment step. Ideas come from the Marginal Gains Workshop, a health check or a retro. Results are reviewed in the next retrospective.

Purpose: Turn one small improvement idea into a testable experiment with a clear owner, measure and review date.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
