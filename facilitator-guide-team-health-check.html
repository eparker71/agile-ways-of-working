Team Health Check Guide

How to run a team health check, read the results, and turn them into improvement.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Inspired by the Spotify Squad Health Check · Team & Facilitator Tool

“How healthy is our team right now, and which way is it trending?”

The health check gives a team a structured, safe way to talk about how things really are across eleven dimensions, from delivering value to teamwork. It is a conversation tool for the team, not a management scorecard. Its value comes from discussing the votes and repeating the check every quarter so trends show up.

Duration45–60 min
GroupOne team
5–10 people
CadenceEvery quarter
or planning cycle
Output1–2 focus areas
& trend

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Green, amber and red dot stickers: one of each colour per person per dimension (or coloured cards for silent voting)
  • Bring the previous health check so you can set the trend arrows
  • Agree with the team who sees the results, and keep it within the team by default
  • Pre-read each dimension's healthy and unhealthy description, and adapt the wording if your team uses different language
  • Prepare a few copies of the 1% Experiment Canvas

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeActivityFacilitator notes
0–5Frame itExplain the purpose: an honest snapshot for the team, not a performance review. There are no wrong answers.
5–12Walk the dimensionsRead each dimension and its healthy / unhealthy examples. Clarify meaning, but don't debate it yet.
12–22VoteEach person places one dot per row: green, amber or red. For more anonymity, vote with cards first and then place the dots.
22–27Set the trendCompare with last time and circle ↑ → ↓ for each row. If this is the first check, leave it for next time.
27–47DiscussStart with the reddest rows and rows where votes are split. Ask “What makes this red for you?” and capture notes in the right column.
47–57Pick focus areasDot-vote or agree on 1–2 rows to improve. Start a 1% Experiment Canvas for each.
57–60CloseThank the team, confirm where results are kept, and set the date of the next check.

3Probing questions by dimension

Use these to deepen the conversation on any row that is red, amber or split.

1 · Delivering Value

  • How do we know customers value what we ship?
  • When did we last see a user use our work?

2 · Easy to Release

  • What's the scariest part of releasing?
  • How many manual steps are there?

3 · Fun

  • What gives us energy? What drains it?
  • When did we last celebrate something?

4 · Health of Codebase

  • Which part of the code do we avoid touching?
  • Is tech debt visible in the backlog?

5 · Learning

  • What did we learn this quarter?
  • Is learning planned or squeezed in?

6 · Mission

  • Could each of us explain why the team exists?
  • How does our work link to our quarterly objectives?

7 · Pawns or Players

  • Which decisions do we wish we could make?
  • Where do we feel we have no say?

8 · Speed

  • Where does work wait?
  • What blocks us most often?

9 · Suitable Process

  • Which ritual adds the least value?
  • What would we change if we could?

10 · Support

  • Who do we struggle to get help from?
  • How long does it take to get unblocked?

11 · Teamwork

  • Do we help each other or work in silos?
  • How do we handle disagreement?

4Facilitation tips

Safety firstIf people don't feel safe, the dots will all be green. Keep results in the team, and never compare teams' scores.
Split votes are goldA row with green and red votes tells you more than an all-red row. Explore why people see it differently.
Trends beat snapshotsA single check is a conversation starter. Three checks show whether things are actually changing.
Adapt the dimensionsSwap or reword dimensions that don't fit your context, but keep them stable over time so you can compare.
Don't fix everythingPick 1–2 areas. Improving one red row is worth more than vague intentions on five.
Escalate what's outsideSome reds (such as support or environments) need help from outside the team. Make them visible to leadership.
Common pitfalls: using results to rank or judge teams; managers voting or watching the vote; skipping the discussion and only collecting colours; changing the dimensions every time so trends are lost; no follow-through on the chosen focus areas.

5After the session

Within 24 hours

Photograph the board and store it with previous checks. Record the votes per row so you can track trends.

Focus areas

Turn each focus area into a 1% Experiment Canvas with an owner and review date.

Between checks

Revisit the focus areas at retros. Did the experiments help?

Next check

Repeat at the same point next quarter, using the same dimensions, and set the trend arrows.

6How it fits the toolkit

The health check is the diagnose step of the toolkit. Feed red or amber rows into a 1% Experiment Canvas. Where the cause sits outside the team, take it to a Marginal Gains Workshop.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Team Health Check”, a workshop board.

Purpose: How healthy is our team right now, and which way is it trending? Vote, discuss, then pick 1–2 areas to improve.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Green = Healthy, nothing to fix · Amber = Some issues to discuss · Red = Unhealthy, needs attention

Sections:
Rows, top to bottom (each is a full-width band; the first label in bold is the row heading):
1. DIMENSION: HEALTHY · SOME ISSUES · UNHEALTHY · TREND · NOTES & ACTIONS
2. Delivering Value: We deliver great stuff and customers are delighted. · We deliver poor work and feel uneasy shipping it.
3. Easy to Release: Releasing is simple, safe, painless and automated. · Releasing is risky, manual, painful and slow.
4. Fun: We love coming to work and have fun together. · Work feels like a grind.
5. Health of Codebase: Clean, readable code with great test coverage. · Technical debt is out of control.
6. Learning: We're learning interesting things all the time. · We never have time to learn anything.
7. Mission: We know why we're here and we're excited about it. · No clear purpose, big picture or focus.
8. Pawns or Players: We control our destiny: we decide what and how. · We're pawns with no influence on what we do.
9. Speed: We get things done quickly. No waiting, no delays. · We keep getting stuck, blocked or interrupted.
10. Suitable Process: Our way of working fits us perfectly. · Our way of working gets in our way.
11. Support: We get great help and support when we ask. · We get stuck because we can't get the help we need.
12. Teamwork: We're a gelled team with awesome collaboration. · We're individuals who don't really know each other.

Footer questions: “Which 1–2 areas would make the biggest difference if they got 1% better?”
