How to run a short daily check that moves the team toward its goal, instead of a round of status updates.

“What will we finish today?”

Most teams meet briefly every day, whether they call it the Daily Scrum, a stand-up or the Kanban meeting. Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the goal and adapt the plan for the next day. It often turns into a round of status reports to a manager. The board fixes that by putting the goal at the top, walking the board right to left so finishing comes before starting, focusing on blocked and ageing work, and moving detailed discussion to a parking lot. A weekly health check keeps the habit honest.

1Before the session

  • Hold it at the same time and place every day, in front of the team's board
  • Put the current goal where everyone can see it
  • Agree who facilitates; rotating the role works well
  • For remote teams, share the board on screen and keep cameras optional

2Agenda & run-sheet

MinutesStepFacilitator notes
0–11 · GoalRead the goal and the days left. Ask: are we on track?
1–103 · Walk the boardStart at the right: what's nearly done and what's needed to finish it? Then blocked and old items. Then what to finish today.
10–135 · Blockers & swarmsGive each blocker an owner. Decide whether to swarm on anything.
13–15Plan & parkConfirm what we'll finish today. Note parking-lot topics and who stays for them.
Weekly7 · Health checkTick the health check together and agree one thing to try if a check fails.

3Making it work

What good looks like for each part of the board.

1 · Our Goal

  • Visible every day
  • Discussion links back to it

3 · Walk the Board

  • Right to left
  • Oldest and blocked first
  • Finish before starting

4 · Questions to Ask

  • About the work, not people
  • The classic three are optional

5 · Blockers & Swarms

  • Every blocker has an owner
  • Swarm when the goal is at risk

6 · Parking Lot

  • Details after the sync
  • Only the people needed stay

7 · Health Check

  • Weekly
  • One change when a check fails

4Facilitation tips

Talk about work items, not peopleWalking the board item by item avoids a round of personal status reports.
Right to leftFinishing work delivers value; starting it only adds to WIP.
Keep it to 15 minutesIf it overruns, the parking lot isn't being used. Move discussions after the sync.
It's the team's meetingManagers and stakeholders can listen, but the team runs it and decides the plan.
Rotate the facilitatorTaking turns stops it becoming one person's update meeting.
Look for the oldest itemWork that's been open longest is usually the biggest risk to the goal.
Common pitfalls: a status report to a manager; no reference to the goal; walking people instead of work; problem-solving in the meeting; overrunning; starting new work while items sit blocked; skipping it when “busy”.

5After the session

Straight away

Parking-lot groups meet; blocker owners act.

During the day

Update the board as work moves, not just before the next sync.

Weekly

Run the health check and adjust the format.

At the retro

Ask whether the sync is helping the team reach its goal.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Daily Sync”, a workshop board.

Purpose: A short daily check on progress toward the goal: walk the board, raise blockers, and plan the day together.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Team, Time & Place

Sections:
1. Our Goal: top left, about 65% wide and 15% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Every conversation points here
   Content: We're working toward · Days left: · Sprint goal, iteration goal, or the outcome the team is focused on this week
2. Ground Rules: top right, about 35% wide and 15% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Content: 15 minutes, same time, same place · For the team, not a status report · Details go to the parking lot
3. Walk the Board: middle left, about 65% wide and 45% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Right to left · finish before you start
   Content: 1. What's nearly done, and what's needed to finish it? · 2. What's blocked or getting old? Who can help? · 3. What will we finish today? · 4. Only then: what should we start? · Walk this way: · Ready: · In progress: · Review / test: · Done: · Work items (×4)
4. Questions to Ask: middle right, about 35% wide and 45% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Subtitle: About the work, not the people
   Content: For each item on the board · What's needed to finish this? · Is anything blocking it? · Who can help, today? · Is it older than our SLE? · The classic three questions · What did I do yesterday toward the goal? · What will I do today toward the goal? · Do I see anything in the way? · Useful, but easily becomes a status report. · The Scrum Guide no longer requires them.
5. Blockers & Swarms: bottom left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Act today
   Content: Item: · Blocked by: · Swarm? · Who:
6. Parking Lot: bottom centre, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: After the sync
   Content: Topics that need more than a minute · Note who stays to discuss
7. Sync Health Check: bottom right, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Once a week
   Content: Finished within 15 minutes · Talked about the goal, not just activity · Oldest and blocked items discussed first · Every blocker has an owner · We know what we'll finish today · Everyone could take part · If a check fails, we'll try:

Footer questions: “What will we finish today?” “Who needs help, and who can give it?”
