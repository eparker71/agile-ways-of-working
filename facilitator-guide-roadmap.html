Now · Next · Later Roadmap Guide

How to build a roadmap that ties work to goals and shows certainty honestly, without promising dates you can't keep.

“Which goal does this serve, and how will we know it worked?”

A date-driven roadmap promises things nobody can know yet. The Now / Next / Later roadmap, popularised by Janna Bastow of ProdPad, swaps dates for horizons of certainty: Now is committed and in progress, Next is shaped and ready to start, and Later holds problems worth exploring, not promises. Every item sits in a lane for one of up to three goals, so the roadmap explains why as well as what. The board adds what you're not doing, the assumptions to test before an item moves left, and how often you'll review it.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Bring the product vision and current goals or OKRs; agree no more than three goals
  • List candidate items on stickies beforehand: work in progress, ideas, requests
  • Invite someone who can say no: the product owner or sponsor

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–151 · GoalsAgree up to three goals and a measure for each. If an item fits no goal, question it.
15–302 · NowPlace work already in progress. If Now is overflowing, that's the first conversation.
30–453 · NextAdd items that are shaped and ready. Keep it to what the team could start soon.
45–554 · LaterAdd problems and opportunities, not solutions. Write them as questions where you can.

70–807 · Review & ShareAgree how often to review it, when an item moves to Now, and who sees it.

3Using each horizon well

Certainty falls from left to right.

Goals

  • Up to three
  • Each with a measure

Now

  • Committed, in progress
  • Small enough to finish

Next

  • Shaped, not started
  • Could change

Later

  • Problems, not features
  • No dates

Not Doing

  • Said out loud
  • With a reason

4Facilitation tips

Problems on the rightLater items written as problems keep options open: “Shoppers abandon at delivery slots”, not “Build slot picker”.
Dates only in NowIf someone needs a date, forecast the Now items with the Release Forecast, not the whole roadmap.
Not Doing builds trustStakeholders accept a no far more easily when they can see it and the reason.
Move items both waysAn item can move back from Next to Later when you learn it's harder or less valuable.
Show it, don't send itWalk stakeholders through it; the conversation matters more than the picture.
Keep it freshA roadmap reviewed every month or quarter is a plan; one never reviewed is a wish list.
Common pitfalls: a Gantt chart with the dates rubbed off; Later used as a promise; too many goals; items with no goal; Now overflowing; never reviewing it; treating it as a commitment to every stakeholder.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the board or move it to your digital tool, and share it with a short walk-through.

Each iteration

Plan from the Now column; refine items in Next.

Monthly or quarterly

Review: move items, retire goals that are met, check the assumptions.

When asked for dates

Use the Release Forecast for Now items, and explain the horizons.

6How it fits the toolkit

The roadmap sits between strategy and delivery. Start from the Product Vision Board and Objectives & Key Results for the goals, use the Opportunity Solution Tree to explore Later, the Prioritisation Board to choose what moves left (its small Now / Next / Later panel is a quick version of this board), and the Release Forecast when someone needs a date.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Now · Next · Later Roadmap”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Show what you're working on, what's coming and what you're exploring, tied to goals, without promising dates you can't keep.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Product, Owner, Updated

Sections:
1. Goals: top left, about 15% wide and 60% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Subtitle: Why
   Content: Goal a: · Measure: (×3) · Goal b: · Goal c:
2. Now: top left, about 25% wide and 60% tall.
   Subtitle: Committed · high certainty
   Content: Being built for this goal (×3)
3. Next: top centre, about 25% wide and 60% tall.
   Subtitle: Shaped · medium certainty
   Content: Shaped and ready to start (×3)
4. Later: top right, about 25% wide and 60% tall.
   Subtitle: Exploring · low certainty
   Content: Problems worth exploring (×3)
5. Not Doing: bottom left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: And why · saying no out loud
   Content: Item: · Why not (for now):
6. Assumptions to Test: bottom centre, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Before it moves left
   Content: We believe…: · We'll test it by:
7. Review & Share: bottom right, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: A living plan
   Content: We review this every: · Next review: · An item moves to Now when: · Shared with: · Every item links to a goal · No dates beyond Now

Also on the board: CERTAINTY FALLS FROM LEFT TO RIGHT · LATER HOLDS PROBLEMS, NOT PROMISES

Footer questions: “Which goal does this serve, and how will we know it worked?” “What would have to be true for this to move from Later to Next?”
