“Are we treating a complex problem as if it were only complicated?”

Many failures come from using the wrong kind of approach: detailed plans for problems nobody can predict, or experiments where a known answer exists. The Cynefin® framework, created by Dave Snowden, helps a group decide what kind of situation they're in. In clear situations the answer is known; follow the practice. In complicated ones, experts can analyse their way to a good answer. In complex ones, cause and effect only make sense afterwards, so run safe-to-fail experiments. In chaotic ones, act first to stabilise. In the centre, confusion, you don't yet know which applies. This board is a simple, practical introduction; the full framework goes much further. 1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Ask everyone to bring current situations, problems or pieces of work, one per sticky
  • Prepare one example for each domain from your own context
  • Agree beforehand that the aim is a fitting approach, not a perfect classification

3Approach by domain

Match the approach to the situation.

Clear

  • Sense, categorise, respond
  • Follow the practice

Complicated

  • Sense, analyse, respond
  • Ask the experts

Complex

  • Probe, sense, respond
  • Safe-to-fail experiments

Chaotic

  • Act, sense, respond
  • Stabilise first

Confused

  • Not sure which
  • Break it into parts

4Facilitation tips

Discuss the disagreementsWhen people place the same sticky in different domains, the conversation is the value.
Most product work is complexNew products, markets and team changes rarely behave predictably. Experiment.
Several experiments at onceIn complex situations, run a few small, safe-to-fail probes in parallel.
Beware the cliffClear situations can tip into chaos when people stop paying attention.
Situations moveAs you learn, complex problems can become complicated. Revisit the board.
It's not a 2×2The domains aren't a grid to score; use them to choose how to act.
Common pitfalls: treating everything as complicated and writing a detailed plan; forcing every sticky into a domain; turning it into a classification argument; experiments that aren't safe to fail; never revisiting.

5After the session

Straight away

Start the safe-to-fail experiments and share the board.

At the revisit date

Check the signals, amplify what's working, dampen what isn't.

Next planning

Use the domains to decide how much to plan up front for each piece of work.

Go deeper

The Cynefin Company's own materials cover the full framework.
