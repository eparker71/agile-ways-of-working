“Why does this keep happening, and what in our system lets it?”

When the same problem keeps coming back, fixing the symptom isn't enough. The board combines two classic techniques. The fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram explores possible causes across six categories, so the group looks wide before going deep. 5 Whys, from the Toyota Production System, then follows the most likely branch down to a cause you can fix. A quick check makes sure it really is the root cause, and the countermeasures are treated as experiments.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Collect facts about the problem: when, where, how often, and its impact
  • Invite the people who experience the problem, not just those who manage it
  • Yellow stickies and markers
  • Agree up front that this is about the system, not about blaming a person

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–101 · The ProblemWrite a factual problem statement. Agree when, where and how often it happens, and its impact.
10–352 · FishboneBrainstorm possible causes onto the six bones. Aim for breadth: every category gets at least one sticky.
35–40Pick a branchDot-vote the most likely cause on the fishbone. That's the starting point for the Whys.
40–553 · 5 WhysAsk “why?” of that cause, writing each answer. Stop when you reach something you can change.
55–654 · Check the CauseRun the checklist. Any “no”? Ask why again, or try another branch.
65–805 · CountermeasuresAgree small, testable fixes with owners and dates.
80–906 · Did It Work?Agree what you'll measure, the baseline, and when you'll check.

3Using each part well

What good looks like on each part of the board.

1 · The Problem

  • Facts, not opinions
  • No names or blame
  • Impact is clear

2 · Fishbone

  • Every bone considered
  • Causes, not solutions

3 · 5 Whys

  • Each answer is evidence-based
  • Stop at something you can change
  • It may take 3 or 7 whys

4 · Check the Cause

  • All boxes ticked
  • Would explain the problem fully

5 · Countermeasures

  • Small and testable
  • Owned and dated

6 · Did It Work?

  • Measured against a baseline
  • Checked on the date

4Facilitation tips

Wide, then deepFill the fishbone before asking why, so you don't chase the first idea that comes up.
System, not personIf a “why” lands on a person, ask why the system allowed it. People act sensibly in their context.
Five is a guideStop when you reach a cause you can change. Sometimes that's three whys, sometimes seven.
Evidence beats opinionCheck each answer against data where you can. Guesses lead to the wrong fix.
Several roots are normalComplex problems often have more than one cause. Pick the one with the most leverage.
Fix as an experimentTreat countermeasures like the 1% Experiment Canvas: measure, then persevere or pivot.
Common pitfalls: blaming a person; jumping to a solution in the first five minutes; following only one branch; stopping at a symptom; answers based on opinion; fixing without measuring whether it worked.

5After the session

Straight away

Add the countermeasures to the backlog and share the board with those affected.

Next retro

Report progress on the countermeasures.

On the check date

Measure against the baseline and fill in Did It Work?

If it didn't work

Go back to the fishbone and try the next most likely branch.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Root Cause Analysis”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Dig beneath a recurring problem with a fishbone and 5 Whys, check the cause, and test a fix.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Team, Date

Sections:
1. The Problem: top full width, about 100% wide and 15% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Facts, not blame
   Content: What happened: · When, where, how often: · Impact:
2. Fishbone: middle left, about 65% wide and 50% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Stickies on the bone they belong to
   Content: People & Skills · Process · Tools & Tech · Problem: · Environment · Information · Requirements
3. 5 Whys: middle right, about 35% wide and 50% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Ask why until you reach a cause you can fix
   Content: Why 1? · ↓ (×4) · Why 2? · Why 3? · Why 4? · Why 5? · Root cause:
4. Check the Cause: bottom left, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Before you fix it
   Content: If we fixed this, would the problem stop? · Do we have evidence, not just opinion? · Is it about the system, not a person? · Is it within our control or influence? · Would it explain other problems too? · Any “no”? Ask why again, or test another branch.
5. Countermeasures: bottom centre, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Small, testable fixes
   Content: Action: · Owner: · By when:
6. Did It Work?: bottom right, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: We'll measure: · Baseline: · Check on: · Result: · Fixed · Better · No change

Footer questions: “Why does this keep happening, and what in our system lets it?” “If we fixed the cause, what else would get better?”
