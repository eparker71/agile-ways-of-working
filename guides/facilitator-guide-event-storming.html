Event Storming Guide

How to map a business domain as a timeline of events with everyone who knows it, and find gaps, hotspots and boundaries.

“What happens in this domain, in the order it really happens?”

Event Storming, created by Alberto Brandolini, gets business experts and engineers to build a shared picture of how a domain really works. Everyone writes domain events (things that happened, in the past tense) on orange stickies and places them on a timeline. The group then sorts them, marks pivotal events where the story changes phase, adds commands, actors, policies, systems and read models, and flags hotspots: questions, risks and disagreements. It works at three levels: big picture (the whole business flow), process (one process in detail) and design (enough to shape software and its boundaries).

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 as a guide, then use a long wall of paper for the real timeline; or use the SVG digitally
  • Buy stickies in the eight colours from the key (orange most of all) and thick markers
  • Invite people who know the domain from different angles: business, support, engineering, operations
  • Remove chairs and tables from the wall; people need to move
  • Pick the scope: big picture, or one process in detail

2Agenda & run-sheet

70–100Add the restCommands, actors, policies, systems, external systems and read models around the events.

120–1404 · Boundaries · 5 · NextLook for natural clusters; agree where to zoom in and who to invite next.

3The sticky colours

The key is the grammar of the workshop.

Domain events

  • Past tense
  • The backbone

Commands & actors

  • Who asks for what
  • Causes an event

Policies

  • Whenever X, then Y
  • Often automation

Systems & read models

  • What records it
  • What people need to see

Hotspots

  • Questions, risks
  • Disagreements

4Facilitation tips

Events firstHold back commands and systems until the event timeline makes sense; it keeps the energy up.
Past tense only“Order Placed”, not “Place Order”. It keeps the focus on what really happens.
Disagreement is goldMark it with a hotspot and move on. Those spots are where the real learning is.
Unlimited modelling spaceA long wall beats a crowded table. Give the timeline more room than you think.
Facilitate lightlyLet the group self-organise; step in to keep events orange and the timeline flowing.
Boundaries emergeClusters of events and language often show where team or service boundaries belong.
Common pitfalls: too small a space; only engineers in the room; jumping to software design too early; arguing instead of marking hotspots; one person writing for everyone; photos taken but nothing followed up.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the whole wall in sections and share it.

Within a week

Work through the hotspots with their owners.

Zoom in

Run a process or design-level session where the boundaries or hotspots matter most.

Feed design

Use the boundaries in Team Topology Design and record big decisions in an ADR.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Event Storming”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Map a business domain as a timeline of events with everyone who knows it, to find shared understanding, gaps and boundaries.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Domain, Date

Sections:
1. Sticky Key: top full width, about 100% wide and 20% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: One colour per kind of sticky
   Content: Domain event: Something that happened, in the past tense · Command: A request or decision that causes an event · Actor: The person or role who issues a command · Policy: “Whenever this happens, we do that” · System: What accepts a command and records the event · External system: Something outside we depend on · Read model: Information someone needs to decide · Hotspot: A question, risk, gap or disagreement
2. The Timeline: middle full width, about 100% wide and 55% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: Events first · then order · then the rest
   Content: Phase: (×4) · Time: · Orange events left to right in time, other colours around them · Dashed lines mark pivotal events, where the story changes phase
3. Hotspots: bottom left, about 35% wide and 20% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: To resolve, not ignore
   Content: Question or problem: · Owner:
4. Boundaries We Found: bottom centre, about 35% wide and 20% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Candidate contexts
   Content: Area: · Key events: · Team:
5. Next Steps: bottom right, about 35% wide and 20% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Zoom in where it matters
   Content: Zoom into (process or design): · Who to invite next: · Next session:

Footer questions: “What happens in this domain, in the order it really happens?” “Where do people disagree about what happens next?”
