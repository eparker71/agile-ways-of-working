Skills Matrix Guide

How to map the team's skills, find single points of failure, and plan learning so the team can swarm.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Manage Flow · Team Capability · T-Shaped Skills

“If one person was away for a month, what would stop?”

Swarming and steady flow need more than one person who can do each kind of work. A skills matrix maps the team's skills on a simple 0–3 scale, from no experience to able to teach. It quickly shows single points of failure and skills nobody can teach, and it turns what people want to learn into a learning plan. It's a team tool for growth, not a performance assessment.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Agree up front that the matrix is for team learning, not for managers to rate people
  • Draft a list of 8–10 skills the team's work needs: technical, domain and practice skills
  • Markers; sticky notes for the wants-to-learn column

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10PurposeExplain why: to spread skills so the team can swarm and isn't stuck when someone is away.
10–25SkillsAgree the skills that matter for the team's work. Write one per column. Keep to ten.
25–451 · Self-assessEach person scores themselves 0–3 for each skill, then the team discusses any surprises.
45–55Wants to learnEach person adds what they'd like to learn.
55–702 · Single Points of FailureList skills with only one person at 2 or 3, and skills with nobody at 3.
70–853 · Learning Plan · 4 · Ready to Swarm?Pair learners with teachers, set dates, and check the readiness list.

3Reading the matrix

Patterns to look for once it's filled in.

One person at 2+

  • A single point of failure
  • Top of the learning plan

Nobody at 3

  • No one to teach it
  • Consider outside help or training

Lots of 0s in a row

  • A specialist; check they're not overloaded
  • Pair them with learners

Wants to learn

  • Match with teachers
  • Plan time for it

4 · Ready to Swarm?

  • Two or more people per key skill
  • Pairing is planned

4Facilitation tips

Self-assess firstPeople score themselves, then discuss. It builds trust and surfaces modesty or over-confidence.
Learning, not ratingNever use the matrix for performance reviews. If you do, the scores stop being honest.
Aim for T-shapesDeep skill in one area plus working skill in others lets people help where the work is.
Pair to teachPairing on real work spreads skills faster than courses.
Make timePlan learning into the iteration, or the matrix won't change.
Revisit itUpdate every quarter and celebrate the levels that went up.
Common pitfalls: managers filling it in about people; using it in performance reviews; too many skills; no time for learning; experts never given time to teach; filling it in once and forgetting it.

5After the session

Straight away

Keep the matrix where the team can see it. Start the first learning pairs.

Each iteration

Plan pairing time. Note when a learner helps in a swarm.

Each retro

Ask: did work wait for one person? Update the learning plan.

Quarterly

Re-score together and compare. Single points of failure should be falling.

6How it fits the toolkit

The flow pack works as a set. Kanban Board Design makes the workflow visible and limits WIP. Ageing WIP & SLE shows which items are at risk and sets the swarm triggers used in Buffering & Swarming. Blocker Clustering finds why work gets stuck, and the Skills Matrix shows who can swarm. Bring the results to retrospectives and the Team Health Check.
