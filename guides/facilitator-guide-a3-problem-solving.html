A3 Problem-Solving Guide

How to work through one stubborn problem on a single sheet, from the facts to a fix that sticks.

“What's really happening, and have we gone to see it?”

An A3 is a way of solving a problem on one sheet of A3 paper. It comes from Toyota, where managers use it to coach people through a problem, and is described in John Shook's Managing to Learn. The left half is Plan: the background, the current condition (with facts, not opinions), the goal, and the root cause. The right half is Do, Check and Act: countermeasures that tackle the cause, a plan with owners and dates, a check of whether it worked, and the follow-up that makes it stick. One sheet forces clear thinking, and makes the reasoning easy for others to follow and challenge. Use it for problems that keep coming back; for a quick fishbone and 5 Whys, the Root Cause Analysis board is lighter.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or share it on screen
  • Pick one problem and one owner; the owner keeps the A3 and updates it
  • Gather facts first: data, examples, and time spent watching the work where it happens
  • Invite the people who do the work; they know where the problem really is

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10BackgroundWhy does this problem matter, and to whom? Keep it to three lines.
10–30Current conditionDraw or chart what's actually happening, from facts. Mark where it goes wrong.
30–40GoalAgree a measurable target and a date. What would good look like?
40–60Root causeAsk why until you reach a cause you can fix. Check it explains the facts.
60–75Countermeasures & planList small changes that tackle the cause, then who does what, by when.
LaterCheck & follow-upAt the agreed date, compare the result with the goal; make it standard, or try again.

4Facilitation tips

Facts before fixesMost A3s go wrong by jumping to a solution. Spend real time on the current condition.
Go and seeWatch the work where it happens. Reports and opinions hide what's really going on.
Countermeasures, not solutionsCall them countermeasures: they're experiments, and might not work first time.
One ownerOne person owns the A3 and keeps it current, but they don't solve it alone.
Coach with questionsA leader's job is to ask good questions about the A3, not to write the answer.
Keep it to one pageIf it doesn't fit, the thinking isn't clear enough yet. Cut, don't shrink the font.
Common pitfalls: filling in the boxes after the decision is made; skipping the current condition; a goal with no number; a root cause that blames a person; countermeasures nobody owns; never coming back to check.

5After the session

This week

Start the first countermeasures, and update the plan as you go.

At the check date

Compare the result with the goal, honestly, and record what you learned.

If it worked

Make it the standard way of working, and share the A3 with teams who have the same problem.

If it didn't

Go back to the root cause: was it the real one? Try another countermeasure.

Purpose: Work through one problem on one sheet: understand it, find the root cause, test countermeasures and make the fix stick.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
