Buffering & Swarming Guide

How to size a capacity buffer from real data, agree swarm triggers, and make swarming a team habit.

“What would we stop starting so we could finish this?”

Two habits keep work flowing when reality interrupts the plan. Buffering means deliberately leaving part of the team's capacity unplanned, sized from how much unplanned work actually arrives, so surprises don't wreck the plan. Swarming means that when an item is blocked, ageing or critical, people stop starting new work and gather on it until it's done. The board helps the team size the buffer, agree its rules, set clear triggers, agree how a swarm works, and learn from each one.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Pull data for the last five iterations: planned capacity and unplanned work (items, points or hours)
  • If you have them, bring cycle time percentiles from the Ageing WIP board
  • Bring the Skills Matrix if the team has one: it shows who can help in a swarm
  • Agree with the PO in advance that a buffer is a planning tool, not idle time

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the board.

1 · Unplanned Work History

  • Based on data, not memory
  • Types of unplanned work noted

2 · Size the Buffer

  • Starts from the real average
  • Clear what it's for

3 · Buffer Rules

  • Unused buffer goes to improvement or the next item
  • Running out triggers a conversation

4 · Swarm Triggers

  • Specific thresholds
  • Linked to the SLE
  • Few enough to remember

5 · How We Swarm

  • Anyone can call a swarm
  • Just enough people join
  • Finishing beats starting

6 · Swarm Log

  • Every swarm recorded
  • Learning captured

7 · Iteration Check

  • Buffer adjusted from use
  • Did swarms help?

4Facilitation tips

Buffers aren't slackA buffer is planned capacity for work you know will come. Unused, it goes to improvement or the next item.
Size from dataIf 20% of the team's time goes on unplanned work, planning for 100% guarantees a missed plan.
Swarm on the oldestWhen in doubt, swarm on the item that has been open longest, not the newest.
Stop starting firstThe first step of a swarm is to not pick up anything new. That alone frees people up.
Not everyone, every timeA swarm needs enough people to finish the item, not the whole team crowded round.
Ask why it needed a swarmFrequent swarms point to a cause: big items, missing skills, or blockers. Use Blocker Clustering.
Common pitfalls: a buffer that managers fill with extra features; triggers so vague nobody acts; swarming on everything; the same expert always rescuing; never logging swarms, so nothing is learned; swarming without stopping new work.

5After the session

Straight away

Put the triggers and swarm steps on the team board where everyone can see them.

Every day

At the daily sync, check: has anything hit a trigger? Call the swarm then.

Every retro

Run the iteration check: buffer used, swarms, did they help? Adjust the buffer.

Over time

If the same skill always needs the same person, update the Skills Matrix and learning plan.

6How it fits the toolkit

The flow pack works as a set. Kanban Board Design makes the workflow visible and limits WIP. Ageing WIP & SLE shows which items are at risk and sets the swarm triggers used in Buffering & Swarming. Blocker Clustering finds why work gets stuck, and the Skills Matrix shows who can swarm. Bring the results to retrospectives and the Team Health Check.
