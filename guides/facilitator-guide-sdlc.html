“Where in our lifecycle does work wait longest?”

The software development lifecycle (SDLC) describes how software goes from an idea to something people use. Traditional SDLCs, such as waterfall, run each stage once, in order, with an approval gate between stages. Agile delivery keeps the same stages but runs the whole loop many times, for small slices of value, with feedback at every turn. The poster shows eight stages, from idea to learn, with what happens in each, its key question, the tools in this kit that help, and the traditional gate it replaces. It's useful for explaining agile delivery to people used to stage gates, such as a PMO, governance or audit, and for helping a team see the whole picture.

1Before the session

  • Print the poster at A3 or 11×17, or share it on screen
  • Bring your organisation's current lifecycle or governance process, if it has one
  • List the approvals a typical change needs today, and how long each takes
  • Invite people from governance, risk or the PMO if the conversation is about gates

3The eight stages

In agile delivery the loop runs for every slice, not once per project.

Idea & Discover

  • Is it worth it?
  • What problem, for whom?

Design & Build

  • Smallest valuable slice
  • Main always releasable

Test & Release

  • Quality built in
  • Release safely, undo safely

Operate & Learn

  • Know when it breaks
  • Did it make a difference?

Governance

  • Keep the questions
  • Answer them with evidence

4Facilitation tips

Same stages, different rhythmAgile doesn't skip design or testing; it does a little of each, continuously.
Start with the questionsEvery gate exists to answer a question. Keep the question; change how it's answered.
Bring governance inRisk, audit and PMO colleagues are partners. Involve them early, not at the end.
Look for waitingThe biggest delays are usually between stages, not within them.
Use it to navigateEach stage points to tools in this kit; use the poster as a map to the rest of the toolkit.
Not a new processThe poster describes stages, not a method to follow. Fit it to how your organisation works.
Common pitfalls: presenting agile as having no governance; replacing one set of document gates with another; treating the stages as phases to finish one at a time; leaving operations and learning out; using it to argue rather than to understand.

5After the session

Straight away

Try the chosen tool at the weakest stage.

Within a month

Meet governance colleagues to rethink one gate.

Quarterly

Onboarding

Use the poster to show new joiners how work flows and where tools fit.

6How it fits the toolkit

The delivery engineering tools work as a set. SDLC at a Glance shows where each stage of the lifecycle fits. The Delivery Pipeline Canvas maps how changes reach production, the Test Strategy decides how quality is built in, and Release Readiness & Strategy plans each release. The Continuous Delivery Health Check tracks the practices over time, and Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt measures the results. For the wider flow from idea to customer, use the Value Stream Map.

Sections:
1. Idea: top left, about 10% wide and 55% tall.
   Content: What happens: · A problem or opportunity is raised and weighed against other work. · Key question: · “Is this worth exploring?” · Tools in this kit: · Product Vision Board · Prioritisation Board · Portfolio Kanban · Traditional gate: · Business case approved
2. Discover: top left, about 10% wide and 55% tall; header colour blue.
   Content: What happens: · Understand users, the problem and the options before committing. · Key question: · “What problem, for whom?” · Tools in this kit: · Persona & Journey Map · Opportunity Solution Tree · Impact Map · Traditional gate: · Requirements signed off
3. Design: top left, about 10% wide and 55% tall; header colour sky blue.
   Content: What happens: · Shape the solution into slices and stories, with examples and key technical decisions. · Key question: · “What's the smallest valuable slice?” · Tools in this kit: · User Story Map · Epic Breakdown Canvas · Example Mapping · Architecture Decision Record · Traditional gate: · Design approved
4. Build: top centre, about 10% wide and 55% tall; header colour amber.
   Content: What happens: · Write code and tests together, integrate continuously and keep work flowing. · Key question: · “Is main always releasable?” · Tools in this kit: · Sprint Planning · Pairing & Mobbing · Kanban Board Design · Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Traditional gate: · Code complete
5. Test: top centre, about 10% wide and 55% tall.
   Content: What happens: · Automated checks on every change, plus exploratory testing for what automation can't see. · Key question: · “How do we know it works?” · Tools in this kit: · Test Strategy · Example Mapping · Definition of Done (Team Charter) · Traditional gate: · Test sign-off (UAT)
6. Release: top right, about 10% wide and 55% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: What happens: · Deploy safely, then switch on for users at the right pace, with a way back. · Key question: · “Can we release, and undo it, safely?” · Tools in this kit: · Release Readiness & Strategy · Release Forecast & Burn-up · Traditional gate: · Go-live approval (change board)
7. Operate: top right, about 10% wide and 55% tall; header colour red.
   Content: What happens: · Monitor, support users and recover quickly when something goes wrong. · Key question: · “Would we know if it broke?” · Tools in this kit: · Blameless Incident Review · Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Traditional gate: · Handover to operations
8. Learn: top right, about 10% wide and 55% tall; header colour dark green.
   Content: What happens: · Measure the outcome, review what happened and feed the learning back in. · Key question: · “Did it make the difference we hoped?” · Tools in this kit: · Sprint Review · Value Metrics · Retrospective Kit · Traditional gate: · Post-implementation review
9. Traditional SDLC and Agile Delivery: bottom left, about 65% wide and 40% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Same stages, different rhythm
   Content: Traditional (stage-gate): · Agile delivery: · Planning · Detailed plan up front; change is controlled · Plan a little, often; change is expected · Requirements · Complete and signed off before building · Discovered and refined just in time, with examples · Testing · A phase near the end · On every change, automated, from the start · Release · Rare, large and risky · Frequent, small and routine · Teams · Hand-offs between specialist groups · Cross-functional teams own the whole slice · Feedback · From users, after launch · From users, every iteration · Risk · Found late, when change is costly · Found early, while change is cheap · Progress · Phases and documents completed · Working software and outcomes · Governance · Document gates between phases · Evidence from working software, every iteration
10. Keeping Governance, Losing the Delay: bottom right, about 35% wide and 40% tall; header colour purple.
   Content: Keep the questions each gate asks, and answer them with evidence every iteration. · Put compliance checks in the Definition of Done, not a final phase. · Let the pipeline produce audit evidence: · tests run, approvals, who deployed what. · Review working software, not documents about software. · Match approval to risk: small, routine changes go straight through. · Invite risk, audit and security partners to reviews early, not just at the end.
