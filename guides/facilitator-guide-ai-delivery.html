“Who's accountable when an agent gets it wrong?”

Most organisations now use AI somewhere in delivery, but far fewer have agreed how. The 2025 State of Agile report found AI use rising to 84% of respondents while only 49% had guardrails in place. This board is for leaders, engineering managers and coaches setting that direction across teams. Map where AI fits at each stage of delivery and at which autonomy level (it assists, it acts and a person approves, or it acts alone within limits), agree the guardrails and risks, name who's accountable, and check whether it's actually helping with outcome measures rather than usage counts. Teams then agree their own day-to-day rules with the AI Working Agreement.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Collect how teams use AI today: tools, stages, any incidents or near misses
  • Invite security, legal or data protection, and people who do the work, not only managers
  • Bring current delivery measures (lead time, change failure rate) as a baseline
  • Read your organisation's existing policies so the board builds on them

3Three autonomy levels

Move up one level at a time, with evidence.

Assists

  • AI suggests
  • A person decides and does

Acts, we approve

  • AI does the work
  • A person reviews first

Acts alone

  • Within set limits
  • Audited, can be stopped

Guardrails

  • Data, review, access
  • Visible and reversible

Accountability

  • Named people
  • Never the tool

4Facilitation tips

Start at AssistsEarn each step up in autonomy with evidence from a smaller, reversible use.
Same bar for AI workAI-written code and documents go through the same review, tests and scans as anyone's.
Least accessAn agent should have only the permissions its task needs, and a way to be switched off.
Outcomes, not usageLots of AI use isn't the goal. Watch lead time, quality, rework and how the team feels.
Make it visibleLabel AI-assisted changes so you can learn where it helps and where it hurts.
Review oftenTools and risks change monthly. Revisit the board at least every quarter.
Common pitfalls: banning AI so people use it out of sight; adopting it everywhere at once; measuring licences or prompts instead of outcomes; agents with broad access; no named owner; a policy written once and never reviewed.

5After the session

Within a week

Publish the agreed levels, guardrails and owners where every team can see them.

Each team

Agree day-to-day rules with the AI Working Agreement.

Monthly

Check the measures and the experiments; move one stage up or down if the evidence says so.

Quarterly

Review the whole board with the same group.

Purpose: Decide where AI and AI agents fit in how you deliver, the guardrails for each, who's accountable, and whether it's helping.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Scope, Owner, Review Date

Sections:
1. Where AI Fits: top left, about 65% wide and 35% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Tick one autonomy level per stage
   Content: Stage: · Where we use it: · Assists: · Acts, we approve: · Acts alone: · Human check: · Discover & research · Plan & refine · Build · Test · Release & deploy · Operate & support
2. Autonomy Levels: top right, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Move up only with evidence
   Content: 1 · Assists · AI suggests; a person decides and does the work. · Lowest risk. Start here. · 2 · Acts, we approve · AI does the work; a person reviews it before it counts. · 3 · Acts alone · AI acts within set limits; people check afterwards and can stop it.
3. Guardrails: middle left, about 35% wide and 35% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Agreed before we scale
   Content: No confidential or personal data in unapproved tools · AI output is reviewed like any other change · Agents get the least access they need · Generated code passes the same tests and scans · AI use is visible in commits and tickets · Licences and ownership of outputs checked · A person can stop or roll back any agent

   Subtitle: Likelihood × impact, 1–3
   Content: Risk: · L × I: · What we'll do:

   Subtitle: People, not tools
   Content: Owns the AI policy: · Approves new tools: · Approves agent access: · Reviews AI incidents: · Trains and supports people: · Questions go to:

   Subtitle: Outcomes, not usage
   Content: Measure: · Before: · Now: · Target: · Lead time for changes · Change failure rate · Rework and escaped defects · Team experience (survey)
7. Experiments & Review: bottom right, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Small, visible, reversible
   Content: Next experiment: · Owner: · Review on: · We review this whole board every:
