Design Sprint Guide

How to run a five-day design sprint: map the problem, sketch, decide, build a realistic prototype and test it with customers.

“What question, if answered on Friday, would make this week worth it?”

A design sprint answers a big product question in five days instead of months. Developed by Jake Knapp at Google Ventures and described in Sprint (2016), it takes a small team through Map (Monday), Sketch (Tuesday), Decide (Wednesday), Prototype (Thursday) and Test (Friday) with five real customers. A named Decider makes the hard calls, so the week ends with evidence instead of opinions. Many teams now run a compressed four-day version; the board works for both.

DurationFour or five full days
GroupUp to seven, including
a Decider and a facilitator
Use whenA big, risky question
with no clear answer
OutputA tested prototype
and a decision

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 and keep it on the wall all week, or use the SVG on a digital whiteboard
  • Agree the challenge and the Decider at least a week before
  • Clear everyone's diaries for the whole week; devices away during sessions
  • Start recruiting five target customers for Friday before the sprint begins
  • Book a room with lots of wall space, and line up experts to interview on Monday

2Agenda & run-sheet

DaySectionFacilitator notes
Before1 · Challenge · 2 · TeamWrite the challenge, name the Decider and the roles, and book the customers.
Monday3 · MapSet the long-term goal and sprint questions, map the journey, interview experts, pick a target.
Tuesday4 · SketchLightning demos for inspiration, then everyone sketches a solution alone.
Wednesday5 · DecideSilent gallery, heat-map dots, straw poll; the Decider chooses. Storyboard the prototype.
Thursday6 · PrototypeBuild only the façade customers will see. Trial run and finish the interview script.
Friday7 · Test · 8 · Results · 9 · NextFive interviews while the team watches. Mark the grid, find patterns, decide.

3What each day produces

Each day's output feeds the next.

Map

  • Goal and questions
  • A target on the map

Sketch

  • Individual solutions
  • Five customers booked

Decide

  • A winning idea
  • A storyboard

Prototype

  • A realistic façade
  • An interview script

Test

  • Five interviews
  • Patterns and a decision

4Facilitation tips

Together alonePeople work silently and individually, then the group critiques; it beats brainstorming.
Respect the DeciderInput is welcome, but the Decider decides. That's what keeps the week moving.
Façade, not productBuild just enough to feel real for an interview. Slides or a clickable mock-up are often enough.
Five is enoughPatterns usually show up after five interviews; more adds little in a week.
Watch behaviourWhat customers do with the prototype matters more than what they say about it.
Timebox hardThe schedule is tight on purpose. Use a visible timer for every activity.
Common pitfalls: no real Decider; people dropping in and out; a challenge that's too small or already decided; recruiting customers too late; building a real product on Thursday; arguing about results instead of looking for patterns.

5After the session

Monday after

Share the results, the patterns and the decision with stakeholders.

If it worked

Turn the prototype into a backlog with the Epic Breakdown Canvas and Story Map.

If it didn't

A failed test in a week is a win; decide whether to iterate or stop.

Next time

Run a short retro on the sprint format itself.

6How it fits the toolkit

A design sprint fits in discovery. Use the Opportunity Solution Tree or Product Bet Canvas to choose the challenge, the Persona & Journey Map for Monday's map, and the Epic Breakdown Canvas and User Story Map to turn a winning prototype into work.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Design Sprint”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Answer a big question in five days: map the problem, sketch ideas, decide, build a realistic prototype and test it with customers.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
