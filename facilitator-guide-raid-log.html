RAID Log Guide

How to keep a project's risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies visible, owned and acted on.

“What's the risk we're not talking about?”

A RAID log brings four kinds of uncertainty into one view. Risks might happen; assumptions are believed but unproven; issues are happening now; dependencies are what you need from others, or they need from you. Keeping it on a board, reviewed every iteration, stops it becoming a spreadsheet nobody opens.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Bring the worries from the Project Kickoff and any existing risk register
  • Bring the Dependency Map if you work with other teams
  • Agree the scales: likelihood and impact as high / medium / low

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–15R · RisksList what might happen. Rate likelihood and impact. For the high ones, agree a response: avoid, reduce, transfer or accept.
15–25A · AssumptionsWhat are we treating as true without evidence? Agree how and when to test the riskiest.
25–35I · IssuesWhat's going wrong now? Agree the impact, an action, an owner and a due date.
35–45D · DependenciesWhat do we need from others, or they from us? Record who, by when, and the status.
Every iterationReview (15 min)Walk each quadrant: what's new, what's changed, what's closed? Move risks that happened to Issues.

3Making each quadrant useful

Quality checks for each part of the log.

R · Risks

  • Written as cause → effect
  • High risks have a response
  • Each has an owner

A · Assumptions

  • Each has a test
  • Proven or disproven ones are closed

I · Issues

  • An action and a due date
  • Escalated when the team can't fix it

D · Dependencies

  • Named teams and dates
  • Status kept current
  • The other side knows

4Facilitation tips

Cause and effect“If the supplier API is late, then integration testing slips” is actionable. “Supplier risk” isn't.
Test assumptionsAn untested assumption is a risk in disguise. Use the 1% Experiment Canvas for the big ones.
Owners, not committeesEvery row needs one named person who will act.
Keep it shortIf the log has 80 rows, nobody reads it. Close, merge and prioritise.
Review in the openA short review in the team's regular rhythm keeps it current and shared.
Escalate earlyIssues the team can't solve go to the decision forum straight away.
ROAM for shared risksIn multi-team planning, SAFe sorts each risk as Resolved, Owned, Accepted or Mitigated. It's a quick way to make sure every risk ends with a decision.
Common pitfalls: vague risks with no cause or effect; no owners; never closing items; reviewing only at steering meetings; assumptions that are never tested; dependencies the other team doesn't know about.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the board or move it into your tool. Tell owners what they own.

Every iteration

Review in 15 minutes: new, changed, closed.

Monthly

Share the top risks and issues with the sponsor, with the actions being taken.

At the end

Review what happened. Which risks came true? What would you watch for next time?

6How it fits the toolkit

The project tools work together: run a Project Kickoff first, then build the Stakeholder Map, keep the RAID Log up to date, and refresh the Release Forecast after every iteration. Inside the project, teams use the lifecycle tools (Team Charter, Prioritisation, Sprint Planning and Review) and the break-down tools.

Create an editable whiteboard for “RAID Log”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Keep the project's risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies visible, owned and reviewed.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Project, Last Reviewed

Sections:
1. Risks: top left, about 50% wide and 50% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Might happen · response: avoid · reduce · transfer · accept
   Content: Risk: · Likelihood: · Impact: · Response: · Owner:
2. Assumptions: top right, about 50% wide and 50% tall; header colour amber.
   Subtitle: Believed true · not yet proven
   Content: Assumption: · How we'll test it: · By when: · Owner:
3. Issues: bottom left, about 50% wide and 50% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: Happening now
   Content: Issue: · Impact: · Action: · Owner: · Due:
4. Dependencies: bottom right, about 50% wide and 50% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: We need from others · others need from us
   Content: Dependency: · From → to: · Needed by: · Status: · Owner:

Footer questions: “What's the risk we're not talking about?” “Which assumption, if wrong, would hurt us most?”
