“Who hasn't spoken yet, and how could we make it easy for them?”

Most meetings follow the same shape: a few people talk, everyone else listens, and decisions are fuzzy. Small changes in structure fix this. The poster collects twelve simple techniques, from check-ins and silent writing to dot voting and agreement scales, each with when to use it, how to run it, and a tip. The bottom strip covers remote and hybrid sessions. For a larger library of structures, see Liberating Structures, a separately licensed collection.

1Before the session

  • Print the poster at A3 and keep it where you plan sessions
  • For each session, write the purpose and the decision or output you need
  • Choose techniques for the opening, the middle and the close
  • For remote sessions, set up the shared board and test it beforehand

3Designing a session

A simple shape that works for most meetings and workshops.

Open

  • Purpose and outcome stated
  • Everyone speaks once

Diverge

  • Silent writing first
  • Quantity before quality

Make sense

  • Cluster, then name
  • Park what's off-topic

Converge

  • Vote together
  • Test support, don't assume it

Close

  • Decisions and owners read back
  • One takeaway each

4Facilitation tips

Structure frees peopleA clear structure makes it safer for quieter people to contribute.
Diverge before convergingMixing idea generation and decision-making leads to early, weak decisions.
Write before talkingSilent writing stops the first speaker setting the agenda.
Show the timeA visible timer keeps everyone, including the facilitator, honest.
Test agreementSilence isn't consent. Use a fist of five or an agreement scale.
Equal in hybridIf one person is remote, everyone joins from their own device.
Common pitfalls: no clear purpose; discussing before writing; voting with hands raised one at a time; running over time; remote participants as spectators; ending without decisions and owners.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Facilitation Techniques”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Twelve simple techniques for better group conversations, plus tips for remote and hybrid sessions.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Session, Facilitator

Sections:
1. Check-in: top left, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · Start: help everyone arrive and speak once early. · How: · 1. Ask one light question · 2. Go round, a sentence each · 3. No discussion yet · Voices heard early speak up later.
2. Silent Writing: top centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · Before discussing, so ideas aren't shaped by the loudest. · How: · 1. Pose one clear question · 2. Everyone writes alone, 3–5 min · 3. One idea per sticky · Quiet people contribute as much as anyone.
3. Think · Pair · Share: top centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · To deepen ideas before the whole group hears them. · How: · 1. Think alone for a minute · 2. Discuss in pairs · 3. Pairs share with the group · Pairs filter and strengthen ideas fast.
4. Round-Robin: top right, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · When a few voices dominate, or you need every view. · How: · 1. Go round in order · 2. Each person speaks briefly · 3. “Pass” is always allowed · Equal airtime, without putting people on the spot.
5. Affinity Clustering: middle left, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · To make sense of many ideas quickly. · How: · 1. Put all stickies up · 2. Move similar ones together, silently · 3. Name each cluster · Silent moving avoids arguments about wording.
6. Dot Voting: middle centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · To prioritise a list in minutes. · How: · 1. Give each person 3 dots · 2. Vote at the same time · 3. Count, then discuss the top items · Vote together, so no one follows the leader.
7. Fist of Five: middle centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · To check support for a proposal or plan. · How: · 1. Everyone shows 0–5 fingers · 2. Ask the lowest votes what's missing · 3. Adjust and re-vote · Low votes are information, not obstruction.
8. Agreement Scale: middle right, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · To show how far people really support a decision. · How: · 1. Offer a 1–5 or 1–8 scale · 2. From “fully support” to “block” · 3. Discuss the spread · Shows lukewarm support that a yes/no vote hides.
9. Timeboxing: bottom left, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · For any activity that could run on forever. · How: · 1. Agree the time up front · 2. Show a visible timer · 3. At zero, decide: stop or extend · Ending on time builds trust in the process.
10. Parking Lot: bottom centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · When a useful topic is off the current agenda. · How: · 1. Write it on the parking lot · 2. Move on · 3. Review it before closing · Respects the idea without derailing the session.
11. Fishbowl: bottom centre, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · For a big group to hear a small, focused conversation. · How: · 1. A few people talk in an inner circle · 2. Others listen around them · 3. Leave an empty chair to join · Lets a large group follow a real dialogue.
12. Check-out: bottom right, about 25% wide and 30% tall.
   Content: When: · End: capture value and close the session well. · How: · 1. Each person says one takeaway · 2. Optional: rate the session 1–5 · 3. Thank everyone · A score of 1–5 for time well spent tells you what to change.

Also on the board: Remote & hybrid: · One shared board · Everyone works in it, not just the facilitator. · Everyone on their own device · In hybrid sessions, room and remote are equal. · Use chat for silent writing · Then paste ideas onto the board. · Name the roles · Facilitator, timekeeper, scribe, tech help. · Break every 50 minutes · Screens tire people faster than rooms. · Cameras optional · Ask for voices and participation, not faces.
