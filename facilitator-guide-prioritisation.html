Prioritisation Board Guide

How to compare options by value, effort and cost of delay, agree scope, and build a Now / Next / Later roadmap.

“What does it cost us, each week, to not have this yet?”

Most teams have more good ideas than capacity. The board combines four complementary techniques: a quick value vs. effort grid to sort many items, cost of delay to rank the top few with numbers (CD3 from Don Reinertsen, which SAFe calls WSJF, Weighted Shortest Job First), MoSCoW to agree scope for a fixed date, and a Now / Next / Later roadmap to share the result. Use the parts you need: a quick sort might only use the grid and the roadmap.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • List the candidate items in advance: epics, features or outcomes, one per sticky
  • Bring any data on value: customer demand, revenue, risk, deadlines
  • Invite the people who decide priorities, and team members who can judge effort
  • Agree who makes the final call if the group can't agree (usually the product owner)

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10FrameRecap the product goal or vision. Agree the scope: which items, and for what time horizon.
10–351 · Value vs. EffortPlace each sticky on the grid. Compare items to each other, not to an absolute scale. Discuss the outliers.
35–602 · Cost of Delay (WSJF)Take the top 5–8 items. Score one column at a time across all items: value, then time criticality, then risk reduction. The smallest item in each column scores 1. Add them up for the cost of delay, divide by job size and rank.
60–753 · MoSCoWIf there's a fixed date or release, sort the ranked items into Must, Should, Could and Won't (this time).
75–1004 · Now · Next · LaterPlace the items on the roadmap and write the outcome for each column. Only Now is committed.
100–110CloseRead the roadmap back. Confirm the decision owner agrees, and who will share it with stakeholders.

3Using each technique well

What each part is for, and what to watch for.

1 · Value vs. Effort

  • Relative placement is enough
  • Big bets: slice before committing
  • Drop the money pits

2 · Cost of Delay (WSJF)

  • One column at a time, across all items
  • Smallest item scores 1 in each column
  • CoD / job size gives the order

3 · MoSCoW

  • Musts should be about 60% of capacity
  • Won't means not this time
  • Needs a fixed date or scope

4 · Now · Next · Later

  • Outcomes, not dates
  • Now is committed; Later is options
  • Keep Next short

4Facilitation tips

Compare, don't score in isolationAsk “is this more or less valuable than that?” Relative judgements are faster and more reliable.
Make delay visibleAsk what it costs each week not to have an item. Time-critical work often moves up.
Slice the big betsHigh-value, high-effort items usually hide a smaller, high-value slice. Use the Epic Canvas or Story Map.
Protect the Could listIf everything is a Must, MoSCoW stops working. Push back until Musts fit comfortably.
One decision ownerThe group advises; one person decides. Say who before you start.
Roadmaps changeReview Now / Next / Later every iteration or month. Moving items is learning, not failure.
Other scoring modelsRICE (reach × impact × confidence, divided by effort) suits teams with usage data; the Kano model sorts features into basics, performance and delighters. Pick one model and use it consistently.
Common pitfalls: scoring all of one item's columns before moving to the next (score one column across every item instead); scoring items in isolation; letting the loudest stakeholder set the order; everything as a Must; a roadmap full of dates and features instead of outcomes; using cost of delay for 50 items; never revisiting it.

5After the session

Straight away

Photograph the board. Update the backlog order and the roadmap in your tools.

Share it

Walk stakeholders through the roadmap and the reasons for the order.

Next

Break the Now items down with the Epic Canvas and Story Map, ready for Sprint Planning.

Regularly

Revisit the order at each Sprint Review, when feedback and data change the picture.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Prioritisation Board”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Compare value and effort, put a number on the cost of delay, agree what's in, and lay out Now, Next and Later.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Product / Scope, Date

Sections:
1. Value vs. Effort: top left, about 50% wide and 65% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Place each item on the grid
   Content: Quick Wins · Big Bets · High value · low effort · do these first · High value · high effort · slice them first · Fill-ins · Money Pits · Low value · low effort · if there's spare time · Low value · high effort · avoid or drop · Low effort: · Effort →: · High effort:
2. Cost of Delay · WSJF: top right, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: CD3 · called WSJF in SAFe · highest score first
   Content: Score one column at a time across all items: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 (smallest item = 1). CoD = value + time + risk. · Item: · Value: · Time: · Risk / opp.: · CoD: · Job size: · CoD / size: · Rank:
3. MoSCoW: middle right, about 50% wide and 30% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: For a fixed date or scope
   Content: Must · Should · Could · Won't (this time) · Fails without it · Important, not vital · Nice if there's time · Agreed out
4. Now · Next · Later: bottom full width, about 100% wide and 35% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: A roadmap of outcomes, not dates
   Content: Now: · Committed and in progress · Next: · Planned and being shaped · Later: · Ideas and options, not committed · Outcome: (×3)
