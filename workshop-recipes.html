Start well

New team launch day

For
a team that's just formed, or has changed so much it's effectively new.
Time
1 day (about 7 hours with lunch and breaks)
Outcome
a shared purpose, working agreements, a first backlog and a plan for the first iteration.
Before
  • Agree the team's mission with the sponsor, in one or two sentences, so the day starts from something.
  • Print the boards at A3, or set them up as backgrounds on a digital whiteboard.
  • Invite the product owner and anyone the team depends on for the backlog session.
After
  • Photograph or export every board and share it in the team channel the same day.
  • Review the charter at the first retrospective: what did we agree that isn't happening?
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0020 min—Welcome, why we're here, and a personal check-in: “what I hope this team becomes”.
0:2075 minTeam Charter guidePurpose, members and roles, values, and working agreements.
1:3545 minSkills Matrix guideWhat we can do today, and where we'll need to learn or pair.
2:2015 min—Break
2:3560 minProduct Vision Board guideWho it's for, the problem, and how we'll know it's working.
3:3560 min—Lunch
4:3575 minUser Story Map guideThe journey end to end, and a first thin slice.
5:5015 min—Break
6:0545 minTeam Launch (Sprint Zero) guideWays of working, environments and the first iteration's goal.
6:5015 min—Close: one word each, and agree who writes up and shares the boards.

Uses 5 tools: Product Vision Board, Skills Matrix, Team Charter, Team Launch (Sprint Zero), User Story Map.

Project kickoff (half day)

For
the start of a project with a fixed scope, budget or date, with the team and key stakeholders.
Time
3½ hours
Outcome
an agreed purpose and scope, the people who matter, the biggest risks and the first plan.
Before
  • Draft the project's purpose and constraints with the sponsor.
  • Make a first list of stakeholders to bring to the session.
After
  • Share the RAID log and agree how often it's reviewed.
  • Send stakeholders a one-page summary using the Outcome Status Report format.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0015 min—Welcome, and the sponsor explains why this project, why now.
0:1560 minProject Kickoff guidePurpose, scope in and out, constraints, and how we'll work.
1:1540 minStakeholder Map guideWho's affected, who decides, and how we'll keep each group involved.
1:5515 min—Break
2:1045 minPre-mortem guideImagine it failed: why? Turn the top reasons into actions.
2:5525 minRAID Log guideRecord the risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies we found.
3:2010 min—Close: decisions made, owners, and the date of the first review.

Uses 4 tools: Pre-mortem, Project Kickoff, RAID Log, Stakeholder Map.

Plan

Quarterly planning day

For
a team, or a few teams, setting goals and plans for the next quarter.
Time
1 day (about 6¾ hours with lunch and breaks)
Outcome
a few objectives with key results, a prioritised list, known dependencies and a confidence vote.
Before
  • Gather last quarter's results: what moved, what didn't, and what we learned.
  • Ask leaders for the strategic priorities the plan should support.
  • Pre-score the larger items for value and size, so the day isn't spent estimating.
After
  • Publish the objectives and the Now · Next · Later roadmap where everyone can see them.
  • Put a short OKR check-in on the calendar every two weeks.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0030 minValue Metrics guideReview last quarter's outcomes and what they tell us.
0:3075 minObjectives & Key Results guideDraft two or three objectives, each with measurable key results.
1:4515 min—Break
2:0075 minPrioritisation Board guideCost of delay and size for each candidate; agree Now, Next and Later.
3:1560 min—Lunch
4:1560 minDependency Map guideWho we need, who needs us, and by when.
5:1515 min—Break
5:3045 minPre-mortem guideWhat could stop us? Agree the actions that make the plan safer.
6:1530 min—Confidence vote (fist of five), then adjust the plan until most people are at 3 or more.

Uses 5 tools: Dependency Map, Objectives & Key Results, Pre-mortem, Prioritisation Board, Value Metrics.

Shape a big idea

For
a product trio or small team turning a large, fuzzy request into small, testable pieces of work.
Time
3 hours
Outcome
a clear goal, the options for reaching it, and the first stories with examples.
Before
  • Bring any research, data or customer quotes you have about the problem.
After
  • Add the stories to the backlog, with their examples, ready for refinement.
  • Decide what you'll measure to know whether the epic worked.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0040 minImpact Map guideGoal, the people whose behaviour must change, and how.
0:4040 minOpportunity Solution Tree guideOpportunities under the outcome, and a few solutions for the best one.
1:2015 min—Break
1:3545 minEpic Breakdown Canvas guideTurn the chosen solution into an epic with a hypothesis and a first slice.
2:2040 minExample Mapping guideRules, examples and questions for the first two or three stories.

Uses 4 tools: Epic Breakdown Canvas, Example Mapping, Impact Map, Opportunity Solution Tree.

Improve

Reset a struggling team

For
a team where delivery has slowed, frustration is high or people have stopped speaking up.
Time
3 hours, ideally away from the usual meeting room
Outcome
an honest shared picture, the few things that matter most, and owned experiments to try.
Before
  • Talk one-to-one with a few team members first, so nothing in the room is a surprise to you.
  • Be clear with leaders that the session is the team's, and what will be shared afterwards.
After
  • Check the experiments at the next two retrospectives, and celebrate small wins.
  • Repeat the health check in six to eight weeks to see whether it has moved.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0020 min—Check-in, and agree how we'll treat each other today.
0:2030 minPsychological Safety Check guideAnonymous scores first, then talk about the pattern, not individuals.
0:5040 minTeam Health Check guideWhere we're strong, where we're struggling, and the trend.
1:3015 min—Break
1:4545 minRoot Cause Analysis guideTake the top one or two problems and ask why until you reach something you can change.
2:3030 min1% Experiment Canvas guideOne or two small experiments, each with an owner and a check-in date.

Uses 4 tools: 1% Experiment Canvas, Psychological Safety Check, Root Cause Analysis, Team Health Check.

Flow tune-up

For
a team whose work takes too long, gets stuck or piles up half-finished.
Time
2½ hours
Outcome
a board that shows the real flow, sensible WIP limits, and a plan for the worst blockers.
Before
  • Export the dates each item entered and left each column over the last two or three months, if you can.
After
  • Try the new board for two weeks, then review it at the retrospective.
  • Start walking the board right to left in the daily sync, using Buffering & Swarming when items stall.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0045 minValue Stream Map guideFrom request to customer: steps, wait times and hand-offs.
0:4540 minBlocker Clustering guideGroup recent blockers to find the few causes behind most of them.
1:2515 min—Break
1:4030 minKanban Board Design guideRedesign the columns to match the real flow, and set WIP limits.
2:1020 minAgeing WIP & SLE guideAgree a service level expectation and how you'll spot items going stale.

Uses 4 tools: Ageing WIP & SLE, Blocker Clustering, Kanban Board Design, Value Stream Map.

Make releases routine

For
a team whose releases are slow, risky or stressful, with whoever runs the pipeline.
Time
3 hours
Outcome
a shared map of the pipeline, a test strategy, a safer way to release, and the first improvements.
Before
  • Pull rough numbers: deploys per week, time from commit to production, failed releases and recovery time.
  • List bugs that reached production recently.
After
  • Track deployment frequency and lead time on the Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt board.
  • Repeat the health check in a quarter, and re-map the pipeline when it changes.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0015 minSDLC at a Glance guideWhere our lifecycle is strong and weak, and where work waits.
0:1545 minDelivery Pipeline Canvas guideWalk a real change from commit to users; find the biggest bottleneck.
1:0015 min—Break
1:1540 minTest Strategy guideThe shape of our tests, the quality gates, and what to test hardest.
1:5535 minRelease Readiness & Strategy guidePlan the next release: approach, go/no-go checklist and rollback.
2:3030 minContinuous Delivery Health Check guideScore the practices and pick one or two experiments.

Uses 5 tools: Continuous Delivery Health Check, Delivery Pipeline Canvas, Release Readiness & Strategy, SDLC at a Glance, Test Strategy.

After an incident

For
the team and anyone else involved, within a week of a significant incident.
Time
2 hours
Outcome
a shared timeline, the contributing factors, and actions that make a repeat less likely.
Before
  • Collect the timeline from logs, chat and alerts, and share it before the session.
  • Remind everyone the review is blameless: we look at the system, not at who did what.
After
  • Share a summary with the wider organisation, so others can learn from it too.
  • Check the actions are done at the next retrospective.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0010 min—Set the tone: why the review is blameless, and what we'll do with the output.
0:1060 minBlameless Incident Review guideTimeline, what helped, what got in the way, and contributing factors.
1:1030 minRoot Cause Analysis guideDig into the most important factor.
1:4020 min—Agree actions, owners and dates; record them in the team's backlog.

Uses 2 tools: Blameless Incident Review, Root Cause Analysis.

Lead change

Leadership offsite

For
a leadership team agreeing how it will support its teams over the next year.
Time
1 day (about 6½ hours with lunch and breaks)
Outcome
shared priorities, clear decision rights, and a plan for the change the organisation needs.
Before
  • Ask each leader for the one thing that would most help their teams this year.
  • Bring the current portfolio: what's in flight, and how money and people are split.
After
  • Share the commitments with teams, in person, within a week.
  • Review delegation and the investment mix every quarter.
StartLengthToolWhat happens
0:0030 minLeading for Psychological Safety guideHow leaders' behaviour shapes what teams feel able to say.
0:3075 minPortfolio Kanban & Investment Mix guideWhat we're funding, what we should stop, and the mix we want.
1:4515 min—Break
2:0060 minTeam Topology Design guideHow teams are organised around the work, and where hand-offs hurt.
3:0060 min—Lunch
4:0060 minDelegation Board guideWhich decisions sit where, and what we'll hand to teams.
5:0015 min—Break
5:1560 minADKAR Change Readiness guideFor the biggest change: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement.
6:1515 min—Close: the three commitments we'll make to our teams.

Uses 5 tools: ADKAR Change Readiness, Delegation Board, Leading for Psychological Safety, Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix, Team Topology Design.

Starter kits by project size

The minimum set of tools for each size of project, in the order to use them. A four-person project needs far less structure than a forty-person one; see Right-Size Your Structure for why, and for when to turn the dial up.

One team

3–10 people

Start with
  1. Team Charter agree purpose, roles and how you'll work
  2. Prioritisation Board decide what comes first
  3. Kanban Board Design one visible board for all the work
  4. Sprint Planning plan each iteration around a goal
  5. Retrospective Kit improve a little every few weeks
Add as you grow
  • Pre-mortem if the work is risky
  • Stakeholder Map if many people care about the result
  • Release Forecast & Burn-up when someone asks “when?”

Team of teams

10–50 people · 2–5 teams

Start with
  1. Right-Size Your Structure check you have enough structure, and not too much
  2. Cross-Team Planning plan together each planning period
  3. Dependency Map see who needs what from whom
  4. Cross-Team Sync a short weekly cross-team check-in
  5. RAID Log one shared list of risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies
  6. Outcome Status Report a one-page update for sponsors
Add as you grow
  • RACI & Decision Log when decisions get lost or contested
  • Cross-Team Retro when the same cross-team problems repeat
  • Release Readiness & Strategy for coordinated releases

Programme

50+ people · many teams

Start with
  1. Right-Size Your Structure set the level of governance on purpose
  2. Objectives & Key Results outcomes everyone works toward
  3. Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix decide what to fund and limit work in progress
  4. Team Topology Design organise teams around the work
  5. Value Metrics show whether the investment is paying off
  6. Delegation Board push decisions to the right level
Add as you grow
  • ADKAR Change Readiness when the programme changes how people work
  • Value Stream Map to find delays across the whole system
  • SDLC at a Glance to replace document gates with evidence
