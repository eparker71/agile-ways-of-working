Opportunity Solution Tree Guide

How to connect an outcome to customer opportunities and solutions, and test the riskiest assumptions first.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Continuous Discovery · Inspired by Teresa Torres

“What must be true for this solution to work, and how could we find out this week?”

Teams often jump from a goal straight to a feature. The opportunity solution tree, from Teresa Torres' Continuous Discovery Habits, adds the missing step: start with one measurable outcome, map the customer opportunities (needs, pains and desires) that could move it, then explore several solutions for each. The assumption map sorts what must be true by importance and evidence, so the team tests the riskiest assumptions first with small, cheap experiments.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17 (Tabloid), or larger, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Agree one product outcome to focus on, ideally from the OKRs or Product Vision Board
  • Bring evidence from customers: interviews, support tickets, analytics
  • Blue, yellow, pink and green stickies, and markers
  • Ideally, keep interviewing customers each week to feed the tree

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–10OutcomeWrite one measurable product outcome at the top. Not a feature or an output.
10–30OpportunitiesFrom customer evidence, list needs and pains that could move the outcome. Group them and pick the top three.
30–50SolutionsFor each opportunity, brainstorm at least two or three solutions. Compare them; don't fall for the first one.
50–652 · Assumption MapFor the leading solutions, write what must be true (pink). Place each by importance and evidence.
65–803 · Assumption TestsFor the top-right assumptions, design small tests with a clear success measure.
80–90CloseAgree who runs which tests this week, and when the trio will update the tree.

3Making each level strong

Quality checks for each part of the tree and the map.

Outcome

  • One measurable outcome
  • Within the team's influence

Opportunities

  • From the customer's point of view
  • Based on evidence
  • Not solutions in disguise

Solutions

  • Several per opportunity
  • Compared, not just listed

Assumption Map

  • Desirable, viable, feasible, usable, ethical
  • Top-right tested first

Assumption Tests

  • Small, fast, cheap
  • Success defined before running

4Facilitation tips

Outcome, not output“Increase repeat purchases” gives room to explore. “Build a loyalty scheme” doesn't.
Opportunities are customer words“I can't tell if my order shipped” is an opportunity. “Add tracking” is a solution.
Compare and contrastExploring several solutions at once avoids falling in love with the first idea.
Test assumptions, not ideasBreak each solution into what must be true, and test the riskiest part only.
Keep it aliveUpdate the tree every week as you learn. It's a living map, not a plan.
Talk to customers weeklyRegular interviews keep opportunities grounded in real evidence.
Common pitfalls: an output as the outcome; opportunities that are really features; only one solution per opportunity; testing whole solutions instead of assumptions; building before testing; never updating the tree.

5After the session

This week

Run the assumption tests and record results on the board.

Each week

The trio updates the tree with new interviews and test results.

When an assumption holds

Move the solution into delivery: Epic Canvas, Story Map and splitting.

When it fails

Celebrate the learning, then try the next solution or opportunity.

6How it fits the toolkit

The lifecycle tools follow the work from start to finish: a Team Charter when a team forms, a Product Vision Board to agree why the product exists, the Prioritisation Board to decide what comes first, then Sprint Planning and Sprint Review each iteration, with a retrospective from the Retro Kit to close the loop. The break-down tools (Epic Canvas, Story Map, Story Splitting, Example Mapping) sit between vision and planning.

Create an editable whiteboard for “Opportunity Solution Tree”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Connect a product outcome to customer opportunities, candidate solutions, and the assumptions to test first.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.
Key: Blue = Opportunity · Yellow = Solution · Pink = Assumption · Green = Test

Sections:
1. The Tree: middle left, about 65% wide and 100% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Outcome → opportunities → solutions → tests
   Content: OUTCOME · ONE MEASURABLE PRODUCT OUTCOME · Opportunity 1: · Opportunity 2: · Opportunity 3: · A customer need, pain or desire (×3) · Solution: (×6) · Riskiest assumption (×6) · Test (×6) · Result (×6) · Compare and contrast: explore at least two or three solutions per opportunity before choosing. · Parked opportunities and ideas
2. Assumption Map: top right, about 35% wide and 55% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Test top-right first
   Content: Watch · Test First · Important · good evidence · Important · little evidence · Ignore for now · Park · Less important · evidence · Less important · little evidence · Have evidence: · Evidence →: · No evidence:
3. Assumption Tests: bottom right, about 35% wide and 45% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Small, fast, cheap
   Content: Assumption: · Test: · Success if…: · Result: · Types: desirable · viable · feasible · usable · ethical

Footer questions: “Which customer need, if we solved it, would move our outcome most?” “What must be true for this solution to work, and how could we find out this week?”
