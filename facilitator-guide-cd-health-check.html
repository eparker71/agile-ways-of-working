Continuous Delivery Health Check Guide

“Which practice, if it improved, would make releasing least scary?”

Research by the DORA programme, described in Accelerate by Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble and Gene Kim, found that a set of technical practices predicts both faster and more stable delivery. This health check groups twelve of them into four areas: build and integrate, test, deploy and release, and run and learn. Like the Team Health Check, the team scores each practice silently, then talks about the spread. The wording is original to this toolkit and isn't the official DORA list; it's a conversation starter, not an audit or a maturity rating.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Give each person five dot stickers per practice, or use anonymous digital voting
  • Bring the last check's scores if this isn't the first time
  • Bring delivery metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, failure rate, recovery time) if you have them
  • Make clear the results stay with the team

TimeSectionFacilitator notes
0–5Set upExplain the scale: 1 not yet, 3 sometimes, 5 always. Scores are for learning, not comparison.
5–201 · PracticesEveryone scores every practice silently, then reveals. Mark the median and the spread.
20–35Discuss the spreadStart with the widest spread: why do we see it differently? Note evidence beside each practice.
35–452 · Biggest GapsStickies for the lowest scores and the gaps that hurt most. Dot-vote the top one or two.

3The four areas

Each area builds on the one before.

Build & Integrate

  • Version control for everything
  • Short-lived branches
  • Fast CI

Test

  • Trusted automation
  • Test data on demand
  • Security built in

Deploy & Release

  • Automated deployment
  • Deploy separate from release
  • Small batches

Run & Learn

  • Monitoring
  • Fast recovery
  • Blameless learning

Trend

  • Repeat every quarter
  • Compare with yourselves

4Facilitation tips

Vote first, then talkSilent scoring stops the loudest or most senior voice setting everyone's score.
The spread is the storyWhen some say 1 and others 5, people are seeing different parts of the system. Find out why.
Ask for evidence“What makes you say 4?” turns opinions into facts the team can act on.
Pick one or twoTwelve practices can feel like a lot. Small experiments on the biggest gap beat a long improvement plan.
Never compare teamsDifferent products and starting points make team league tables meaningless and harmful.
Link to the numbersOver time, check whether better practice scores show up in your delivery metrics.
Common pitfalls: treating it as a maturity audit or scoring teams against each other; discussing before voting; trying to improve everything at once; ignoring practices outside the team's control instead of raising them; doing it once.

5After the session

Straight away

Add the experiments to the backlog with owners and dates.

Each iteration

Check progress on the experiments at the retrospective.

Monthly

Watch the delivery metrics for the effect.

Quarterly

Repeat the health check and compare the trend.
