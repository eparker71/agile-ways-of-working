Reports by Role

One report for each role in the role guide: the question it answers, who it's for, what goes in it, an example filled in for the fictional Basket team, and an AI prompt to build it from your Jira, Azure DevOps or Confluence data with Atlassian Rovo, Microsoft Copilot or a similar assistant. More everyday prompts are in the Prompt Library.

Before you send an AI-drafted report

  • Check the numbers. AI assistants can miscount or misread fields. Every prompt asks for the query and the counts behind each figure, so check them.
  • Keep it to teams. Report on teams and outcomes, never individuals, and don't compare teams' velocity.
  • Use approved tools. Only share work data with AI tools your organisation allows. Assistants built into your tools, such as Rovo in Jira and Confluence or Copilot in Azure DevOps and Microsoft 365, can read the data where it lives; otherwise, export what you need and paste it in.
  • Make it yours. The AI drafts; you decide what it says. Read it as your audience would before it goes out.

Scrum Master / Delivery Lead

Iteration report

“Did we meet the iteration goal, and what got in the way?”

Who it's for

The team, the product owner and interested stakeholders

How often

At the end of each iteration, before the retro

What goes in it

  • The goal, and whether it was met
  • Items finished, carried over and added mid-iteration
  • Blocked time, and what caused it
  • Progress on the last retro's actions

Draws on

Sprint Review · Blocker Clustering · Retrospective Kit · Ageing WIP & SLE

Prompt to build it

Using the Jira or Azure DevOps project [KEY] and the iteration [name or dates], draft an iteration report for the team and stakeholders. Include: the iteration goal and whether it was met, saying how you judged it; items planned, finished, carried over and added mid-iteration; days items spent blocked or flagged, grouped by cause; the oldest item still in progress and its age; and the status of the last retrospective's actions: [paste or link]. Keep it to one page, at team level, in plain language. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Don't report velocity as a score. Carry-over is information for planning, not a failure to explain away.

Agile Coach

Team health trends

“Is the team getting healthier, and are our experiments working?”

Who it's for

The team, and their manager if the team agrees

How often

Each quarter, after the health check

What goes in it

  • Each health check area, with its trend since last time
  • Signs of psychological safety
  • Experiments tried, and what they showed
  • One or two areas to focus on next

Draws on

Team Health Check · Psychological Safety Check · 1% Experiment Canvas · Marginal Gains Workshop

Prompt to build it

Here are our team health check results for the last [two or three] rounds: [paste results or link the Confluence page], and notes from our experiments: [paste]. Summarise how each area has moved and why, what each experiment showed against its measure, and suggest one or two areas to focus on next, with a small experiment for each. Quote the team's own words where they help, without identifying anyone. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Health checks are the team's data. Share the trends only with the team's agreement, and never turn the scores into a target.

Product Owner

Outcome and backlog report

“Are we moving the outcome, and is the backlog ready for what's next?”

Who it's for

Stakeholders and the team

How often

Each iteration, at the review

What goes in it

  • The outcome measures against their targets
  • What shipped, and what we learned from users
  • How much of the backlog is ready, and how old it is
  • What's coming next, and why

Draws on

Sprint Review · Backlog Refinement · Backlog Item Anatomy · Value Metrics

Prompt to build it

Using the Jira or Azure DevOps project [KEY], draft a product owner's report for this iteration's review. Include: our outcome measures [list them with baselines and targets, or paste the latest figures]; the items finished this iteration and what each was for; how many backlog items meet our Definition of Ready [paste it] for the next two iterations; how many backlog items are more than six months old; and the next five items in order, with a line on why each. End with any decisions I need from stakeholders. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Outcomes first, output second. A long list of finished items says little about whether customers are better off.

Product Manager

Bet review

“Did the bet pay off, and should we continue, change course or stop?”

Who it's for

Product leadership and sponsors

How often

At each bet's checkpoint

What goes in it

  • The hypothesis, and the go or stop rule agreed up front
  • What the test showed, against the rule
  • What we learned that we didn't expect
  • The decision, and the next step

Draws on

Product Bet Canvas · Opportunity Solution Tree · Design Sprint · Now · Next · Later Roadmap

Prompt to build it

Here is our product bet: [paste the hypothesis, the test and the go or stop rule], and the results: [paste figures, or link the Confluence page or dashboard]. Write a one-page bet review: the hypothesis, what we tested and with whom, the results against the rule we agreed up front, anything surprising, and a recommendation to continue, change course or stop, with the next step and its cost. Flag where the evidence is thin. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Judge the result against the rule you set before the test, not one invented afterwards. Moving the goalposts turns a bet into a plan.

Project Manager

Outcome status report

“Are we on track, and what do we need from our sponsors?”

Who it's for

Sponsors and the steering group

How often

Every two weeks, or monthly

What goes in it

  • A one-line headline and an honest status
  • Outcomes against their measures
  • A forecast as a range, with its assumptions
  • Top risks, and the decisions needed

Draws on

Outcome Status Report · Release Forecast & Burn-up · RAID Log · Stakeholder Map

Prompt to build it

Using the Jira or Azure DevOps project [KEY], the RAID log at [link] and these notes: [paste], draft a one-page status report for the steering group. Include: a one-line headline and a status (green, amber or red) with the reason; our outcome measures against their targets; a delivery forecast as a range based on recent throughput, with its assumptions; what was delivered; the top three risks or issues with owners; and the decisions we need, from whom, by when. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Don't let the AI soften the message. If it's amber, say so, and say what would make it green.

Program Manager

Programme roll-up

“Are the teams together moving our shared objectives, and where are they stuck?”

Who it's for

Programme sponsors and team leads

How often

Monthly

What goes in it

  • Each shared objective, with its key results
  • Progress and confidence for each objective
  • Cross-team dependencies and risks
  • Decisions and escalations

Draws on

Objectives & Key Results · Dependency Map · Cross-Team Sync · RAID Log

Prompt to build it

Using the Jira or Azure DevOps projects [KEYS] and our objectives and key results [paste or link], draft a monthly programme roll-up. For each objective: the latest key result figures against target, the teams contributing, and an on track, at risk or behind rating with the reason. Then list the cross-team dependencies that are late or at risk [from our dependency board or issue links], the top cross-team risks, and the decisions or escalations needed, with owners. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Roll up outcomes, not activity. Don't compare teams' throughput or velocity: their work isn't the same size.

Release Train Engineer / Chief Scrum Master

Planning-period progress

“Midway through the planning period, will we meet the objectives we committed to?”

Who it's for

The teams, product management and business owners

How often

Every two weeks in the planning period

What goes in it

  • Each team's objectives, and confidence now against confidence at planning
  • Dependencies done, due and late
  • Risks raised at planning, and their status
  • What needs to change before the next sync

Draws on

Cross-Team Planning · Cross-Team Sync · Dependency Map · Cross-Team Retro

Prompt to build it

Using the Jira or Azure DevOps projects [KEYS] for the teams in our planning period [dates], and the objectives each team committed to [paste or link], draft a progress report for the next cross-team sync. Include: each team's objectives with a status; the confidence vote now and at planning [paste both]; cross-team dependencies that are done, due or late; the risks raised at planning and their status; and the two or three things that need to change before the next sync, with owners. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Confidence is the teams' call, not the AI's. Ask the teams to vote, and don't let a report guess it from ticket counts.

Portfolio Manager / PMO

Portfolio view

“Are we working on the right things, and not too many at once?”

Who it's for

The portfolio board and investment owners

How often

Monthly or quarterly

What goes in it

  • Initiatives by stage, against the WIP limits
  • The investment mix: agreed against actual
  • Value delivered against the expected outcomes
  • Proposals to start, stop or change

Draws on

Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix · Value Metrics · Objectives & Key Results · Right-Size Your Structure

Prompt to build it

Using our portfolio board [link] and the investment mix we agreed [paste], draft a portfolio report. Show: the number of initiatives at each stage against its WIP limit; the investment mix agreed against actual [from effort, cost or team allocation: say which]; expected against actual outcomes for initiatives in delivery or recently finished; and proposals to start, stop or change, with the reasoning for each. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: Count started work honestly. Starting more initiatives rarely makes any of them finish sooner.

Executive / Sponsor

One-page executive brief

“Is the work delivering the outcomes we're paying for, and what do you need from me?”

Who it's for

Executives and sponsors

How often

Monthly

What goes in it

  • Outcomes against strategy, in three lines
  • The biggest risk, and what's being done
  • What's changed since last month
  • What we need from leadership

Draws on

Value Metrics · Outcome Status Report · Objectives & Key Results · Portfolio Kanban & Investment Mix

Prompt to build it

Using these sources [our status reports, OKR updates and value metrics: links or paste], write a one-page brief for the executive team. Start with a two-sentence summary. Then: progress on our outcomes against strategy, in three lines at most; the single biggest risk and what's being done about it; what's changed since last month; and up to three specific asks of leadership, each with a deadline. No jargon, and no ticket-level detail. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The asks matter most. If there are none, say so, rather than inventing work for leaders.

Engineering Manager / Tech Lead

Delivery and quality report

“Are we delivering faster and more safely, and where is quality slipping?”

Who it's for

Engineering leads and the team

How often

Monthly

What goes in it

  • The four key metrics, with their trends
  • Incidents, and reliability against SLOs
  • Pipeline waits and flaky tests
  • Technical debt: what's growing, and what's being paid down

Draws on

Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt · Continuous Delivery Health Check · SLOs & Error Budgets · Blameless Incident Review

Prompt to build it

Using our deployment pipeline [name it], incident tickets in [project KEY] and our SLO dashboard [link], draft a monthly delivery and quality report for the team. Include: the four key metrics (deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore) for this month and the last three; incidents, and the error budget used; where changes wait longest in the pipeline; flaky or slow tests; and the technical debt that grew or was paid down. Say how each figure was calculated. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: These are team and system measures. Never break them down by person, or people will start gaming them.

Designer / Business Analyst

Discovery summary

“What have we learned about users, and what does it change?”

Who it's for

The product trio, the team and stakeholders

How often

After each round of research or testing

What goes in it

  • What we set out to learn
  • What we did, and with whom
  • What we found, with the evidence
  • What changes in the backlog or design

Draws on

Persona & Journey Map · Opportunity Solution Tree · Cynefin® Sense-Making · Design Sprint

Prompt to build it

Here are my research notes and test results: [paste interview notes, survey results or test data, or link the Confluence pages]. Write a one-page discovery summary: what we set out to learn; what we did and with whom; the main findings with the evidence for each, quoting users without names where it helps; how confident we are; and what this changes in the backlog, design or roadmap. Keep what users did separate from what they said. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: The AI will find patterns in anything. Check each finding against the raw notes, and keep “one person said” apart from “most people did”.

Team Member

Weekly flow snapshot

“How is our work flowing this week, and what should we swarm on?”

Who it's for

The team, for themselves

How often

Weekly, before a daily sync

What goes in it

  • Items finished this week
  • Work in progress against our limits
  • The oldest items, and any past our SLE
  • Blockers, and how long they've waited

Draws on

Daily Sync · Ageing WIP & SLE · Kanban Board Design · Blocker Clustering

Prompt to build it

Using our board in Jira or Azure DevOps [project KEY and board name], give the team a short flow snapshot for this week: items finished; work in progress in each column against our WIP limits [paste them]; the five oldest items in progress with their age in days; any items older than our service level expectation of [N] days; and blocked or flagged items, with how long each has been blocked. Suggest which item to swarm on first, and why. Show the query or filter you used (for example, the JQL) and the count behind each figure, so I can check them. List any assumptions you made, and ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Watch out: This is for the team, not a manager's dashboard. If it's used to judge people, it stops being honest.
