“Where does AI genuinely help us, and where does it get in the way?”

Most teams now use AI tools for drafting, summarising, analysis, tests or code, often without agreeing how. This board helps the team make its use explicit: which tasks AI is encouraged for, used with care, or not used for; the guardrails on data and tools; how output is reviewed; how skills are shared; the risks to watch; and how to measure whether it actually helps. It complements, and never replaces, your organisation's AI and data policies.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Find your organisation's AI, data and security policies, and the approved tools
  • Ask team members how they use AI today, without judgement
  • Gather a baseline for a few measures, such as cycle time or defects found later
  • Invite someone from security or data protection if the team handles sensitive data

3Making each section strong

Quality checks for each part of the agreement.

1 · Where We Use AI

  • Named tasks, not “anything”
  • Not-for list is explicit

2 · Guardrails

  • Matches organisation policy
  • Data rules are clear

3 · Quality & Review

  • Same bar for all work
  • A person owns every result

4 · Skills & Sharing

  • Good prompts are shared
  • Core skills are practised

5 · Risks We'll Watch

  • Errors, data, bias, over-reliance
  • Who notices and how

6 · Measure the Impact

  • A baseline exists
  • Quality as well as speed

7 · Review

  • A short review cycle
  • Whole team agreed

4Facilitation tips

Policy firstThe team agreement sits inside the organisation's rules. Check them before you start.
Specific tasks“Drafting release notes” is clear. “Writing” is not.
People own resultsWhoever uses AI is accountable for the output, as with any tool.
Measure quality tooFaster isn't better if defects or rework go up. Track both.
Share openlyMake it normal to say how work was produced. It builds trust and spreads good practice.
Review oftenTools and policies change quickly. Revisit the agreement monthly at first.
Common pitfalls: ignoring organisation policy; banning AI so people use it quietly; no review of AI output; measuring only speed; letting core skills fade; writing the agreement once and never revisiting it.

5After the session

Straight away

Put the agreement next to the Team Charter and link it from the team's space.

Each retro

Ask: is the agreement working? Any near misses or great wins?

Monthly

Check the impact measures and update the agreement.

When tools change

Revisit the guardrails and review rules before using new tools.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “AI Working Agreement”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Agree how the team uses AI tools: where it helps, the guardrails, how output is reviewed, and how you'll know it's working.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Team, Date

Sections:
1. Where We Use AI: top left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: Be specific: name the tasks
   Content: Encouraged · With care · Not for · e.g. drafts, summaries, test ideas · e.g. code, analysis: always reviewed · e.g. decisions about people
2. Guardrails: top right, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour red.
   Subtitle: Check with your organisation's policy
   Content: Only approved tools for work data · No confidential, customer or personal data in unapproved tools · A person reviews AI output before it's used or shipped · We say when work is substantially AI-assisted · We check sources, facts and licences · Approved tools: · Policy owner / contact:
3. Quality & Review: middle left, about 50% wide and 35% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Same bar, whoever wrote it
   Content: AI-assisted code gets the same review and tests as any code · The person who uses AI owns the result · Reviewers can ask how output was produced · Additions to our Definition of Done:
4. Skills & Sharing: middle centre, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour emerald green.
   Content: Shared prompts live at: · We share what works at: · Who can help others: · Skills we'll keep sharp:
5. Risks We'll Watch: middle right, about 25% wide and 35% tall; header colour amber.
   Content: Wrong or invented answers · Security and data leaks · Bias · over-reliance · lost skills
6. Measure the Impact: bottom left, about 65% wide and 30% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Is it actually helping?
   Content: Measure: · Before: · After: · What we noticed: · Try: cycle time · defects found later · review time · rework · team confidence
7. Review This Agreement: bottom right, about 35% wide and 30% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: AI changes fast
   Content: Review every: · Last reviewed: · Owner: · Agreed by the whole team · Checked against organisation policy

Footer questions: “Where does AI genuinely help us, and where does it get in the way?” “Would we be comfortable explaining how this was made?”
