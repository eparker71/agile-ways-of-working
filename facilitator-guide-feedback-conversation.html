Feedback Conversation Guide

How to prepare and hold one feedback conversation: describe what happened and its effect, listen, and agree what's next.

“Did I describe what happened, or judge who they are?”

Feedback goes wrong when it's vague, late or sounds like a judgement of the person. The Situation–Behaviour–Impact (SBI) model from the Center for Creative Leadership keeps it specific: describe the situation, the observable behaviour and its impact. This board adds a request, a self-check before you start, space to hear the other side, agreed next steps, and a reminder that the same shape works for praise. Anyone can use it: team members, leads, coaches and managers.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or use it on screen; it's your preparation, not a script to read out
  • Write it soon after the event, while the details are fresh
  • Book a private, unhurried time that suits the other person
  • Check how they like to receive feedback, from their user manual if they have one

Before2 · Prepare ItWrite the situation, behaviour, impact and request. Strip out labels and guesses.

3Situation, behaviour, impact, request

Specific, observable, and about the effect.

Situation

  • When and where
  • Recent and specific

Behaviour

  • What was seen or heard
  • No labels

Impact

  • Effect on you, team, work
  • Your experience

Request

  • What you'd like next
  • Then ask theirs

Hear it back

  • Their view
  • Feedback for you

4Facilitation tips

Behaviour, not character“You interrupted twice” can be changed; “you're rude” can only be argued with.
Own the impactSay how it affected you or the work, rather than claiming to know their intent.
Ask, then listenTheir side often changes the picture. Be ready to change your request.
Soon and privateFeedback days later, or in front of others, is harder to hear.
Praise specifically“Great job” fades; “your summary got the decision made in five minutes” sticks.
Ask for feedback tooLeaders who ask for feedback get more honest feedback.
Common pitfalls: the feedback sandwich hiding the message; labels instead of behaviour; guessing intent; saving it up for a review; doing it by message; not listening; no follow-up.

5After the session

Straight away

Write down what you agreed and share it if that helps.

At the check-in

Notice and say what's changed, specifically.

For yourself

Reflect on how it went and what you'd do differently.

Over time

Aim for more praise than correction.
