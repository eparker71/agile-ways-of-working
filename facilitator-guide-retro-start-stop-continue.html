Start · Stop · Continue Guide

How to run the classic three-column retrospective, for a sprint or a quarter.

Facilitator Run-Sheet · Retrospective Kit · Reflect, Learn, Adapt
Open board →SVG →Word (editable) ↓

“If we could only change one thing, what would it be?”

A simple, action-oriented format: what should we start, stop, and keep doing? It works well for new teams, short timeboxes, and teams that want to focus on behaviour change rather than a long look back.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or load the SVG into Miro / Mural / FigJam. Write the period on the actions strip (sprint, month or quarter); allow about 60 min for a sprint and 2 h for a quarter
  • Yellow and green stickies, markers, dot stickers (3 per person)
  • Bring the actions from the last retro and their status
  • Have data to hand: velocity or throughput, cycle time, escaped defects, any experiment results
  • For quarter retros, invite key stakeholders and bring trends across the sprints
  • Make sure the room (or call) is the team only, unless the team agrees otherwise

3Prompts for each column

Read these out if a column stays empty.

1 · Start

  • What have other teams tried that we haven't?
  • What experiment keeps getting postponed?
  • What would make next sprint easier?

2 · Stop

  • What do we do out of habit that nobody would miss?
  • Which meeting or step adds the least value?
  • What slows us down every sprint?

3 · Continue

  • What worked so well we must protect it?
  • Which recent change paid off?
  • What should we tell other teams about?

Actions strip

  • Up to 3 actions
  • Owner + done when for each
  • Bigger ideas go on a 1% Experiment Canvas

4Facilitation tips

Write before you talkSilent writing gets every voice on the wall before the loudest voice sets the direction.
Systems, not peopleAsk “what made that hard?” instead of “who did that?”. Protect psychological safety at all times.
Fewer, better actionsOne action that gets done beats five that don't. Cap it at three, and check last time's actions before adding more.
Make actions visiblePut actions on the team board or backlog, so they compete for capacity like any other work.
Quarter = zoom outIn the quarter format, look for patterns across sprints and things outside the team's control that need escalating.
Vary the formatThe same retro every time leads to the same answers. Rotate formats every few sprints.
Common pitfalls: actions with no owner; repeating the same complaints without asking why previous actions failed; jumping to solutions during silent writing; letting one topic eat the whole timebox; managers in the room who make people hold back.

5After the session

Within 24 hours

Photograph or export the board. Add each action to the team backlog with its owner, and share a short summary.

During the sprint / quarter

Check actions at stand-up or in the weekly sync. Unblock early rather than reporting failure at the next retro.

At the next retro

Open by reviewing each action: done, in progress, or dropped, and why. Celebrate the ones that worked.

Running remotely

Lock the SVG as a background, pre-create sticky stacks in both colours, use built-in voting and a visible timer.

6How it fits the toolkit

Feed experiment results from the 1% Experiment Canvas into each retro, and rotate formats (Start · Stop · Continue, 4 Ls, Mad · Sad · Glad, Sailboat) so conversations stay fresh. If the same theme keeps coming back, it is a system issue: take it to a Marginal Gains Workshop.
