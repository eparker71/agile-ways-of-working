Tools by Industry

Agile ideas started in software, but the tools work wherever work is uncertain and people need to learn as they go. For each kind of work: where to start, what else helps, the words people there use, what to watch for, and what to skip. Pair it with Tools by role and Right-size your project.

Software & digital products

Most of the toolkit was written with you in mind. Work is uncertain, you can release often, and feedback is fast if you build for it. The risk is busyness: shipping lots of output without checking that it changed anything.

Start with

  1. Opportunity Solution Tree
  2. Example Mapping
  3. Delivery Pipeline Canvas

Also useful

User Story Map · Backlog Refinement · Test Strategy · Release Readiness & Strategy · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Architecture Decision Record · Event Storming · Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt · Blameless Incident Review · AI in Delivery

Incrementrelease, version
Spiketime-boxed investigation

Technical debtthe cost of shortcuts, to non-technical people

Watch out for

  • Work is “done” when it's merged, not when customers have it anti-pattern →
  • Going faster while incidents and rework rise anti-pattern →
  • Using velocity to compare or push teams anti-pattern →

Skip or adapt

Nearly everything fits. Use the project tools (RAID Log, Status Report) when you have a fixed-scope project, not for ongoing product work.

Marketing & communications

Work arrives as campaigns with fixed launch dates, many reviewers and approvers, and results you only see after launch. Small, frequent experiments beat one big launch you can't change.

Start with

  1. Kanban Board Design
  2. Prioritisation Board
  3. 1% Experiment Canvas

Also useful

Stakeholder Map · Objectives & Key Results · Persona & Journey Map · Now · Next · Later Roadmap · Product Bet Canvas · Design Sprint · Lean Coffee · Retrospective Kit

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Backlogcampaign pipeline
Storydeliverable, asset
Definition of Doneready to publish
Incrementlaunch
WIP (work in progress)campaigns in flight
Retrospectivecampaign wash-up

Watch out for

  • Approvals wait out of sight: show “waiting for sign-off” as its own column
  • Every campaign is urgent, so nothing gets finished anti-pattern →
  • Counting posts and emails sent instead of leads or sign-ups

Skip or adapt

Event Storming · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Test Strategy · Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Architecture Decision Record. These are about building and releasing software. Release Readiness still works for a big launch.

HR & people teams

Your customers are employees and managers, and much of your work is change: new policies, systems and ways of working. Some work is confidential, and roll-outs succeed or fail on adoption, not on the launch date.

Start with

  1. Kanban Board Design
  2. Stakeholder Map
  3. ADKAR Change Readiness

Also useful

Persona & Journey Map · 1% Experiment Canvas · Objectives & Key Results · Team Onboarding · RACI & Decision Log · Psychological Safety Check · Personal User Manual · Motivation & Recognition · Feedback Conversation · Coaching Conversation · Retrospective Kit

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Stakeholdermanagers, leaders, unions
Personaemployee or candidate persona
Incrementroll-out, pilot
Story“As a new starter, I want…”
Minimum viable product (MVP)pilot with one team

Watch out for

  • Announcing a change and calling it done anti-pattern →
  • Rolling out several big changes at once anti-pattern →
  • Putting confidential cases on a shared board: use a private lane with no names

Skip or adapt

Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Test Strategy · Architecture Decision Record · Event Storming. These are for software delivery. For a new HR system, the delivery team will use them; you'll need Release Readiness.

IT operations & service desks

Work arrives unpredictably, much of it urgent, with service levels to meet. Planning fixed iterations rarely survives the first day, so manage flow: limit work in progress, make queues visible and learn from incidents.

Start with

  1. Kanban Board Design
  2. Ageing WIP & SLE
  3. Blocker Clustering

Also useful

Buffering & Swarming · Kanban Cadences · Blameless Incident Review · Root Cause Analysis · Value Stream Map · Delivery Metrics & Tech Debt · Impediments Board · RACI & Decision Log · Marginal Gains Workshop

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Backlogticket queue
Class of servicepriority (P1 to P4)
Service level expectation (SLE)service level target
Incrementchange
Swarmingmajor-incident response

Watch out for

  • Planned work collapses under urgent tickets anti-pattern →
  • The same blockers come back every week anti-pattern →
  • Ranking people by tickets closed anti-pattern →

Skip or adapt

User Story Map · Sprint Planning · Design Sprint · Example Mapping. Fixed iterations fit badly with unplanned work. Use Kanban Cadences instead of sprint events, and keep Story Mapping for bigger improvement projects.

Data & analytics

Work is a mix of requests, exploration and building pipelines. Many questions turn out to be the wrong question, and a dashboard nobody uses is waste. Start from the decision the work will inform.

Start with

  1. Kanban Board Design
  2. Opportunity Solution Tree
  3. Story Splitting

Also useful

1% Experiment Canvas · Persona & Journey Map · Stakeholder Map · Backlog Refinement · Example Mapping · Architecture Decision Record · Test Strategy · Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Value Metrics · AI in Delivery

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Storyquestion, analysis request
Spikeexploration
Acceptance criteriathe decision this informs
Definition of Donechecked, documented and handed over
Incrementdataset or dashboard version

Watch out for

  • Tickets that say “build table X” with no question behind them anti-pattern →
  • Requests pile up that nobody will ever use anti-pattern →
  • Dashboards nobody opens: ask what decision each one informs

Skip or adapt

User Story Map · Release Readiness & Strategy · Design Sprint. Story maps and release plans fit products better than analysis. A data product with users is the exception.

Hardware, manufacturing & engineering

Changes cost more once something is built, parts have long lead times, and testing needs physical builds. You can still work iteratively: short build-test-learn cycles on prototypes, and flow on the factory floor.

Start with

  1. Value Stream Map
  2. Kanban Board Design
  3. Pre-mortem

Also useful

Root Cause Analysis · Marginal Gains Workshop · 1% Experiment Canvas · Dependency Map · Cross-Team Planning · RAID Log · Release Forecast & Burn-up · Test Strategy · Architecture Decision Record · Blameless Incident Review · Design Sprint

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Iterationbuild-test cycle
Incrementprototype, build
Spikeproof of concept
Definition of Donepasses test and inspection
Minimum viable product (MVP)engineering prototype

Watch out for

  • Promising one ship date when parts and testing are uncertain anti-pattern →
  • Teams blocked by each other's parts and sign-offs anti-pattern →
  • Forcing two-week sprints on a six-week build cycle: match the cadence to the work

Skip or adapt

Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Story Splitting. Pipeline and CD tools are for software, so use them only for firmware. Splitting still works, but slice by capability or test, not by user story.

Public sector & government

Services must work for everyone, decisions are scrutinised, and funding and governance often assume you know everything up front. Show learning early, through research, pilots and open plans, to build trust.

Start with

  1. Persona & Journey Map
  2. Stakeholder Map
  3. Now · Next · Later Roadmap

Also useful

Project Kickoff · Impact Map · Product Bet Canvas · Pre-mortem · RAID Log · Outcome Status Report · Value Metrics · ADKAR Change Readiness · RACI & Decision Log · Design Sprint · Project Close-out

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Personaservice user
Stakeholderministers, policy teams, the public
Minimum viable product (MVP)pilot, private beta
Incrementgo-live of a service change
Product goalpolicy intent

Watch out for

  • Annual business cases that fix scope a year ahead anti-pattern →
  • Roadmaps of features and dates that become commitments anti-pattern →
  • Agile introduced by decree, without changing governance anti-pattern →

Skip or adapt

Event Storming · Continuous Delivery Health Check. Use them only where you build digital services in-house. Accessibility isn't optional here: put it in your Definition of Done.

Non-profit & education

Capacity is tight, people wear many hats, volunteers come and go, and funders want evidence of impact. Keep tools light, make priorities explicit, and measure difference made, not activity.

Start with

  1. Impact Map
  2. Kanban Board Design
  3. Retrospective Kit

Also useful

Objectives & Key Results · Stakeholder Map · 1% Experiment Canvas · Lean Coffee · Team Health Check · RACI & Decision Log · Now · Next · Later Roadmap · Team Onboarding · Motivation & Recognition · Project Close-out · Value Metrics

Say it this way

In the toolkitHere you might say
Personabeneficiary, learner, member
Outcomeimpact
Stakeholderfunders, trustees, parents
Iterationterm, unit, funding round
Retrospectiveend-of-term review

Watch out for

  • Saying yes to every project a funder suggests anti-pattern →
  • Everything is urgent because everything matters anti-pattern →
  • Volunteers relearn how things work: keep ways of working on one page with a Team Charter

Skip or adapt

Delivery Pipeline Canvas · Continuous Delivery Health Check · Test Strategy · Architecture Decision Record · Event Storming · Estimation. These assume a software team or detailed estimates. Simple counts of finished work are enough here.

Not on the list?

Pick the closest kind of work: a legal or finance team often works like HR (case work, confidentiality, change), a construction or lab team like hardware (physical builds, long lead times). Then start with three tools, use them for a few weeks, and keep what helps. The Scenario Playbook covers specific situations, and the Glossary explains any term.
