Blameless Incident Review Guide

How to learn from an incident by understanding how it made sense at the time, and improving the system.

“Why did it make sense to them at the time?”

After an incident, the question “who caused this?” stops learning: people get defensive and hide what really happened. A blameless review asks instead how the system made the incident possible. It rebuilds the timeline with what people knew at each point, looks for several contributing factors rather than one root cause, notes what helped and where you got lucky, and ends with actions to prevent, detect or recover sooner. The approach is widely used in software reliability engineering, including Google's SRE practice.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3 or 11×17, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Collect facts: logs, alerts, chat messages, the timeline from the incident channel
  • Choose a facilitator who wasn't directly involved
  • Invite everyone who responded, and anyone affected who can add context
  • Remind people in the invitation that the review is blameless

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeSectionFacilitator notes

5–151 · SummaryAgree the facts: what happened, impact, how it was detected, duration and severity.

3Making each part strong

Quality checks for the review.

2 · Ground Rules

  • Read aloud
  • Facilitator steps in if blame appears

3 · Timeline

  • What people knew then
  • Not hindsight

4 · Contributing Factors

  • Several categories
  • Systems, not individuals

5 · What Helped

  • Things to keep and strengthen

6 · Where We Got Lucky

  • Hidden risks
  • Often the most useful insight

7 · Follow-up Actions

  • Few and specific
  • Owned and dated

4Facilitation tips

Ask how, not who“How did the change reach production?” invites learning. “Who approved it?” invites blame.
Avoid hindsightIt's obvious now. Ask what people could see at the time.
Several factorsIncidents usually need several things to go wrong at once. Look for them all.
Count the luckNear misses and lucky breaks show risks that will strike again.
Fewer, better actionsThree meaningful actions that happen beat ten that don't.
Share widelyPublish the review so other teams learn too.
Common pitfalls: naming someone as the cause; stopping at “human error”; one root cause for a complex incident; too many actions; actions with no owner; reviews only for big incidents, not near misses.

5After the session

Within a day

Write up the review and share it openly.

This iteration

Plan the actions into the backlog.

Next month

Check the actions were done, and whether they helped.

Over time

Look across reviews for repeating factors, and bring them to the Root Cause board.

6How it fits the toolkit

Incident reviews connect to the improvement tools: take repeating factors to Root Cause Analysis, track recovery time in Delivery Metrics, and use the Psychological Safety Check if people are reluctant to speak openly.

Show the prompt
Create an editable whiteboard for “Blameless Incident Review”, a workshop board.

Purpose: Learn from an incident without blame: what happened, why it made sense at the time, and how to make it less likely.

Layout: a wide landscape board (about 16:11). Make one frame per section below, placed as described. In each frame, add the section name as a heading, then its content as short text labels or empty fields to fill in. Leave space in each frame for sticky notes. Use the header colour given. Keep the wording as written and don't add extra content.

Header fields to fill in: Incident, Review Date

Sections:
1. Summary: top left, about 65% wide and 20% tall; header colour slate grey.
   Subtitle: Facts only
   Content: What happened: · Detected by / how: · Impact (who, how long): · Duration to recover: · Severity: · 1 · critical · 2 · major · 3 · minor · 4 · near miss
2. Ground Rules: top right, about 35% wide and 20% tall; header colour purple.
   Subtitle: Read aloud at the start
   Content: We look at systems, not at who to blame · People did what made sense with what they knew · Curiosity over judgement: ask “how”, not “who” · What we learn is shared openly
3. Timeline: middle full width, about 100% wide and 30% tall; header colour blue.
   Subtitle: What did people know at each point?
   Content: Time: · What happened: · What we knew then: · WHAT WE DID AND WHY
4. Contributing Factors: bottom left, about 65% wide and 30% tall; header colour orange.
   Subtitle: Usually several · rarely one root cause
   Content: Technology · Process · Knowledge · Environment · Communication
5. What Helped: bottom right, about 15% wide and 30% tall; header colour emerald green.
6. Where We Got Lucky: bottom right, about 15% wide and 30% tall; header colour amber.
7. Follow-up Actions: bottom full width, about 100% wide and 15% tall; header colour dark green.
   Subtitle: Prevent · detect sooner · recover faster
   Content: Action: · TYPE: PREVENT / DETECT / RECOVER · Owner: · Due:

Footer questions: “Why did it make sense to them at the time?” “What would make the next incident less likely, or easier to recover from?”
