Ageing WIP & SLE Guide

How to work out a service level expectation from cycle times, and use work item age to act before items are late.

“Which item is oldest, and what would it take to finish it today?”

Cycle time tells you how long finished work took; work item age tells you how long open work has been running. Plotting each open item's age against the team's cycle time percentiles shows which items are at risk while there's still time to act. The 85th percentile gives a service level expectation (SLE): “85% of our items finish within N days”. It's a forecast for a single item, not a target. Work item age and SLE are described in the Kanban Guide and in Daniel Vacanti's work on flow metrics.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A3, or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Export start and finish dates for at least the last 20–30 finished items
  • Agree when the clock starts (e.g. first in-progress column) and stops (e.g. Done)
  • Know the in-progress columns of your board, left to right
  • Small dot stickers or markers for plotting

2Agenda & run-sheet

TimeStepFacilitator notes
0–102 · Cycle Time DataList recently finished items with start, finish and days (count partial days as a whole day).
10–20PercentilesSort the days, smallest to largest. The 85th percentile is the value 85% of the way along the list. Repeat for 50th and 70th.
20–253 · Our SLEWrite the 85th percentile into the SLE sentence, with the data range and the clock start and stop.
25–351 · Ageing WIP ChartWrite the column names along the bottom and age in days up the side. Draw the three percentile lines. Plot each open item.
35–454 · Act on Ageing ItemsFor each item past a line, agree the action. Swarm the ones past the 85th.
DailyUpdateMove dots up a day and across as items progress. Start the daily sync with the oldest items.

3Reading the chart

What the dots tell you.

Below the 50th

  • On track
  • No action needed

Past the 50th

  • Worth a mention
  • Is anything slowing it?

Past the 70th

  • At risk
  • Ask what's needed to finish

Past the 85th

  • Likely to miss the SLE
  • Swarm it

Dots high in early columns

  • Stuck before real work started
  • Check for blockers or big items

4Facilitation tips

Age is the leading signalCycle time is only known at the end. Age tells you about risk while you can still act.
An SLE isn't a targetPushing people to hit it leads to gaming. Use it to spot risk and to answer “when?” for one item.
Use enough dataTwenty to thirty items gives a reasonable start. Recalculate monthly.
Start with the oldestWalk the chart from the top. The oldest items need attention first.
Right-size before you startAsk “can we finish this within our SLE?” when pulling work. If not, split it.
Link it to swarmingUse the 85th percentile as the age trigger in Buffering & Swarming.
Common pitfalls: treating the SLE as a deadline; using averages instead of percentiles; too little data; never updating the dots; ignoring items in early columns; tracking age but never acting on it.

5After the session

Straight away

Put the SLE sentence on the team board and in the team's working agreements.

Every day

Update the chart and discuss the oldest items first at the daily sync.

Each retro

Look at items that passed the 85th line. Why? Feed causes into Blocker Clustering.

Monthly

Recalculate the percentiles with new data and update the SLE.

6How it fits the toolkit

The flow pack works as a set. Kanban Board Design makes the workflow visible and limits WIP. Ageing WIP & SLE shows which items are at risk and sets the swarm triggers used in Buffering & Swarming. Blocker Clustering finds why work gets stuck, and the Skills Matrix shows who can swarm. Bring the results to retrospectives and the Team Health Check.
