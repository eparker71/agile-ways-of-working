“Where does work wait, and why?”

Most of the time it takes to deliver something is spent waiting, not working: in queues, hand-offs and approvals. A value stream map, a lean technique popularised by Mike Rother and John Shook's Learning to See, follows one type of work from request to customer. It records each step's work time and the waits between steps, then calculates flow efficiency: work time as a share of total lead time. The % complete and accurate measure, from Karen Martin and Mike Osterling, shows where rework starts.

1Before the session

  • Print the board at A1 or A3 (or 11×17), or load the SVG as a digital background
  • Pick one value stream and one type of work, such as a customer feature request
  • Gather data where you can: dates for recent items, queue sizes, rework rates
  • Invite someone who does each step; people closest to the work know where it waits
  • Ideally, walk the process first (go and see) with a real item

3Reading the map

Patterns to look for.

Waits much bigger than work

  • Common: efficiency under 15%
  • Attack the biggest wait first

Low % complete & accurate

  • Rework starts upstream
  • Fix quality at the source

Many hand-offs

  • Each adds a wait and a risk
  • Combine or co-locate steps

High WIP in a step

  • A bottleneck
  • Limit WIP, swarm, or add skills

Future State

  • A small number of changes
  • Measured again after

4Facilitation tips

Map realityMap what actually happens, including workarounds, not the official process.
Time, not effortMeasure elapsed time. A step with one hour of work can take a week.
Estimates are fineRough numbers from the people doing the work are enough to start. Refine later.
No blameWaits are almost always caused by the system: approvals, priorities, handovers.
Go and seeWalking a real item through the process reveals steps nobody mentions.
Re-map laterRepeat after the changes. The improvement in lead time is the result.
Common pitfalls: mapping the ideal process; mixing several types of work; optimising one step while the biggest wait sits elsewhere; leaving out the people who do the work; mapping without changing anything.

5After the session

Straight away

Share the map and the biggest wait with leaders and every team in the stream.

Next month

Run the improvement changes as experiments.

Each review

Track lead time and flow efficiency.

Quarterly

Re-map the stream and pick the next constraint.
